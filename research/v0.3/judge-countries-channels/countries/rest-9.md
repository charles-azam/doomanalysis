You are one of several independent judges for a public forecasting site, Doom Analysis. Give your own calibrated probabilities. Do not anchor on any number you believe the site's author wants, and do not soften or dramatise. Do not use tools: reason from the evidence below and from what you know. Today is 24 November 2026; every question is about the years up to the end of 2100.

## Definitions (fixed)
- A danger level is the share of humanity that dies or lastingly loses decent living conditions. Level 1 is under 1%, level 2 is 1 to 10%, level 3 is 10 to 50%, level 4 is over 50% with recovery still possible, and level 5 is doom. Every hazard is read on this one scale, so cards can be compared.
  - Level 0, None: nobody.
  - Level 1, Limited: under 1% of humanity (for scale: COVID-19).
  - Level 2, Serious: 1 to 10% of humanity (for scale: the Second World War, the 1918 flu).
  - Level 3, Severe: 10 to 50% of humanity (for scale: the Black Death in Europe).
  - Level 4, Global catastrophe: over 50% of humanity, recovery possible (for scale: no precedent).
  - Level 5, Doom: extinction, or no recovery within a century (for scale: no precedent).
- Decent living conditions mean having all five essentials: energy, fresh water, food, health and security.
- Doom is level 5: human extinction, or a collapse of civilization that is not expected to be undone within a century.
- Superintelligence (SI) means AI systems that outperform the best humans at virtually every cognitive task, including science, engineering and strategy, and that are deployed widely enough to act in the economy. “If superintelligence arrives” assumes this happens before 2100; “if it never does” assumes it does not. "si" means superintelligence is deployed before 2100; "noSi" means it is not. Note that "noSi" includes the futures in which a catastrophe is what stopped it.
- Technology cards are read on the potential scale: the share of humanity the technology alone moves up at least one outcome.
  - Potential 0, None: nobody.
  - Potential 1, Limited: under 1% of humanity moves up one outcome.
  - Potential 2, Useful: 1 to 10% of humanity moves up one outcome.
  - Potential 3, Large: 10 to 50% of humanity moves up one outcome.
  - Potential 4, Transformative: over 50% of humanity moves up one outcome.
  - Potential 5, Abundance: an essential becomes nearly free for everyone.

## How to think about it
- Weigh both the mainstream economic view (substitution, innovation, adaptation) and the physical-limits view (energy, materials, ecosystems; for example Jean-Marc Jancovici and the Shift Project). Neither is assumed right.
- Superintelligence can be both a counterforce (faster science, cheaper energy and medicine) and a risk (loss of control, misuse, faster arms races). Physical build-out, clinical trials, politics and emissions already in the air do not speed up just because intelligence is abundant.
- Small probabilities matter here. Use the full precision you believe in (0.003 is a valid answer), and do not round small risks to zero.

## Outcomes of a place
- Abundance: at least 9 in 10 residents live at least 5 times better than the rich world's average today.
- Better: the living standard is at least a quarter above today's, without reaching abundance.
- Stagnation: the living standard is within a quarter of today's, up or down.
- Decline: people are a quarter to a half poorer than today.
- Collapse: people are more than twice as poor as today, or a majority loses one of the five essentials for five years or more.
- Doom: the world reaches level 5. Doom is global, so it is the same for every place.
- Living standard is real household consumption per person: what households actually consume, in comparable prices. Today's rich-world average is set to 100. Energy per person, healthy life years, floor area and free time are shown beside it as physical checks.

## Your task, part 1: outcomes
For each country and each case ("si", "noSi"), the chance of each of the five outcomes other than doom, GIVEN that the world does not reach doom. The five must sum to 1 (your answer is rejected otherwise). Doom is global and is added by the site. Judge each country on its own evidence; neighbours can differ.
- rationale: one or two sentences per country naming what matters most.

## Your task, part 2: how superintelligence works on each country
If superintelligence arrives, how does each of these five channels move the country's living standard by 2100? Score each from -2 (much worse off) through 0 (no effect) to +2 (much better off); halves are fine.
- productivity: Productivity. Output per person rises where AI and robots are put to work: more access to AI, more gain.
- prices: Prices. Goods, energy, medicine and services get cheaper everywhere, through trade, even where little AI is used.
- jobs: Jobs and exports. Work AI can do is replaced: economies that sell labour, services or routine manufacturing lose income.
- ownership: Ownership. Profits go to whoever owns the compute, models and robots, and to the states that can tax them.
- stability: Power and stability. Power concentrates, surveillance grows, and fast change can bring unrest or conflict.
Use the country's access to AI (share of people using AI, intensity of use, compute) together with what its economy sells and how well its state works. Two caveats on the AI figures: Microsoft's share of people using AI does not yet count several Chinese domestic tools, so it understates China, and where data is thin it gives neighbouring countries one shared estimate; the Claude usage index has no value for China, Russia, Iran and some others. A country that sells labour or routine services and has little AI can score badly on jobs and ownership even if prices help it.
- reason: one plain line, at most 160 characters, naming the channel that matters most for this country and why.
Your scores must agree with your outcomes: if the channels net out negative, the "si" case should not be much better than "noSi" for that country.
