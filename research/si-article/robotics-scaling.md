# Robotics and elastic industrial scaling: update

Evidence cutoff: 1 November 2026. No repository edits. This updates, rather than reverses, the initial physical-bottleneck framing.

## Editorial correction

The first draft risks making current industrial throughput sound like a fixed property of matter. It is not. SI could improve the *machinery that turns resources into more productive machinery*: robots, production equipment, factory layouts, yield, commissioning, maintenance and supply-chain coordination. The appropriate hypothesis is conditional: the time between abundant intelligence and material abundance depends on whether SI can accelerate that entire replication loop. It is not justified to assume either unchanged historical build rates or instantaneous physical scaling.

The distinction should be between (1) hard physical and ecological budgets, (2) engineerable supply-rate bottlenecks and (3) institutional choices. Today's gearbox shortage, factory construction time, low yield or small installed robot base belongs mostly in category2. Energy conservation, heat rejection, finite available energy/land/material flows and ecological damage belong in category1. Finance, property rights, distribution, conflict, permitting and public consent belong mostly in category3; they can change, but not solely through higher model intelligence. Geology and infrastructure can span categories.

## Ten primary sources and defensible claims

### 1. Industrial robotics is already at million-unit scale

**IFR, 24 September 2026, “Five Million Robots now Operate in Factories Globally.”** https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally

Verified full text: approximately5million industrial robots operational in2025; more than600,000 newly installed that year, +11%. China installed354,000, +20%, representing59% of global installations; domestic Chinese manufacturers supplied195,000. Thus the relevant present scale is O(10^6) installed machines and O(10^5–10^6) annual additions, not a handful of prototype robots. Annual installations are not the same as manufacturing capacity or net stock growth; some replace retired machines. An industrial arm is not a general-purpose human-equivalent worker. IFR's2026/2029 installation forecasts are forecasts, not observations.

### 2. Humanoids are a separate, smaller and less mature population

**IFR, 30 September2026, “Global Sales of Professional Service Robots Surge24%.”** https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-sales-of-professional-service-robots-surge-24-percent

Verified full text: almost250,000 professional service robots sold/shipped in2025, including117,500 transport/logistics robots. About7,000 full-size humanoids taller than140cm were sold globally. IFR says current humanoid applications remain specialised and often require teleoperation; safety, training, maintenance and economics constrain adoption. Those counts have differing scopes: do not add humanoids to industrial/service totals without checking overlap, or compare this7,000 directly with estimates including shorter research platforms. This establishes demonstrated current scale; it does not set the future growth ceiling.

### 3. Real factory deployment, narrower than general labour

**BMW,25June2026, “BMW Group advances the use of Physical AI in production with Figure03 project in Spartanburg.”** https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/global/bmw-group-advances-the-use-of-physical-ai-in-production-with-figure-03-project-in-spartanburg/

Verified full text: Figure02 supported production of more than30,000 BMW X3s over ten months, inserting sheet-metal parts in a body-shop welding process. Figure03 logistics sequencing is the next planned/tested use case, including unsorted component picking into a sequencing trolley. This is evidence of sustained useful physical work in a real factory. It is not evidence one robot built30,000 cars, handled every factory job, achieved fully unsupervised general autonomy, or replaced all labour. Earlier BMW release's indexed official text gives90,000components/~1,250operating hours, but its old URL redirects; the robust direct source supports30,000cars/tenmonths without that issue.

### 4. Logistics deployment is commercially real, with disclosure limits

**Agility Robotics,26October2026, “Agility Robotics Highlights Commercial Momentum and Growing Enterprise Demand for Digit Humanoid Robot.”** https://www.agilityrobotics.com/content/agility-robotics-highlights-commercial-momentum-and-growing-enterprise-demand-for-digit-humanoid-robot

Verified: the supplier reports100,000+ tote movements at GXO's Georgia site under a commercial agreement beginningJune2024; further customer agreements and a2027pilot are separately identified. Attribute to Agility; customer contracts, planned sites and actual operation are different milestones. The100,000count was already announced inNovember2025, so do not call it a new October2026 operating breakthrough. Public count alone lacks robot-hours, intervention rates and full costs, so it cannot establish human-equivalent productivity or scale economics.

### 5. A concrete bottleneck can be redesigned

**Schaeffler,2September2026, “Ready for the mass market: Schaeffler presents formed strain wave gearboxes for humanoid robots.”** https://www.schaeffler.de/en/news_media/press_releases/press_releases_detail.jsp?id=88212928

Verified company claims: forming instead of precision machining reduces manufacturing cost of the described gearbox by>25%, material use by>75%, and some manufacturing steps from minutes to seconds; mass-manufacturing rollout to humanoid makers is planned2027. Actuators include motors, gears/screws, bearings, sensors and controls; supply of these can be scaled or redesigned. This is direct evidence that a component bottleneck need not be permanent. It is a supplier claim, not independent measured whole-robot cost reduction or proof of operational mass production. Do not apply25% to total robot cost.

### 6. Tesla/Panasonic's original factory plan

**Tesla/Panasonic joint announcement,31July2014.** https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/panasonic-and-tesla-sign-agreement-gigafactory

Verified full text: planned35GWh/year cells and50GWh/year packs by2020. Panasonic was to manufacture cells and invest in production equipment; Tesla to provide/manage buildings/utilities and assemble modules/packs; suppliers to provide precursor materials. The rationale included co-location, process optimization, scale and lower logistics/inventory costs. These were contemporary targets, not actual2014production. This is strong evidence for the *factory as an engineered product* argument and for partner/supply-chain contributions, not sole-inventor history.

### 7. The factory subsequently delivered at the intended order of magnitude

**Tesla,24January2023, “Continuing Our Investment in Nevada.”** https://www.tesla.com/en_gb/blog/continuing-our-investment-nevada

Verified first-party retrospective: Tesla reports7.3billion cells cumulatively and37GWh+annually, alongside1.5million packs; it reports$6.2billion invested since2014. These figures show an O(10^1GWh/year) plan became O(10^1GWh/year) production; attribute figures to Tesla. A further100GWh factory was a new plan, not output already delivered. Avoid relying on Tesla's assertion the original goal exceeded all2013global battery production without a comparable independent denominator. Existing industrial scaling succeeded, but on a multi-year timeline and with substantial accumulated expertise.

### 8. Panasonic predates Tesla's gigafactory

**Panasonic corporate history,1994entry; history page current.** https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/about/history/chronicle/1994.html

Verified: lithium-ion products developed/marketed1994. Panasonic's2022investor history separately records in-vehicle lithium-ion batteries2008: https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/investors/pdf/irday2022_ene_e.pdf . Accordingly, “nobody thought of large-scale batteries before Tesla” is unsupported. Tesla's contribution can fairly be framed as ambitious integration and scaling with experienced partners. No Isaacson quotation has been used or verified: prefer primary industrial records to an unverified biography quotation.

### 9. China and the global battery industry demonstrate elastic capacity

**IEA, Global EV Outlook2026, Electric vehicle batteries (2026).** https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/electric-vehicle-batteries

Verified full text: installed global lithium-ion nameplate manufacturing capacity>4TWh/year at end2025, up~30% from2024; China>80% of both capacity and output. Actual EV battery deployment~1.2TWh in2025, a narrower and different measure. Thus capacity is not utilized output, and not all battery production goes to EVs. The IEA reports Tesla still sourced most EV cells from Panasonic/LGES/CATL; ramp-up and supplier expertise matter. It also notes capacity can grow before output/demand catches up. This simultaneously supports rapid industrial scaling and cautions against counting announcements or nameplate as delivered services.

### 10. China's photovoltaic scale is already physically large

**IEA PVPS, Trends in Photovoltaic Applications2026, September2026.** https://iea-pvps.org/trends_reports/trends-2026/

Verified:690GW worldwide PV installations during2025,415GW inChina (~60%). Manufacturing overcapacity persists. Those are actual nameplate additions, not constant output or announced factories. **Calculation:**415GW×assumed20%capacity factor×8,760h≈727TWh/year once fully operating. This is O(10^3TWh/year) of new annual electricity from one country's single-year installations. The record rebuts a blanket claim physical infrastructure cannot scale fast, without proving indefinite exponential growth or full-system renewable reliability.

## Reinvestment model with explicit units

Let K(t) be installed production capacity, measured in useful units/year. Utilization u is dimensionless; margin m is dollars/useful unit *after running costs*; reinvestment fraction r is dimensionless; κ is capital cost per unit of annual capacity, dollars/(unit/year); depreciation/retirement δ is1/year.

If reinvestment can buy capacity without upstream shortages or construction delay,

  dK/dt = [r u m/κ − δ] K ≡ gK.

Thus K(t)=K0 exp(gt) and doubling time τ=ln2/g. This is a deliberately simple scenario model, not an empirical industrial law. Construction lag, learning, debt, imports, labour, grid connections, materials, tooling, demand and safety can alter or cap growth. More useful autonomy can increase u and margins, reduce κ, accelerate commissioning and reduce maintenance. It need not remove every bottleneck to change g substantially.

**Order-of-magnitude comparison:** capacity doubling every2years for20years means2^10=1,024×. Doubling every5years means2^4=16×. From an indexed starting capacity1, those paths differ by64× despite both being exponential. For10years, the factors are32×and4×. They are explicitly hypothetical and bounded-duration scenarios, not a2030/2050robot forecast.

Useful labour/services are also endogenous:

  useful output ≈ robot stock × productive hours/robot-year × accepted output/hour.

A million manufactured robots is not a million worker equivalents. If stock doubles and accepted useful output per robot doubles, delivered output grows4× over that interval. Robots building factories/robots can feed back into stock growth, while AI improves uptime and task scope. We should measure the numerator *and* rejected work, maintenance and human intervention.

**Double exponential:** multiplying two exponential improvements remains exponential: e^(at)e^(bt)=e^((a+b)t). A genuinely double-exponential model requires the proportional growth rate itself to grow exponentially, e.g. g(t)=g0e^(kt), giving K(t)=K0 exp[(g0/k)(e^(kt)−1)]. This could approximate a short phase where faster intelligence continually accelerates factory replication, but it is not established by today's data and cannot run indefinitely under finite energy/material/ecological budgets. A stronger article should keep this as an upside scenario and test its prerequisites, not make it the central forecast.

## Compact replacement prose (~190words)

The factory is part of the invention. Tesla and Panasonic planned35GWh of cells a year in2014; by2023 Tesla reported more than37GWh annually at Nevada. Panasonic had been making lithium-ion batteries since the1990s. The achievement was not discovering that batteries could be mass-produced; it was combining cell expertise, co-located suppliers, equipment and capital at a much larger scale. China's record makes the same point: it installed415GW of solar in2025, while the world ended the year with more than4TWh/year of battery manufacturing capacity. Existing production limits can move.

Robotics could move them faster. About5million industrial robots are already working worldwide. New humanoids have performed useful factory and warehouse tasks, although general autonomy and economics remain unproven. SI could improve the robot, the machine that builds it, and the factory that makes that machine. A production system doubling everytwo years expands1,000-fold in twenty years; everyfive years gives only16-fold. These are scenarios, not predictions. The question is whether verified autonomy, reinvestment and upstream supply can sustain the faster loop. Thermodynamics constrains the budget; it does not fix today's factory throughput.
