# Earth services and physical limits

Evidence checked 1 November 2026. The calculations below use **10 billion people as an author-selected scale**, not an exact population forecast. Technology is assessed by delivered service and ecological effect. No physical quantity is silently converted into an outcome probability.

## Can abundant energy supply water and food without unlimited land?

### Takeaway

The energy required for basic domestic water is tractable at world scale; inland delivery, sanitation, agriculture, cost and reliability remain substantial constraints. Food is already an industrial-scale energy-and-land conversion system. Controlled environments and microbial foods could change its footprint, but electricity does not make calories or balanced diets free.

### Cited Findings

- Seawater reverse osmosis typically uses **2.5–6 kWh/m³** including the core process and supporting steps. The largest desalination plants produce order **1 million m³/day**. Desalination supplies under 1% of world freshwater withdrawals; concentrated brine and distribution infrastructure matter. These are IEA's March 2026 figures, not a universal thermodynamic minimum. — [IEA, Wired for water, 22 March 2026](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/wired-for-water-how-electrification-is-transforming-desalination).
- Agriculture accounted for **72% of global freshwater withdrawals in 2023**. Withdrawal measures water taken from a source; consumption/depletion measures what is no longer available for another use in that basin and period. FAO's water-stress indicator also reserves environmental water requirements. — [FAO SDG6.4.2](https://www.fao.org/sustainable-development-goals-data-portal/data/indicators/642-water-stress/2/en).
- FAO's July 2026 release reports **4.6 billion hectares of agricultural land in 2024**. Land equipped for irrigation was **351 million ha**, about 22% of cropland. Cropland expanded by92 mha from 2001, while land productivity increased about68%; cropland expansion accounted for about72% of forest land loss. These are a new statistical series: do not silently combine its total with older FAO estimates of4.8billion ha. — [FAO land statistics 2001–2024](https://www.fao.org/statistics/highlights-archive/highlights-detail/land-statistics-2001-2024.-global--regional-and-country-trends/en).
- Irrigation technology can reduce nonbeneficial evaporation and unrecoverable losses. Reducing recoverable runoff or groundwater recharge at one field does not necessarily save water at basin scale; expanded irrigated area can consume the apparent saving. Water allocation and environmental-flow rules therefore matter alongside precision control. — [Linstead 2018, Environmental Flows](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2018.00048/full); [Lankford 2023, Irrigated Systems Accounting](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2023.108437).
- A 2021 system model of photovoltaic-driven single-cell protein includes power, CO₂ capture, electron-donor production, cultivation and processing. It estimates potential protein yield per land over tenfold that of the comparison staple crops, with at least twice caloric yield. This is modelled system potential, not an already deployed global replacement diet. — [Leger etal., PNAS 2021, abstract and figure descriptions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34155098/).
- A 2026 exploratory CEA study finds very different energy costs across crops and systems; its modelled vertical-farm wheat energy is about1,600 MJ/kg harvested biomass, while the authors flag scarce experimental staple-crop data. These results are scenario/model-specific. They support investigating lighting and climate control, not declaring a fixed law that wheat cannot be grown indoors. — [Frontiers 2026 study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1945186/full).
- Gene editing and digital crop/environment monitoring are existing research and deployment routes. FAO identifies potential faster development of improved crop varieties, alongside food-safety assessment, access and regulatory questions. — [FAO SIF 2024](https://fao.sitefinity.cloud/newsroom/detail/world-food-forum--inclusive-innovation-takes-centre-stage-at-sif-2024/en); [FAO 2022, Gene editing and agrifood systems](https://doi.org/10.4060/cc3579en).

### Inferences

**Calculation1 — domestic desalination.** Select50 litres/person/day for a basic domestic-service illustration. This is not asserted to cover agriculture, all industrial uses or a universal adequate-water standard.

- Water =10¹⁰×0.05 m³/day×365 =**1.825×10¹¹m³/year =183 km³/year**.
- At an explicitly selected3 kWh/m³, within the IEA range, electricity =**547.5 TWh/year**, average62.5 GW.
- At2.5–6 kWh/m³, the same water needs**456–1,095 TWh/year**, before long-distance or uphill distribution beyond the plant boundary.
- Daily production is500million m³: about**500 one-million-m³/day plant-equivalents**. These are equivalent throughputs, not a recommended design of500centralised plants.
- A water lift Δh=1,000m requiresρgΔh =9.81 MJ/m³ =**2.73 kWh/m³ minimum**. At assumed70% pump efficiency this is**3.89 kWh/m³**, before friction. A kilometre of elevation can therefore cost more than the selected desalination process. Efficient membranes alone do not supply an inland city.
- Agriculture's72% withdrawal share makes extending this domestic calculation to all freshwater needs invalid. Rainfall, crop evapotranspiration, basin flows and salinity require their own accounts. Better reuse, pipe repair and aquifer management may be cheaper than new desalination. The engineering objective is reliable safe water at the point of use.

**Calculation2 — calories and land.** Select2,500 kcal/person/day as a transparent average food-energy budget, not a clinical recommendation.

-10¹⁰×2,500×4,184J/kcal×365 =**38.2 EJ/year**, average**1.21 TW of edible chemical energy**.
- If a hypothetical all-electric food system converts5% of electricity into edible chemical energy, it needs**24.2 TW electric**, or212,000 TWh/year. At10% it needs12.1 TW. These are efficiency sensitivities, not asserted efficiency ceilings or forecasts. They show why replacing free sunlight with artificial lighting can dominate the energy bill for staple calories.
- For a deliberately simplified grain benchmark, assume15 MJ/kg edible energy and4 tonnes/ha/year yield. The budget requires**2.55billion tonnes/year** and**0.64billion ha**. This is not a complete diet: it excludes feed, losses, seed, crop rotations, nutrients, regional yield variation and reserves. It is a mass-balance reference against order1.6billion ha cropland, not proof that everybody can be fed from one crop.
- Photosynthesis engineering, heat/drought-tolerant breeding, field robotics, crop protection, greenhouse solar light and microbial protein are different routes. SI could improve all of them. Protein fermentation can reduce land demand without making every food, culture or nutrient interchangeable. Track edible nutrients and accepted food output per energy, water, land and capital.

### Gaps

No single adequately validated 2050 whole-diet footprint or energy-to-food efficiency was established. The5% and grain-yield scenarios must stay labelled assumptions. The FAO gene-editing report's DOI could not be retrieved in full; the official FAO 2024 account was read through indexed text. The microbial paper's PubMed abstract/figure descriptions were accessible, while publisher/PMC access was blocked. The 2026 CEA article was accessible; it is an exploratory model, not a large commercial wheat trial.

## Do nutrients and minerals run out, or does the supply rate fail first?

### Takeaway

Nitrogen can be fixed from air with energy; its ecological burden comes from losses as well as manufacturing emissions. Phosphorus cannot be substituted biologically, but current geological inventories do not support a near-term global exhaustion claim. Metals can circulate, while rapidly growing stocks still need new material.

### Cited Findings

- The IEA 2021 ammonia baseline uses**8.6 EJ/year**, with average net production intensity about**41 GJ/tonne** versus best natural-gas routes28 GJ/tonne. About70% of ammonia is used for fertiliser. Electrolysis and other near-zero-emission production routes were already being developed; these dated figures must not be labelled 2026 measurements. — [IEA Ammonia Technology Roadmap 2021](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap/executive-summary); [report scope](https://www.iea.org/reports/ammonia-technology-roadmap).
- FAO's 2023 cropland balance records an annual global surplus of**85 Mt nitrogen and7 Mt phosphorus**. These are inputs minus removals in crop output, not a direct measurement that all85 Mt nitrogen instantly enters waterways. Regional nutrient deficits also persist. — [FAO 2025 nutrient balances](https://www.fao.org/statistics/highlights-archive/highlights-detail/cropland-nutrient-balance-(global--regional-and-country-trends--1961-2023)/).
- Reactive-nitrogen losses damage water, air, climate and biodiversity; locally appropriate legumes and biological fixation, better application, recycling and soil management are available routes. Manufacturing cleaner ammonia alone does not remove field losses. — [FAO Sustainable Nitrogen Management 2025](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-nitrogen-use-efficiency-report/en).
- USGS 2026 estimates 2025 phosphate-rock production at**250million tonnes** and reserves at**73billion tonnes**, with resources above300billion tonnes. USGS states no imminent global shortage of phosphate rock and no substitute for phosphorus in agriculture. Reserve figures are economic/geological classifications, not all atoms available in Earth's crust. — [USGS phosphate rock 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf); [report provenance and reserve definitions](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/mcs2026).
- UNEP 2023 estimates wastewater nutrient recovery could offset**13.4% of global agricultural nutrient demand**; it also identifies water reuse, heat and useful materials. This is potential under recovery assumptions, not currently recovered output. — [UNEP Wastewater 2023](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/down-drain-lies-promising-climate-and-nature-solution-un-report).
- The IEA 2024 Announced Pledges Scenario estimates recycling could reduce new mine development needs by40% for copper/cobalt and25% for lithium/nickel by 2050; mines still need expansion. This is a scenario, not a present recycling share or physical upper bound. — [IEA Recycling 2024](https://www.iea.org/reports/recycling-of-critical-minerals/executive-summary).

### Inferences

**Calculation3 — making fertiliser with electricity.** NH₃ contains3/17≈17.6% hydrogen by mass. For an author-selected**200MtNH₃/year**, producing the stoichiometric hydrogen by electrolysis at an assumed**50 kWh/kgH₂** requires:

200×10⁹kgNH₃×(3/17)×50 kWh/kgH₂ =**1,765 TWh/year**, average201 GW.

This excludes air separation, compression, synthesis, storage and distribution. It is a process-budget example, not all-electric replacement of8.6 EJ by an equal number of electrical joules. Cheaper low-carbon power could materially improve fertiliser production. If fertiliser is overapplied, cheap power can also worsen nutrient losses. Precision application, biological fixation and recovery affect both yield and damage.

**Calculation4 — mineral stock and recycling flow.** 73 Gt/0.25 Gt/year≈**292 years** of 2025 phosphate-rock throughput. This is explicitly **not a depletion date**: demand, grades, discoveries, prices, reserve definitions, location and recoverability change. It rebuts “all phosphorus disappears before 2050,” without guaranteeing affordable secure access.

For a material-intensive fleet, suppose annual installationsQ(t) grow exponentially at rate g, material intensity stays fixed, every product livesL years, and100% of its material is recovered at retirement. Returned end-of-life material is at mostQ(t−L), so:

**Rmax/Q(t)=exp(−gL).**

At doubling every5 years and lifetime15 years, g=ln2/5 and the fraction is**1/8=12.5%**. Fast stock growth requires new inputs even with perfect recovery. When growth slows and old stocks mature, recycling can supply a much larger fraction. This ignores manufacturing scrap and material substitutions and does not predict an actual battery recycling share. SI can improve sorting, chemistry, design for disassembly, lifetime and substitution; the cohort timing still has to be counted.

As an additional illustrative ore-grade sensitivity, recovering1tonne of a metal from1%-grade ore needs at least100 tonnes of ore before recovery losses; at0.5%, at least200 tonnes. This is mass balance under stated grades, not a claim about the current average copper mine. Better beneficiation can move energy/cost; metal atoms alone do not measure economical mine/refinery supply.

### Gaps

No universal mineral energy floor is identified: ore geometry, grade, mineralogy, recovery route and product quality dominate. The USGS PDF could not be fetched directly, but its official indexed table/text supplied the numbers and the report landing page supplied provenance; do not claim full-PDF review. A credible material-service forecast needs regional mines/refineries, actual collection and recycling output, technology substitution and manufacturing schedules.

## Which limits remain even after energy becomes cheap?

### Takeaway

Energy can buy manufactured substitutes for some services. It does not automatically reproduce soils, pollination, a watershed's timing, a complete ecosystem or a lost species. Avoiding ecological damage and increasing delivery can improve abundance together; poorly governed scaling can instead amplify damage.

### Cited Findings

- IPBES's 2016 pollination assessment attributes about**5–8% of crop production by volume** directly to animal pollination, while much larger shares of crop types benefit at least partly. The volume estimate is not the share of all calories, and pollinator-dependent foods contribute important micronutrients. — [IPBES pollination assessment](https://files.ipbes.net/ipbes-web-prod-public-files/downloads/pdf/Decision_IPBES_4_1_EN.pdf).
- The October 2026 FAO/UNEP/CBD restoration assessment documents**252million ha under restoration across149 countries**. This is area with restoration underway, not verified full ecological recovery. Freshwater and marine/coastal progress is much weaker; monitoring and secure long-term institutions matter. — [UNEP 2026 restoration report](https://www.unep.org/en/publications/report/restoration-progress-report-natures-comeback-scaling-ecosystem-restoration); [release and measurement caveats](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/story/un-report-shows-nature-being-restored-unprecedented-scale).

For the probability model, water/food/nutrient/recycling improvements primarily move **M** and actual affordability/access **U**. Ecosystem damage affects **M**, institutional resilience and the ecological-shock pathway together. Do not add a separate independent “desalination success” chance to each: energy, construction, finance and institutions are shared causes. A crossed boundary is evidence of exposure, not a measured probability of irreversible global doom.

**Three neglected candidates, ranked for this article:**

1. **Wastewater as coupled water-and-nutrient infrastructure.** This improves water supply while reducing nutrient discharge and fertiliser demand; UNEP's13.4% nutrient potential makes it a sizeable complement, not a complete replacement. Measure actual safe reused water and recovered nutrients, not proposed treatment capacity. SI helps process control, sensing, logistics and design; pipelines, safe chemistry and collection remain. — [UNEP 2023](https://www.unep.org/en/news-and-stories/press-release/down-drain-lies-promising-climate-and-nature-solution-un-report).
2. **Microbial protein that decouples part of food production from fertile soil.** Its potential land yield is an unusually direct channel from cheap electricity to food. Modelled gains need full energy, nutrient, processing, food-safety, taste and cost accounting. It could materially improve abundance and land pressure if these conditions hold; it is not proof of a worldwide diet transition. — [Leger 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34155098/).
3. **Basin-scale water accounting plus enforceable depletion limits.** The neglected advance may be better coordination rather than a new machine: use remote sensing/control to preserve environmental flows while raising useful crop output. Higher field efficiency can otherwise expand production and increase total depletion. This directly tests the institutional variable shared by abundance and doom. — [Linstead 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2018.00048/full).

### Gaps

The pollination PDF fetch timed out; the institutional indexed text supplied the figure, and the article should keep its dated 2016 status. No universal conversion from habitat loss or species counts to a global extinction/irreversibility probability was found. The new 2026 restoration release has different posting dates across catalogue and story pages; describe it simply as October 2026, and distinguish work underway from ecological success. Not all energy-addressable services are ecologically or socially interchangeable.
