# Energy routes and the constraints SI could move

Evidence checked 1 November 2026. This memo supplements `EnergyPhysics.astro`; it does not replace that component's fusion derivation or global energy budget. All numerical examples labelled **assumed** are dimensional tests, not measured technology performance or 2050 forecasts. No technology probabilities are inferred from a resource-size ratio.

## Which energy routes deserve a place in the portfolio?

### Takeaway

The fundamental inputs are solar radiation, nuclear binding energy, Earth's internal heat and gravitational/rotational energy. Many apparent alternatives are different converters of the same input. Hydrogen made with electricity, synthetic fuels and storage move energy between forms and dates. Heat pumps increase useful service per electrical joule by moving environmental heat. They should not be counted as additional primary generation. [IEA heat-pump explanation](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-heat-pumps/how-a-heat-pump-works), [DOE pumped-storage explanation](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/pumped-storage-hydropower), [IEA Ocean Energy Systems taxonomy](https://www.ocean-energy-systems.org/ocean-energy/what-is-ocean-energy/).

### Inferences

Five useful calculations beyond the detailed fusion/PV ledger:

1. **Wind:** P=½ρAv³Cₚ. With the table's assumed inputs, ½×1.225×π×100²×8³×0.45=4.43MW. Substituting average wind speed into v³ is generally wrong for annual production; integrate the operating curve over actual weather. SI can improve converter performance and deployment, not create an arbitrary wind flux. The coefficient ceiling is a result for a specified ideal rotor/control-volume model, not a universal cap on every conceivable wind-harvesting geometry. [DOE conventional-turbine account](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/windexchange/small-wind-guidebook).
2. **Pumped storage:** E=ρηgHV. At500m head and90% generating efficiency,12GWh usable requires9.8million m³ of usable water transfer. A reservoir's total volume is not all necessarily usable. Additional pumping energy and reservoir construction belong in a whole-system design. Geometry and energy conservation are this calculation's basis; its inputs are author assumptions. [DOE operating mechanism](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/pumped-storage-hydropower).
3. **Heat pumps:** COP4 turns1kWh electricity plus3kWh ambient heat into4kWh delivered heat. This conserves energy. Supplying a given low-temperature heat service can require much less generation than combustion replacement by electrical resistance. COP depends on temperature lift and equipment. The IEA describes typical household COP near4, not a guarantee in every climate. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-heat-pumps/how-a-heat-pump-works).
4. **Hydrogen storage:** take **assumed**50kWh electricity/kg electrolyser consumption,33.3kWh/kg H₂ lower heating value and50% electrical reconversion. Electricity out is16.7kWh/kg, a33% round trip before compression, storage and transport. Lower-temperature electrolysis cannot be evaluated by counting hydrogen energy while omitting the electrical input; high-temperature electrolysis additionally uses heat. These are scenario efficiencies, not a best-possible thermodynamic bound. IEA2024 cites50–55kWh/kg as an electrolyser reference. [IEA2024 report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3ece5ee4-7537-4992-8ba6-b83c994c3fd4/GlobalHydrogenReview2024.pdf).
5. **Ocean heat:** for warm/cold streams at298K and278K, η_Carnot=1−278/298=0.067. Producing100MW therefore requires at least1.49GW of heat transfer even at reversible efficiency. At an **assumed**3% efficiency, heat input is3.33GW. This is why warm-water volume alone does not imply cheap electricity. EIA describes a20°C temperature difference and small demonstration systems; a full-scale plant needs large water handling and marine works. [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/hydropower/ocean-thermal-energy-conversion.php).

### Gaps

- This is a technology/physics coverage matrix, not a least-cost dispatch model. Installed cost, finance, hourly weather, regional demand, network constraints, replacement and ecological exclusions are required to select an actual2050 portfolio. A resource assessment cannot substitute for them. [IEA assessment's explicit cost conditions](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-geothermal-energy/executive-summary).
- No robust global2050 annual production forecast was established for natural hydrogen, salinity-gradient power, waves or OTEC. Their inclusion preserves possible alternatives; it does not assign them a large role by assumption. [USGS uncertainty](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/central-energy-resources-science-center/science/geologic-hydrogen), [EIA OTEC maturity](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/hydropower/ocean-thermal-energy-conversion.php).
- Global thermal-energy storage, flow batteries, sodium-ion, iron-air, compressed air and hydrogen require a storage portfolio analysis of duration, cycles, site geology and cost; one battery-factory number is insufficient. The source-supported distinctions here are storage versus generation and heat versus electricity. [DOE storage mechanism](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/pumped-storage-hydropower), [DOE CSP thermal storage](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/concentrating-solar-thermal-power-basics).

## What would twenty-kilometre geothermal wells actually buy?

### Takeaway

Twenty kilometres is a route to temperature, not a unit of usable power. The technology must solve drilling **and** heat exchange, circulation, lifetime and replication. The large stock of hot rock permits extracting more than natural local replenishment for a period, while making reservoir management essential. [Quaise developers' technical paper](https://www.worldgeothermal.org/pdf/IGAstandard/WGC/2020/21090.pdf), [PNNL closed-loop models](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).

### Cited Findings

- The IEA2024 cost-effective development case reaches up to800GW and almost6,000TWh/year by2050, conditional on innovation and cost reductions. Its much larger technical resource estimates assume finite extraction lifetimes; they are not forecasts of construction. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-geothermal-energy/executive-summary).
- Davies and Davies estimate Earth's natural surface heat loss at47±2TW. This is the whole planet's natural heat flow, not electricity available at wellheads or heat replenishment at a particular field. [Original2010 paper](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/1/5/2010/).
- Quaise's2021 developer paper discusses10–20km direct-energy drilling. Its physical proposal directs millimetre-wave power downhole to melt/vaporise rock, avoiding conventional hot drill-bit constraints; it also identifies stimulation and well construction challenges. These are development proposals. [Conference paper](https://www.worldgeothermal.org/pdf/IGAstandard/WGC/2020/21090.pdf).
- Quaise reported100m of millimetre-wave drilling inJuly2025. InJuly2026 it described its Texas system as **approaching**1km. That wording is not proof of a completed1km test, much less20km. [2025 milestone](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-achieves-drilling-milestone-with-millimeter-wave-technology), [July2026 update](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-raises-134-million-in-first-close-of-series-b-to-build-worlds-first-superhot-geothermal-power-plant).
- Quaise'sSeptember2026 Obsidian release targets a three-well demonstration above25MW gross electric, with first power and a larger phase targeted for2030. ItsOctober26 transcript describes a confirmation well aiming at15,000ft≈4.6km and700°F≈371°C. These are company targets at a favourable volcanic site, not proof that20km works everywhere. [September release](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-selected-for-up-to-25-million-in-u-s-department-of-energy-support-to-advance-worlds-first-commercial-superhot-geothermal-power-plant), [October transcript](https://www.quaise.com/news/confirming-the-resource-at-project-obsidian).
- Fervo'sOctober1,2026 release reports its first Cape Station GeoBlock reached33MW **net** and commercial operation onSeptember30. Two more blocks, approximately100MW forPhaseI overall, were still commissioning and expected byJanuary1,2027; an additional400MW was targeted for2028. Net commercial output is stronger evidence than a contracted gigawatt. [Fervo primary release](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-declares-commercial-operation-at-cape-station-ahead-of-schedule-leading-the-race-for-next-generation-geothermal-energy/).

### Inferences

**Depth and pressure.** Choose a surface temperature15°C and **assumed** linear gradient25K/km. Then T(20km)≈515°C. Local gradients and their evolution with depth vary; this is a scale example, not a universal temperature prediction. At **assumed**rock density2,700kg/m³, lithostatic stressρgz≈530MPa. A cold constant-density1,000kg/m³ water-column reference gives196MPa. Neither is automatically the actual reservoir fluid pressure. High temperature, chemistry, stress and permeability must be considered jointly. The developer paper uses a30°C/km example and discusses the brittle–ductile transition rather than assuming present rock mechanics stays unchanged. [Technical source](https://www.worldgeothermal.org/pdf/IGAstandard/WGC/2020/21090.pdf).

**Heat stock.** Let rock densityρ=2,700kg/m³, heat capacityc=800J/kg/K and usable coolingΔT=100K, all **author assumptions**. A cubic kilometre containsρcVΔT=2.16×10¹⁷J=60TWh thermal. A100MW-electric plant at15% conversion draws667MW thermal, or175TWh over30full-power years. It therefore needs about2.9km³ ideally cooled by100K; at20% recoverable fraction, about15km³. The fraction is an assumed integrated allowance, not an empirical universal recovery rate. This identifies the need for connected exchange volume and lifetime; it does not establish a design. [PNNL modelling shows dependence on geometry, fluids and reservoir conditions](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).

**Heat flow.** For an **assumed**fluid enthalpy dropΔh=0.8MJ/kg, flow100kg/s brings80MW thermal, or12MW electric at15% conversion before pumping and other loads. Actual hot-brine/supercritical enthalpy must come from the appropriate equation of state. A deep reservoir does not automatically supply that flow. Pressure loss and heat-exchange area can bind even after drilling succeeds. In a circulating loop, the full hydrostatic head is not simply a continuously paid pumping loss: downflow and upflow columns partly balance, while friction, density differences and reservoir pressure losses determine the net. [PNNL reservoir/working-fluid analysis](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).

**Stock versus replenishment.** Applying the47TW natural heat flow as an immediate cap on worldwide heat mining would be wrong. Equating the entire crust's heat content to a perpetual well output would also be wrong. A field can cool and later recover; development must manage extraction area, lifetime and new wells. SI could optimise drilling, branching, stimulation, fluid choice, scaling/corrosion control and replacement well schedules. The durable evidence is electricity delivered per well and dollar across many reservoirs, maintained over years. [Earth heat-flow measurement](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/1/5/2010/), [IEA extraction-lifetime conventions](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-geothermal-energy/executive-summary).

### Gaps

- No completed20km commercial geothermal well was established by the primary material checked. No independently verified post-July2026 depth record for Quaise's millimetre-wave demonstrator was found. Preserve the dated “approaching1km” observation rather than replacing it with a guessed current record. [July update](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-raises-134-million-in-first-close-of-series-b-to-build-worlds-first-superhot-geothermal-power-plant).
- The two Obsidian sources use different configurations and power descriptions: the September release's triplet/>25MW gross and theOctober video's50MW heat/10–15MW electrical potential should not be merged into one achieved-power claim. They concern planned tests, not measured exports. [September](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-selected-for-up-to-25-million-in-u-s-department-of-energy-support-to-advance-worlds-first-commercial-superhot-geothermal-power-plant), [October](https://www.quaise.com/news/confirming-the-resource-at-project-obsidian).
- No global drilling-rig, gyrotron, casing and reservoir-success model was constructed. Such a model is necessary before turning technical potential into a probability of fleet deployment. [IEA identifies transferable oil/gas capability and cost conditions](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-geothermal-energy/executive-summary).

## Which additional constraints and advances could change the probability inputs?

### Takeaway

The portfolio makes energy abundance more robust because technologies can substitute. The supply probabilities remain associated through common factories, finance, grids, minerals and institutions. Physics gives necessary thresholds; a probability also needs defensible distributions for cost, build rate, availability and demand. The following ideas should generate tests rather than unsupported new percentages. [IEA geothermal's explicit conditional development framing](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-future-of-geothermal-energy/executive-summary), [DOE fuel-chain dependencies](https://www.energy.gov/ne/nuclear-fuel-cycle).

### Cited Findings

- **Uranium is a stock and a supply chain.** The2024 NEA/IAEA Red Book reports about7.9million tonnes of identified recoverable uranium belowUSD260/kgU as ofJanuary2023. Its2022 world requirements are59,018tU for394GW electric capacity; these dated quantities are a baseline, not today's exact demand. Resource figures respond to prices, exploration and processing assumptions. [Red Book](https://www.oecd-nea.org/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-04/7683_uranium_2024_-_resources_production_and_demand_2025-04-22_14-29-2_928.pdf).
- Fully closed fast-reactor cycles have potential to extract60–70times more energy from uranium than existing thermal reactors, according to the IAEA's2025 technical workshop description. This is a fuel-cycle potential, not a claim that every deployed fast reactor already realises it. [IAEA](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/25/03/evt2404559_information_sheet-workshop_on_fr_fuel-fin_.pdf).
- Closed-loop geothermal models explore geometry, fluid and rock conditions rather than a single universal watt/metre figure. [PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).
- Heat storage can supply industrial heat without converting it back to electricity. CSP already explicitly combines heat collection, storage and industrial uses. [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/concentrating-solar-thermal-power-basics).

### Inferences

**Nuclear countercheck:** hold the Red Book's dated fuel intensity fixed:59,018/394≈150tU perGW-capacity-year. The energy specialist's hypothetical11,100GW fleet then needs about1.7million tU/year. Today's identified7.9Mt resource category would equal only about4.7years at that enormous fixed fleet. That is a stress test, **not** a depletion forecast: resources could expand, tails assays and burnup change, and closed fuel cycles could greatly lower feed requirements. But a fleet forecast that scales reactors while leaving mines, enrichment, fabrication and recycling implicit is incomplete. Apply the60–70factor only to a successfully established fully closed cycle; startup fissile inventories and industrial reprocessing cannot be inferred from the uranium stock. [NEA/IAEA baseline](https://www.oecd-nea.org/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-04/7683_uranium_2024_-_resources_production_and_demand_2025-04-22_14-29-2_928.pdf), [IAEA fast-cycle potential](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/25/03/evt2404559_information_sheet-workshop_on_fr_fuel-fin_.pdf).

**Three neglected candidates worth testing:**

1. **Direct industrial heat and thermal storage.** Cheap high-temperature storage could reduce the electricity/storage build required for factories. At **assumed**solid density2,500kg/m³, heat capacity800J/kg/K and1,000K swing, sensible storage holds2GJ/m³≈560kWh thermal/m³ before insulation and usable-temperature constraints. It is an energy density, not cycle efficiency or cost. SI could improve refractory materials, heat exchangers and integration. Measure delivered heat at the required temperature and cost, including losses and replacement. [DOE's heat/storage pathway](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/concentrating-solar-thermal-power-basics).
2. **Geothermal reservoir engineering, not merely deeper drilling.** Multilateral exchange geometries, stimulation control and fluids may increase accepted lifetime output per well. The relevant breakthrough could occur at5km rather than20km. Measure net power, drawdown, pumping and success rate across replicated sites. [PNNL models](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs), [Fervo's33MW net operational benchmark](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-declares-commercial-operation-at-cape-station-ahead-of-schedule-leading-the-race-for-next-generation-geothermal-energy/).
3. **Fuel-cycle and separation technology.** Faster/cheaper validated recycling, enrichment or alternative fuels could move a fission constraint as decisively as a new reactor geometry. Conversely, a working reactor design can wait for its fuel. Measure qualified annual fuel output, fissile inventory, recovery losses and economics. [DOE fuel cycle](https://www.energy.gov/ne/nuclear-fuel-cycle), [NRC advanced-fuel requirements](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/new-fuels/haleu), [IAEA fast cycles](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/25/03/evt2404559_information_sheet-workshop_on_fr_fuel-fin_.pdf).

Take20km geothermal seriously. With an assumed25K/km gradient and15°C surface, that reaches roughly515°C. But temperature is not power. For assumed rock density2,700kg/m³, heat capacity800J/kg/K and100K cooling,1km³ contains60TWh thermal. A100MW-electric plant at15%conversion over30full-power years needs175TWh:3km³ at perfect recovery,15km³ at20%recovery. The usable exchange volume, fluid path and lifetime are therefore central. [Developer depth concept](https://www.worldgeothermal.org/pdf/IGAstandard/WGC/2020/21090.pdf), [PNNL models](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).

For assumed fluid enthalpy extraction0.8MJ/kg,100kg/s yields80MW heat and12MW electricity at15%conversion, before other loads. SI could improve drilling and reservoir designs enough to move these constraints substantially. It cannot make an unconnected hot rock volume deliver heat through a small exchanger at an arbitrary rate. Natural global heat loss is47TW; short-term heat mining can exceed local replenishment, but then draws down a stock. [Heat-flow measurement](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/1/5/2010/), [PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/numerical-investigation-closed-loop-geothermal-systems-deep-geothermal-reservoirs).

Its volcanic Obsidian site targets hotter resources much shallower. These are reasons to keep geothermal in the portfolio and to demand replicated power-per-well measurements.
