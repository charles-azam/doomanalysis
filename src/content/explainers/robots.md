## Why it matters here

Robots help by making physical work cheap: building, farming, mining, recycling and care. When labour stops being scarce, housing, water systems and food get cheaper. Today there are [4.66 million industrial robots](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years) for [3.74 billion workers](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SL.TLF.TOTL.IN): 3,737 ÷ 4.66 = one robot per 800 workers. Robots can also take jobs and serve as weapons; those harms belong to other cards.

## In numbers

- **Today's pace.** [542,000 industrial robots were installed in 2024](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years). At that rate, with none retired, the stock reaches 4.66 + 0.542 × 76 = 46 million by 2100.
- **One per worker.** Matching today's 3,737 million workers by 2100 means about 3,737 ÷ 75 = 50 million new robots a year, nearly 100 times today's rate, before replacing worn-out ones.
- **Humanoids.** Morgan Stanley expects [nearly 1 billion humanoid robots by 2050, with the price falling from about $200,000 in 2024 to $50,000](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050).

## What each level looks like

- **1–50 million, level 1.** Our judgment: robots stay mostly in car and electronics plants, as now; goods get a little cheaper.
- **50–500 million, level 2.** Our judgment: robots are common in factories, warehouses and large farms.
- **500 million–5 billion, level 3.** Our judgment: one robot per few workers. Heavy and dangerous work is mostly automated where people can afford robots.
- **5–30 billion, level 4.** Our judgment: more robots than workers. Physical work becomes cheap enough to lift over half of humanity a step, if the gains are shared.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published for 2100. The [Morgan Stanley forecast](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050) is a bank's market estimate for 2050, not a probability, and it sees little uptake before the mid-2030s.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Within a few years it could write the control software robots lack: deft hands, balance and coping with messy places. Billions of robots still need factories, motors, batteries and metals, built over decades. Robots that build robots shorten this only once the first factories run, and who owns the robots decides who gains.
