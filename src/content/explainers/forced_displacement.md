## What it is

The number of people forced from their homes by conflict, violence, persecution or human-rights abuses. The UN refugee agency (UNHCR) counts refugees who crossed a border, asylum-seekers, and internally displaced people (IDPs) who fled within their own country. People displaced only by floods, storms and other disasters are tracked separately, by the Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre (IDMC).

## Why it matters

Displacement is collapse in miniature. A displaced family usually loses its home, income, land and local health care at once. Most displacement lasts for years: about 70% of refugees live in protracted situations ([UNHCR via AFP](https://www.geo.tv/latest/668187-number-of-forcibly-displaced-people-dips-to-118-million-un)). Large flows also strain host regions, which are often poor themselves.

## Where it stands

At the end of 2025, UNHCR counted 117.8 million forcibly displaced people. This was down 5.4 million from the end-2024 record of about 123.2 million, the first fall in a decade. Of these, 41.6 million were refugees. About 14.7 million people went home in 2025, including 4.4 million refugees, mostly to Afghanistan, Sudan and Syria. UNHCR warns that many returns are unsafe and may start a new cycle of flight. New crises keep coming: the war that began in February 2026 forced 3.2 million people from their homes in Iran ([UNHCR via AFP](https://www.geo.tv/latest/668187-number-of-forcibly-displaced-people-dips-to-118-million-un)).

IDMC counted 82.2 million internally displaced people at the end of 2025. Of these, 68.6 million fled conflict and 13.6 million fled disasters ([IDMC 2026](https://api.internal-displacement.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/IDMC-GRID-2026-Global-Report-on-Internal-Displacement.pdf)). Conflict caused a record 32.3 million new displacements in 2025. IDMC warns that some of the decline may reflect gaps in data, not real improvement.

No one forecasts the total for 2100. For climate, the World Bank's [Groundswell](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/09/13/climate-change-could-force-216-million-people-to-migrate-within-their-own-countries-by-2050) study projects up to 216 million internal climate migrants by 2050 in six regions, including 86 million in sub-Saharan Africa. That is the pessimistic scenario: high emissions and unequal development. In the climate-friendly scenario it is 44 million ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/climate-change-200-million-move-2050-world-bank-report)). These are people moving within their own country, a different measure from the one this indicator counts. They would still add to the same pressure on poor regions.

## The lines that matter

No agency sets a danger line. The site uses two.
- **123.2 million (the end-2024 record).** A return above it would mean the 2025 dip was a pause, not a turn. This line is a published record, not a tipping point.
- **250 million (judgment).** About 3% of humanity uprooted, roughly double today. At that scale, aid systems that already fall short would fail. Host countries would face pressure on food, water and housing. This line is the site's judgment, not a published threshold.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could improve early warning, logistics and matching of displaced people to jobs and housing. It could also cut the cost of water, shelter and food in camps. It cannot stop the wars and persecution that cause most displacement. Climate-driven moves depend on emissions and adaptation, which are political choices more than technical ones.
