## Why it matters here

A displaced family loses home, income, land and local care at once, and the loss lasts: seven in ten refugees have lived in exile for at least five years ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/mid-year-trends)). The displaced share of humanity therefore reads almost directly on the danger scale. This card is one channel of the civil-war card above it.

## In numbers

- **Today.** 41.7 million refugees + 8.6 million asylum-seekers + 69.4 million people displaced inside their own country − 1.4 million Gaza refugees counted twice = 118.3 million: 1 person in 70, or 1.4% ([UNHCR, mid-2026](https://www.unhcr.org/mid-year-trends)).
- **The rise.** About 41 million of 7.0 billion people in 2010, or 0.6%. Then 123.3 million of 8.2 billion at the end of 2024, or 1.5%, the record ([UNHCR data](https://www.unhcr.org/refugee-statistics), [population](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections)). The share grew 2.5 times in fourteen years.
- **The dip.** The count fell in 2025 as 14.7 million people went back, most of them under pressure or to fragile places ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/global-trends)).
- **The scale.** Level 2 starts at 1% of humanity, 82 million people. Level 3 starts at 10%, 820 million, seven times today's count.

## What each level looks like

Displaced people have lost their home, so each band reads the share on the scale. In Sudan, the worst crisis, 12.6 million people are displaced against 51.7 million living in the country, about one in four ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/mid-year-trends), [population](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections)). So 10% of humanity displaced implies war across about 40% of it, which the parent card already rates level 3.

- **Under 1%, level 1.** Every year from 1990 to 2018 ([UNHCR data](https://www.unhcr.org/refugee-statistics)).
- **1 to 3%, level 2.** Today, up to about 250 million people.
- **3 to 10%, level 2.** 250 to 820 million. Seven in ten refugees live in low- and middle-income countries ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/mid-year-trends)), so the strain lands on poor regions.
- **Over 10%, level 3.** More than one person in ten without a home.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source publishes odds or a forecast for 2100, so the curve is judged. The nearest long-range figure covers a different group, people who move within their own country because of climate: up to 216 million in six regions by 2050, and up to 80% fewer with early cuts in emissions ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/09/13/climate-change-could-force-216-million-people-to-migrate-within-their-own-countries-by-2050)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could improve early warning and aid logistics, and cut the cost of shelter, water and food. It cannot stop the wars and persecution that make people flee. Who may cross a border stays a political decision.
