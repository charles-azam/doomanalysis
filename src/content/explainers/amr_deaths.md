## Why it matters here

Resistance lets infections outlast treatment and [raises the risks of surgery](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/antimicrobial-resistance). It is a slow drag, not a shock: by 2050 [two in three](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718157/) of its deaths will be people over 70. Over a lifetime, its share of deaths is the share of people it kills, which is how the card reads the scale. Every published path stays low in level 2.

## In numbers

- **Today:** 1.14 million deaths in 2021 were attributable to resistant bacteria: the patient would have survived a drug-sensitive infection. That is [14.5 per 100,000 people](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718157/), and 1.14 / 69.7 = 1.6% of that year's [69.7 million deaths](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/births-and-deaths-projected-to-2100).
- **2050:** the reference forecast is 1.91 million deaths, or [20.4 per 100,000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718157/) (range 16.6 to 24.2). Growth and ageing drive the rise: at a given age the rate falls, from 14.2 to 12.4.
- **Reading the scale:** the UN projects [122.9 million deaths](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/births-and-deaths-projected-to-2100) among [10.18 billion people](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections) in 2100, or 1,207 per 100,000. One death in 100 is then 12 per 100,000; one in 10 is 120.
- **The old warning:** [10 million deaths a year by 2050](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1002184), from a 2014 UK review that other researchers call unreliable, would be 103 per 100,000 among 9.66 billion people.

## What each level looks like

- **Under 12, level 1 (derived).** Under 1 death in 100 is due to resistance.
- **12 to 120, level 2 (derived).** 1 to 10 deaths in 100, with today and 2050 at the low end. Other harms do not raise the level: up to [28.3 million people](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/health/cost-antimicrobial-resistance-too-high-ignore) pushed into extreme poverty by 2050, 0.3% of humanity, and [1.8 years](https://www.woah.org/en/amid-the-escalating-impact-of-antimicrobial-resistance-the-global-leaders-group-calls-on-un-member-states-to-take-bold-and-specific-action/) of life expectancy lost by 2035.
- **120 to 300, level 3 (derived).** Over 1 death in 10. Deaths involving the 33 commonest bacteria, drug-sensitive or not, were [13.6% of all deaths](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9763654/) in 2019, so getting here means antibiotics failing against most of them.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source forecasts beyond 2050, so the judges estimate the curve. They can lean on [GRAM's scenarios](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718157/) for 2025–2050: better care and access to antibiotics would avert 92 million deaths of all causes, and new drugs against hard-to-treat Gram-negative bacteria 11.1 million resistance deaths. Governments pledged in 2024 to cut deaths associated with resistance [10% by 2030](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-09-2024-world-leaders-commit-to-decisive-action-on-antimicrobial-resistance).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It could find new antibiotics and rapid tests quickly. Each drug still needs years of human trials, and bacteria evolve around every drug. Many deaths would also fall with [clean water and basic infection control](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718157/), which money fixes more than science.
