## Why it matters here

New medicines help through health: fewer early deaths and longer healthy lives. The card counts novel drugs approved each year by the US regulator's drug centre, [46 in 2025](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2025). Discovery is only half the story: [about 4.5 billion people lacked full coverage of essential health services in 2021](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-09-2023-billions-left-behind-on-the-path-to-universal-health-coverage).

## In numbers

- **Odds.** Only [7.9% of drugs entering human trials are approved](https://www.cphi-online.com/46/resourcefile/11/36/30/2021%20Clinical%20Development%20Success%20Rates%202011-2020%20v17.pdf). 52% pass phase I (is it safe?), 28.9% phase II (does it work?), 57.8% phase III (larger trials) and 90.6% the review: 0.52 × 0.289 × 0.578 × 0.906 = 7.9%.
- **Time.** From first human dose to approval takes [10.5 years: 2.3 in phase I, 3.6 in phase II, 3.3 in phase III and 1.3 in review](https://www.cphi-online.com/46/resourcefile/11/36/30/2021%20Clinical%20Development%20Success%20Rates%202011-2020%20v17.pdf).
- **AI so far.** Molecules from AI-first companies [pass phase I 80–90% of the time, but phase II only about 40%, near past averages, on a small sample](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38692505/). Raising only phase I to 85% gives 0.85 × 0.289 × 0.578 × 0.906 = 13%. Knowing what works in sick people is the bottleneck.

## What each level looks like

- **5–30 a year, level 1.** Our judgment: fewer new drugs than today; health gains come from spreading existing ones.
- **30–100, level 2.** Our judgment: today's pace. A few big advances a decade reach tens to hundreds of millions.
- **100–500, level 3.** Our judgment: treatments for most major killers. How many benefit depends on prices and clinics.
- **500–2,000, level 4.** Our judgment: most diseases, perhaps ageing itself, become treatable. Over half of humanity gains only if drugs are cheap and clinics reach people.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Within a few years it could pick better targets and design molecules that pass safety tests far more often. Dario Amodei argues AI could [compress 50–100 years of biology into 5–10](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace), and that trials move faster when a drug's effect is large. Even a perfect designer cannot skip testing in patients: phases II and III alone take about seven years today. Then each drug must be made, approved country by country and delivered by health workers.
