## Why it matters here

People have always clustered where the yearly average is about 13 °C, with a second cluster near 27 °C; places averaging 29 °C or more held [only 0.3% of people (12 million) in the 1960–1990 climate](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01132-6). Such heat cuts outdoor work, harvests and health, and in humid air sweat stops cooling the body. This card, a channel of the warming card, counts the people living in it.

## In numbers

- **Today.** Under the 2000–2020 climate, [0.9% (62 million)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01132-6), three times the 1960–1990 share.
- **2100.** In a world of 9.5 billion, [1.5 °C puts 5% (0.4 billion) in such heat and 2.7 °C puts 22% (2.1 billion)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01132-6): each 0.3 °C less spares about 410 million.
- **Where.** At 2.7 °C, [India has over 600 million exposed](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01132-6), Nigeria over 300 million, Indonesia about 100 million, the Philippines and Pakistan over 80 million each. At 1.5 °C India falls to about 90 million.
- **Wet-bulb limits.** The wet-bulb temperature, what a wet cloth cools to, combines heat and humidity. The theoretical survival limit is 35 °C, but young healthy adults in humid tests lost control of their body heat at [about 30.6 °C](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00738.2021), and [35 °C has been logged briefly on some subtropical coasts](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw1838). The Middle East and the Indus valley see [brief dangerous spells at 1.5 °C; widespread ones are almost avoided under 2 °C and jump at 3 °C](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305427120).

## What each level looks like

All three are our judgment, with one rule: about one in five people in unprecedented heat loses decent living. Homes can be cooled where there is power; fields and outdoor work cannot.
- **0–5%, level 1.** Under 1% of humanity (5 ÷ 5).
- **5–50%, level 2.** 1% to 10%.
- **50–70%, level 3.** Over 10% (50 ÷ 5).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes odds. We place Lenton's exposure at [Climate Action Tracker's current-policy percentiles](https://climateactiontracker.org/documents/1451/CAT_2026-11-12_GlobalUpdate_COP31.pdf) with straight lines: 2.2 °C (16th percentile) gives 15% (5 + 17 × 0.7 ÷ 1.2), 2.7 °C (median) 22%, 3.4 °C (84th) 31% (22 + 12 × 0.7 ÷ 0.9, toward 34% at 3.6 °C). Lenton's model spread, [±9 points around 23% in 2090](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01132-6), would widen the curve.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheap power and cooling could keep people safe indoors. Farm work, crops and livestock stay outside, and the poorest hot regions need grids and buildings first.
