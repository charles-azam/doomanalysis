## Why it matters here

Fresh water is one of the five essentials. Unsafe water spreads disease, and water fetched from far away costs hours of work every day. "Safely managed" is a strict standard: the water must come from a source on the premises, be available when needed and be free from contamination ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)). A clean public tap down the street does not count.

## In numbers

- **Today.** 73.7% of humanity used safely managed water in 2024, up from 61.3% in 2000 and 67.7% in 2015 ([World Bank/JMP](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). That leaves 2.1 billion people without it, and 106 million drink untreated water straight from rivers, lakes or ponds ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)).
- **Pace.** From 67.7% to 73.7% in 9 years is 0.67 points a year, though 1.7 of those points came in 2024 alone ([World Bank/JMP](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). At that pace the world reaches 90% in (90 − 73.7) ÷ 0.67 = 24 years, around 2048, and 99% around 2062. WHO judges that safe water for everyone by 2030 is slipping away ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)).
- **Town and country.** In rural areas coverage rose from 50% to 60% since 2015, while in cities it has stagnated ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)).
- **The rich world** is at 95.8% ([World Bank/JMP](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). Even there, water that is contaminated does not count as safe ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)).

## The lines on the card

- **61.3%:** the world in 2000.
- **90%:** nine in ten people.
- **95.8%:** the rich world today.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no projection of safe water access to 2100. The curve is judged.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheaper energy makes desalination and treatment affordable, and better sensors can find leaks and contamination. Pipes, treatment plants and the utilities that run them still have to be built, paid for and maintained.
