## Why it matters here

Living longer is not the same as living well. Healthy life expectancy (HALE) is the number of years a newborn can expect to live in full health, given this year's rates of death and disease at each age ([WHO](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/indicators/indicator-details/GHO/gho-ghe-hale-healthy-life-expectancy-at-birth)). A year with a serious illness counts as only part of a year. Health is one of the five essentials, and this card shows how much of a life it actually covers.

## In numbers

- **Today.** The world's HALE was 62.8 years in 2023 ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000002?$filter=SpatialDimType%20eq%20'GLOBAL'%20or%20SpatialDimType%20eq%20'WORLDBANKINCOMEGROUP')), against a life expectancy of 73.3 ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000001?$filter=SpatialDimType%20eq%20'GLOBAL')). 73.3 − 62.8 = 10.5 years lived in poor health.
- **Rich and poor.** High-income countries reach 67.5 healthy years, low-income countries 55.4 ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000002?$filter=SpatialDimType%20eq%20'GLOBAL'%20or%20SpatialDimType%20eq%20'WORLDBANKINCOMEGROUP')). Singapore is highest at 72.9 and the Central African Republic lowest at 44.7: a 28-year gap ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000002?$filter=Dim1%20eq%20'SEX_BTSX'%20and%20TimeDim%20eq%202023)).
- **Longer lives, more sick years.** High-income countries have a life expectancy of 79.8 ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000001?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20'WB_HI'%20and%20Dim1%20eq%20'SEX_BTSX')) but 67.5 healthy years: 12.3 years in poor health, more than the world's 10.5. Where lives are longer, more of the added years are spent in poor health.
- **Trend.** HALE rose from 57.9 in 2000 to 63.2 in 2019 ([WHO](https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/WHOSIS_000002?$filter=SpatialDimType%20eq%20'GLOBAL'%20or%20SpatialDimType%20eq%20'WORLDBANKINCOMEGROUP')), about 0.28 years a year. COVID-19 pulled it to 61.4 in 2021; by 2023 it was back to 62.8.

## The lines on the card

- **57.9 years:** the world in 2000.
- **67.5 years:** the rich world today.
- **72.9 years:** Singapore today, the highest.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no projection of HALE to 2100. The Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) projects it to rise from 64.8 years in 2022 to 67.4 in 2050 ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/05/240517164149.htm)). For 2022, IHME's 64.8 is 2.5 years above WHO's 62.3, so only the rise of 2.6 years carries over to this card. The 2100 curve is judged.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Faster drug discovery and cheaper diagnosis could cut the years lived with disease, and slowing aging itself would lift this most. New treatments still need trials, approval and health workers to reach people.
