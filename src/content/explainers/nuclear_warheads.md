## Why it matters here

The stockpile sets the ceiling on a nuclear war. Most of the harm would come from smoke: burning cities loft soot high into the sky, dim the sun for years and cut harvests worldwide. In [Xia et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0), a few hundred warheads on cities reach level 3; a few thousand, level 4. The count says much less about whether a war starts, which depends on crises, doctrine and leaders. So this card has no danger levels; the danger is read on the top card.

## In numbers

- At the start of 2026, nine states held about [12,187 warheads](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/yb26_summary_en_1.pdf). Of these, 9,745 were in military stockpiles, 4,012 were deployed and 2,100 to 2,200 were on high alert.
- The total peaked at about [70,300 in 1986](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/). 12,187 / 70,300 = 17%: a cut of over four-fifths.
- The total still falls only because the United States and Russia take old warheads apart. SIPRI [expects this to reverse](https://www.sipri.org/media/press-release/2026/increasing-focus-nuclear-weapons-amid-heightened-escalation-risks-new-sipri-yearbook-out-now). China grew from [600 to up to 620](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/yb26_summary_en_1.pdf) warheads in 2025.
- In Xia et al., 4,400 warheads of 100 kilotons (100,000 tonnes of TNT each) kill or starve 85% of people if trade stops. The 4,012 deployed today are close to that number.

## The lines on the card

- **100.** Small warheads on cities kill 27 million and starve 255 million: 282 million of 6.7 billion, 4% (Xia et al.).
- **4,400.** A full war between the United States and Russia: 360 million killed and 5.3 billion starved, 85% (Xia et al.).
- **70,300, the 1986 peak.** About six times today's total (70,300 / 12,187 = 5.8).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes a forecast of the count for 2100. [SIPRI](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/yb26_summary_en_1.pdf) expects new warheads to soon outpace those dismantled. On use, superforecasters in the [XPT tournament](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf) of 2022 gave 4% for nuclear weapons killing over 10% of humanity by 2100, and nuclear experts 8%.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It could make arms-control checks cheap and reliable. It could also make hidden missiles and submarines easy to find, which weakens each side's ability to strike back and pushes states to build more. Dismantling warheads still takes treaties, trust and years of work.
