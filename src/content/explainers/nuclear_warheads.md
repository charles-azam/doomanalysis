## What it is

The number of nuclear warheads held by the nine nuclear-armed states: the United States, Russia, the United Kingdom, France, China, India, Pakistan, North Korea and Israel. The count includes warheads ready for use, warheads in storage and retired warheads waiting to be taken apart.

## Why it matters

Nuclear war is one of the few events that could end modern civilization within weeks. Most of the long-run harm would come from smoke, not blast. Burning cities would loft soot into the upper atmosphere, dim the sun and cool the planet for years. In [Xia et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0), 100 small (15-kiloton) warheads on cities in an India–Pakistan war killed 27 million people directly. Up to 255 million more were left without food by the end of year two if trade stopped. A US–Russia war with 4,400 warheads left 5.3 billion without food.

## Where it stands

At the start of 2026 the world held about 12,187 warheads. Of these, 9,745 were in military stockpiles and 4,012 were deployed. About 2,100–2,200 were on high alert ([SIPRI 2026](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/yb26_summary_en_1.pdf)). The total peaked at about 70,300 in 1986 ([FAS](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/)). It still falls slowly, but only because the US and Russia dismantle old warheads. SIPRI expects new warheads to soon outpace dismantling. China grew from 600 to up to 620 warheads in 2025, and all nine states kept modernizing.

Nobody publishes a forecast for 2100. In the [XPT tournament](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf) (2022), superforecasters expected about 13,500 warheads by 2040, and nuclear experts about 11,990.

The risk of use matters more than the count. On [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/4779/), 535 forecasters give a 34% chance of a nuclear detonation in war by 2050. In the XPT, the median superforecaster gave 4% to a nuclear catastrophe killing over 10% of humanity by 2100, and the median nuclear expert gave 8%. For extinction, the figures were 0.074% and 0.55%.

## The lines that matter

These lines count warheads detonated in war, not warheads stockpiled.
- **1 detonation.** The first use since 1945 would break an 80-year taboo. It would also make further use more likely.
- **About 100 on cities.** In Xia et al., this puts about 255 million people at risk of famine.
- **About 500 larger (100-kiloton) warheads on cities.** This puts 2.5 billion at risk. At this scale, deaths could pass 10% of humanity, the XPT's definition of a catastrophe.
- **About 4,400.** A full US–Russia exchange: nuclear winter, with 5.3 billion at risk of famine.

Conditional on any detonation by 2050, Metaculus forecasters give 39% to more than 100 detonations ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/11602/)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could make arms-control verification cheap and reliable. It could also speed up famine relief, for example with food grown without sunlight. But it could make hidden missiles and submarines easier to find. That would weaken second-strike forces, the ability to retaliate after being hit, and push leaders to strike first. It could also shorten decision times. SI does not change the motives for war, or the physics of soot in the stratosphere.
