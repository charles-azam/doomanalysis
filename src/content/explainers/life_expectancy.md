## What it is

Life expectancy at birth is how long a newborn would live if this year's death rates at each age stayed the same for its whole life ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.DYN.LE00.IN)). It is a summary of current mortality, not a forecast for any real person. It is strongly pulled down by deaths of babies and young children. A one-year shock such as a pandemic shows up at once, even if most people's real lifespans barely change.

## Why it matters

Few indicators sum up the five essentials so well. Hunger, unsafe water, no electricity for clinics, and violence all show up as early deaths. A country can't sustain abundance while its life expectancy is falling. A deep, lasting drop is one of the clearest signs of collapse.

## Where it stands

Globally, life expectancy reached 73.3 years in 2024, 8.4 years more than in 1995. COVID-19 pulled it from 72.6 in 2019 down to 70.9, and it has since recovered ([UN Summary of Results](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)). France is at 83.0, Nigeria at 54.6 and DR Congo at 62.1 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.DYN.LE00.IN), 2024).

The UN medium projection gives the world 77.4 years in 2054 and 81.7 in 2100. The 80% range for 2100 is 80.1 to 83.1 ([WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/1_Indicator%20(Standard)/CSV_FILES/WPP2024_Demographic_Indicators_Medium.csv.gz); [probabilistic](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/4_Mortality/UN_PPP2024_Output_e0.xlsx)). France reaches 92.1 (87.0 to 97.2) and Nigeria 64.7. These ranges reflect normal year-to-year uncertainty. The UN model contains no wars, famines or pandemics, so it says nothing about catastrophe risk.

## The lines that matter

Both lines are our judgment, anchored to the UN's own history series.

- **65 years.** That is about where the world stood in 1995 (64.9). Falling below it would erase three decades of gains. From the UN quantiles, Nigeria has about a 52% chance of still being below 65 in 2100 and DR Congo about 15%. For the world and for France the model gives well under 1%, but only because it leaves out catastrophes. Our own estimate, including war and pandemic risk, is about 3% for the world dipping below 65 in some year before 2100.
- **50 years.** That is about the world level in 1960 (47.8). It would mean mass early death and a health system failing most people.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could speed up drug discovery, diagnosis and the design of vaccines. It could also make good care cheaper where doctors are scarce. These are the channels behind most of the projected gains, so the upside is real. But today's biggest gaps come from known, cheap causes: newborn care, clean water, nutrition and basic medicines. The UN notes that low-cost treatments could save millions of lives in the next decade. That is a matter of delivery and money, not new knowledge. Superintelligence also cuts the other way. The same tools that design medicines could help design pathogens, which is a risk this indicator would only register after the fact.
