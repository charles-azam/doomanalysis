## Why it matters here

Fusion joins hydrogen nuclei: deuterium, which can be extracted from ordinary water, and tritium, which a plant must breed from lithium ([ITER](https://iter.org/sci/fusionfuels)). Until the first plants, aimed at the [early 2030s](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8577/iaea-publishes-2026-world-fusion-outlook/), it helps nobody; it reaches level 3 only above 30% of world electricity.

## In numbers

- In April 2025 the US National Ignition Facility got 8.6 MJ (megajoules) out of a fuel pellet hit with 2.08 MJ of laser light, a gain of 4.13 ([LLNL](https://annual.llnl.gov/sites/annual/files/2026-02/llnlannual25-3-national-ignition-facility.pdf)). Each shot draws about 300 MJ from the grid ([Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/national-ignition-facility-surpasses-long-awaited-fusion-milestone)), so the output is 8.6 / 300 ≈ 3% of the electricity used.
- Europe's JET made a record 69 MJ over 5 seconds from 0.2 mg of fuel before closing in 2023 ([UK Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/jets-final-tritium-experiments-yield-new-fusion-energy-record)): 69 / 5 ≈ 14 MW.
- ITER aims for 500 MW of fusion from 50 MW of heating, but its deuterium-tritium runs are not due before 2039 ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/iter-fusion-reactor-hit-by-massive-decade-long-delay-and-e5bn-price-hike/)).
- From a gain in one shot to a power plant: the whole plant must make more electricity than it draws, fire continually ([Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/national-ignition-facility-surpasses-long-awaited-fusion-milestone)) and breed its own tritium, since only 20–30 kg exists, from Canadian heavy-water reactors ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/the-fusion-industry-must-rise-to-its-tritium-challenge/)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 1%, level 0.** Our judgment: no plants, or a few that other sources could replace.
- **1 to 10%, level 1.** Our judgment: a costly extra source in rich countries, like fission today.
- **10 to 30%, level 2.** Our judgment: steady power for hundreds of millions.
- **30 to 50%, level 3.** Our judgment: a main source for billions.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The IAEA's [2026 World Fusion Outlook](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8577/iaea-publishes-2026-world-fusion-outlook/), citing a EUROfusion study, puts fusion anywhere from a very small share to 20% of electricity by 2100. These are scenarios, not odds. Firms raised $4.48 billion in the year to July 2026, and 71% of 56 surveyed expect a plant to feed a grid by the 2030s ([World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/fusion-industry-raised-usd45-billion-over-past-year-report-says)). Commonwealth Fusion Systems aims for first plasma in 2027 and a 400 MW plant in the early 2030s ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/07/fusion-power-commonwealth-sparc-nuclear-fusion-pilot-ai-siemens-nvidia/)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Control helps first: an AI already shaped plasmas in a Swiss tokamak ([Degrave et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850200/)), and it could speed magnet and materials design. Testing materials under neutrons, breeding tritium, licensing and building plants still take years.
