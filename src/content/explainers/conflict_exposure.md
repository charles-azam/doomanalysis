## Why it matters here

Security is one of the five essentials. People who live near fighting lose it first, and clinics, schools, food and power often go with it. This card counts people near violence, not people harmed: a riot down the road and a siege both count. That is why it has no danger levels. It shows how far conflict reaches, and the top card shows what it costs.

## In numbers

- ACLED, a group that records political violence worldwide, found [17% of humanity](https://acleddata.com/newsletter/raleigh-report-december-2025), one person in six, exposed to conflict from mid-November 2024 to mid-November 2025. Its [annual review](https://acleddata.com/report/whats-driving-conflict-today-review-global-trends) gives 16%, but also 831 million people, which is only 10% of [8.2 billion](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL). ACLED's own figures thus range from 10% to 17%.
- A year earlier the figure was [one in eight](https://acleddata.com/report/conflict-index-results-december-2024), or 12.5%. 17 / 12.5 = 1.36: about a third more in one year.
- ACLED counts people within [1, 2 or 5 km](https://acleddata.com/conflict-exposure) of an event, depending on its type and intensity.
- PRIO, a peace research institute in Oslo, counts people within 50 km of a deadly event instead. On that measure, [almost one woman in five](https://www.prio.org/news/3735) and [23% of children](https://www.prio.org/comments/1934) lived near conflict in 2025.

## The lines on the card

- **12.5%, one in eight.** ACLED's figure for 2024.
- **25%, one in four.** The UN Secretary-General says [over a quarter of all people](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/12/1166677) now live in places touched by conflict. He does not use ACLED's distance rule, so the two are not directly comparable.
- **50%, half of humanity.** No measure has come close. In our judgment it would take war in several of the most populous countries at once.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes a forecast for 2100. ACLED's series is short and moves fast. PRIO's longer series points the same way: the number of women near conflict has [nearly tripled since 1990](https://www.prio.org/news/3735), and the share of children has more than [doubled since the mid-1990s](https://www.prio.org/comments/1934).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Better forecasting and mediation could shrink conflict zones. Drones and long-range strikes can widen them. Who lives near fighting depends on politics and on where wars break out, which no technology decides.
