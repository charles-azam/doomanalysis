## Why it matters here

Pensions, health care and long-term care are paid for and given mostly by people of working age. As the ratio rises, taxes go up, benefits go down, people work longer or output per worker must rise. None of that is doom, but it narrows the margin for other shocks. The ratio is a head count: it says nothing about who actually works or how healthy older people are.

## In numbers

- **Today.** The world had 16.0 people aged 65 or over per 100 of working age in 2025, up from 10.0 in 1990 ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SP.POP.DPND.OL?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). That is 100 ÷ 16 = about six people of working age per older person.
- **Ahead of the world.** The rich world is at 31.0 ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SP.POP.DPND.OL?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)) and Japan at 51.0, two people of working age per older person ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/JPN;KOR;SGP/indicator/SP.POP.DPND.OL?format=json&date=2025)). Japan shows that a rich country can run at that level.
- **The UN path.** The UN's median rises to 25.8 in 2050 and 40.2 in 2100 ([UN WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_Annual_OldAgeDepRatio.xlsx)): 100 ÷ 40.2 = 2.5 people of working age per older person, between the rich world today (3.2) and Japan (2.0). By 2080, people aged 65 or over will outnumber children under 18 ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)).

## The lines on the card

- **25.8:** the UN's median for 2050.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The UN's probabilistic projections are the published points: a median of 40.2 in 2100, an 80% range of 36.8 to 43.7 and a 95% range of 35.3 to 45.5 ([UN WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_Annual_OldAgeDepRatio.xlsx)). The range is narrow partly because everyone who will be 65 or over in 2100 is born by 2035; the rest of the spread comes from future births, which set the number of working age, and from how long people live. In our reading the model does not allow for a breakthrough against aging, which would push the ratio up, or for a catastrophe.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It cannot change the head count much. It could change what it means: if machines do much of the paid work and some of the care, the number of workers per older person matters far less. Who gets the gains is still decided by taxes and transfers.
