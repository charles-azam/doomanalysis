## Why it matters here

Mines dig ore; refineries turn it into the pure metals and chemicals factories use. For six energy minerals, the International Energy Agency (IEA) tracks the share of world refining held by the top three countries. A high value means one government's export licence can stall batteries, grids and motors elsewhere. It harms people only through the mineral cards, so it has no danger scale of its own.

## In numbers

- **Today:** the 2024 shares were copper 59%, lithium 96%, nickel 78%, cobalt 89%, graphite 99% and rare earths 97%. Their average, 518 ÷ 6 = 86%, matches the IEA's figure, up from about 82% in 2020, because some 90% of new supply came from the single leading country: Indonesia for nickel, China for cobalt, graphite and rare earths ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **The top country alone:** its average share, rare earths aside, rose from 70% in 2023 to 72% in 2025 ([IEA 2026 via ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/critical-mineral-concerns-shift-from-meeting-demand-to-securing-supply-chains-iea/)).
- **What a cut-off means:** for battery metals and rare earths, supply outside the leading country would meet only about half of the remaining demand in 2035 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). China's wider rare earth controls of October 2025, if enacted fully, could put $6.5 trillion of yearly production elsewhere at risk ([IEA 2026 release](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)).

## The lines on the card

- **59%, copper:** the least concentrated of the six in 2024 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **77%, the top three miners:** mining of the same minerals is less concentrated than refining (same source).
- **82%, 2020 and 2035:** the 2020 level, and where announced projects bring the average by 2035 (same source).
- **99%, graphite:** the most concentrated in 2024 (same source).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. Rare earths show change is possible: the top refiner's share fell from over 90% in 2023 to 85% in 2025 and could reach 70% by 2035 ([IEA 2026 release](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)). Against that, projects outside the leading countries cost 20% to over 150% more to build ([IEA 2026 via Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)). We could not open the 2026 edition's top-three figure, so today's value comes from the 2025 edition.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could design cheaper refining chemistry and substitutes that remove chokepoints. It cannot skip the years of permits and construction, or change a government's choice to use export controls.
