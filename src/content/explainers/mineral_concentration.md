## What it is

Mining digs ore out of the ground. Refining turns it into the pure metals and chemicals that factories use: lithium salts, cobalt sulphate, battery-grade graphite, rare-earth oxides. This factor takes each key energy mineral, finds the share of world refining held by the single largest country, and averages those shares. Rare earths are left out of the average. A value of 100% would mean one country refines everything.

## Why it matters

Batteries, wind turbines, electric motors, power grids and solar panels all need these refined materials. If one country controls most of the refining, it can cut supply with an export licence, and others cannot replace it quickly because new refineries take years to build. This does not cut off food or water directly. It can slow the shift away from fossil fuels, raise its cost, and give one government leverage over everyone else's industry.

## Where it stands

The International Energy Agency (IEA) puts the average top-refiner share at 72% in 2025, up from 70% in 2023 ([ANI via The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/critical-mineral-concerns-shift-from-meeting-demand-to-securing-supply-chains-iea/); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)). Indonesia leads nickel; China leads almost everything else. China refines about 94% of battery-grade graphite, 76% of cobalt, 74% of lithium, 47% of copper and 31% of nickel, according to IEA figures summarised by the [Bank of Finland](https://www.bofit.fi/en/monitoring/weekly/2026/vw202632_2/).

Concentration is rising. Over the past two years, the top refiners supplied more than three-quarters of all growth in refined output ([IEA release via Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)). Export controls are now in use. China restricted seven heavy rare earths in April 2025, announced wider controls in October 2025, then suspended the wider ones for a year (same source). In November 2026 it extended that pause to 10 January 2027 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/11/09/china-extends-pause-on-rare-earth-export-curbs-for-two-months/)). The IEA estimates that $6.5 trillion of yearly production outside China could be at risk if the controls were fully applied ([Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)).

The outlook is slow change. In its 2025 edition, the IEA measured the top three refiners instead of the top one: their average share rose from about 82% in 2020 to 86% in 2024 ([Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/iea-critical-minerals-report/)) and was projected to ease only to 82% by 2035 ([Hydrocarbon Processing](https://www.hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2025/05/low-diversity-in-critical-mineral-markets-could-hurt-industry-iea-says/)). Rare earths are the one place where change is visible. The top refiner's share fell from over 90% in 2023 to 85% in 2025, and could reach about 70% by 2035 if planned projects are built ([Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)). No one publishes a forecast to 2100.

## The lines that matter

- **50%, one country refines half.** This is the site's own judgment, not an official line. Above it, a single government can cause a global shortage. Today's 72% is well above it.
- **85%, near-monopoly.** Also a judgment. It matches rare earths in 2025, where alternatives barely existed.

No agency publishes an official danger threshold for this measure.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence (SI) could speed up exploration, design cheaper and cleaner refining chemistry, and find substitutes, such as batteries without cobalt or motors without rare earths. These are the most likely routes to a lower number. SI cannot remove the time needed to permit, finance and build refineries. Projects outside the dominant countries already cost 20% to over 150% more to build ([Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)). SI also cannot change a government's choice to use export controls.
