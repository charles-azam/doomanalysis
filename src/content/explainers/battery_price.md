## What it is

The average price of a lithium-ion battery pack, in US dollars per kilowatt-hour of storage, from BloombergNEF's (BNEF) annual survey. It blends packs for electric cars, buses and grid storage, weighted by the volume sold.

## Why it matters

Solar and wind produce power when the weather allows. Batteries shift that power into the evening and steady the grid, and they replace engines in vehicles. Cheaper batteries mean less oil and gas burned, cheaper transport and more reliable clean power. Very cheap storage is a precondition for the "near-free energy" version of abundance.

## Where it stands

BNEF's December 2025 survey found an average of $108/kWh, a record low and 8% below 2024 ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour)). Packs for grid storage averaged $70/kWh, electric-car packs $99/kWh, and packs using lithium iron phosphate (LFP), a cheaper chemistry, $81/kWh. Packs in China averaged $84/kWh, while North American packs cost 44% more and European packs 56% more (same source).

The long-run fall is steep. BNEF's series starts at $1,436/kWh in 2010 ([Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/batteries/chart-lithium-ion-battery-prices-fall-yet-again)). Progress is not smooth. Prices rose once, to $151/kWh in 2022 ([The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2023/11/28/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-hit-record-low-tipped-to-nearly-halve-again-by-2030/)). In 2025, metal prices jumped 124% between January and October, yet pack prices still fell ([Bloomberg via Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/716937/business/why-global-battery-prices-are-expected-to-drop-again-in-2026)).

For 2026, BNEF expects about $105/kWh, a smaller 3% fall because of raw-material costs and tariffs ([Bloomberg via Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/716937/business/why-global-battery-prices-are-expected-to-drop-again-in-2026)). Its forecasts move. In 2023 it expected $80/kWh in 2030 ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-hit-record-low-of-139-kwh/)). In 2024 it expected prices below $100/kWh in 2026 and $69/kWh by 2030 ([Bloomberg via Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/price-ev-battery-packs-falls)). Its newest long-term numbers are behind a paywall, and no one publishes a forecast to 2100.

## The lines that matter

- **$100/kWh, electric-car parity.** BNEF cites this as the price at which electric cars match petrol cars on upfront cost ([Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/price-ev-battery-packs-falls)). Electric-car packs have been below it for two years running ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour)); the all-segment average has not yet crossed it.
- **$150/kWh, back to the 2022 spike.** A judgment line. A return there would signal a lasting materials or trade shock.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Battery chemistry is a search across materials, which is where superintelligence (SI) should help a lot: new cathodes and anodes, solid electrolytes, cheaper chemistries and better factories. BNEF already expects such technologies to drive the next round of price falls ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour)). SI cannot make lithium, nickel or graphite appear faster than mines and refineries can be built, and it cannot remove tariffs. Prices could keep falling, but supply chains and trade policy will still set a floor.
