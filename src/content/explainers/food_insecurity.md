## What it is

This indicator comes from the Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES). It is a survey of eight short questions about the last 12 months: did you worry about running out of food, eat less healthy food, skip meals, go a whole day without eating, because there was not enough money or other resources ([SDG 2.1.2 metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-02-01-02.pdf)).

- **Moderate** food insecurity usually means people cannot regularly eat a healthy, balanced diet.
- **Severe** food insecurity means they very likely ate less than they needed.

In many countries the data come from the Gallup World Poll, which interviews about 1,000 people per country. FAO says the confidence interval is rarely wider than a fifth of the measured rate. That is about ±5 points at a rate of 50%, and about ±1 point at 10%.

## Why it matters

Food is one of the five essentials. The undernourishment rate counts calories. This one asks people directly whether they can afford enough food, so it reacts faster to price spikes and lost income. Abundance means at least 90% of people are food secure. A collapse would show up first as a jump in severe food insecurity.

## Where it stands

In 2025, 25.8% of the world, about 2.1 billion people, were moderately or severely food insecure. That is down from 27.1% in 2024 and 28.7% in 2020, but still above pre-pandemic levels ([FAO, SOFI 2026](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)). The regional gaps are wide. The rate is 56.6% in Africa and 20.3% in Asia, against 8.7% in Northern America and Europe.

France is at 9.4%, of which 3.3% severe. Nigeria is at 76.5% and DR Congo at 81.7% ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS); [severe](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.SVFI.ZS), 2024). The World Bank series still shows 28.0% for the world in 2024. SOFI 2026 has since revised that year down to 27.1%.

No agency publishes a projection of this indicator, so there is no official 2100 outlook.

## The lines that matter

Both lines come from this site's definitions.

- **10%.** Above it, abundance is impossible. The world is far above it today. Our estimate is a 65% chance it is still above 10% in 2100: getting below would need every region at today's richest-region level. France rose from 5.8% in 2019 to 9.4% in 2024, just under the line, so our estimate is a 60% chance it crosses in some year.
- **50%.** A majority food insecure is the collapse line. Our estimate for the world is 5%. The COVID-19 shock moved the rate by only a few points. Several countries are already above 50% on this measure, but "moderate" includes people who cut diet quality. Severe food insecurity is a fairer test of a lost essential.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

This indicator is mostly about money: whether households can afford food. Superintelligence could make food cheaper to grow, store and move, and it could raise incomes. Both would push the rate down. But if its gains go mainly to owners of capital while jobs disappear, people could become more food insecure even as food gets cheaper. The outcome depends on how incomes, transfers and prices are shared, which is a political choice rather than a technical one.
