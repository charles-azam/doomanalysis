## Why it matters here

The Sun sometimes throws a cloud of charged gas at Earth. It shakes Earth's magnetic field, and long power lines pick up the disturbance, which can [trip a grid in under a minute](https://hydroquebec.com/learning/notions-de-base/tempete-mars-1989.html). The harm is the loss of electricity, and with it pumped water, cooling and payments. Storm strength is measured by Dst: how far the magnetic field drops, in nanoteslas (nT). A severe storm before 2100 is likely, but its reach is limited: level 1, perhaps level 2 for a storm stronger than any on record.

## In numbers

- **Beyond 500 nT.** Such a storm has a [4% chance each year](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/526698/): 1 − 0.96^75 = 95% by 2100.
- **The 100-year storm** is [663 nT](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/extreme-event-magnetic-storm-probabilities-derived-rank-statistics-historical-dst): 1 − 0.99^75 = 53%.
- **Carrington-class.** The 1859 Carrington storm is put at [800 to 1,750 nT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2024_solar_storms). An 809 nT storm is a [1-in-151-year event](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/526698/): 1 − (150/151)^75 = 39%.
- **People.** Lloyd's found a Carrington-level storm would cut power to [20–40 million people in the US](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2013/05/23/293060.htm) for weeks to two years. Level 2 starts at 1% of humanity, about 80 million.

## What each level looks like

- **300–850 nT, level 0.** Our judgment, from two storms. The 1989 one, [589 nT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2024_solar_storms), blacked out Quebec for [nine hours](https://hydroquebec.com/learning/notions-de-base/tempete-mars-1989.html). The May 2024 one, 412 nT, [caused no significant outages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2024_solar_storms).
- **850–1,500 nT, level 1.** Derived from the Lloyd's figure: tens of millions without power for weeks or more, under 1% of humanity, though other continents would add some. The May 1921 storm, [907 nT, comparable to Carrington](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/intensity-and-impact-new-york-railroad-superstorm-may-1921), damaged railway telegraph and telephone lines in New York.
- **Over 1,500 nT, level 2.** Our judgment, because no study models a storm this strong. We judge that outages lasting months could then reach more than 80 million people. Experts [disagree](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2017/severe-space-weather/) on whether outages would last days or months.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The three points are the rates above. For a Carrington-class storm of 850 nT, sources disagree, so we publish no point there; the nearest, 809 nT, comes from the most recent study. Riley's [12% per decade](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2017/severe-space-weather/) gives 1 − 0.88^7.5 = 62% by 2100. Riley and Love give [10.3% or 3.0% per decade](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/extreme-geomagnetic-storms-probabilistic-forecasts-and-their-uncertainties), depending on the statistical model: 56% or 20%. Love and colleagues give [1.13 per century](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/lognormality-historical-magnetic-storm-intensity-statistics-implications-extreme-event): 57%. Lloyd's uses a [150-year return period](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2013/05/23/293060.htm): 39%.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It could forecast storms better and design grids that ride through them. The protection is hardware: transformers, spares and shielding have to be installed before the storm arrives.
