## Why it matters here

Magnets are the leading use of rare earths worldwide ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)), above all in electric car motors and wind turbines ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). Rare earths are less than 1% of a car's value, so tripling their price adds about 0.1% to it ([IEA 2026 via Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)). A cut-off halts factories but takes no essentials from people, so this card stops at level 1.

## In numbers

- **Years of reserves:** over 75 Mt ÷ 0.39 Mt mined in 2025 = over 190 years ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)).
- **Risk of being cut off:** China, the United States and Australia mined 270 + 51 + 29 = 350 of 390 kt (90%), China alone 69% ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)). The top three refining countries held 97% in 2024 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). China's share of refining fell from over 90% in 2023 to 85% in 2025, and could reach 70% by 2035 ([IEA 2026 release](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)).
- **Demand:** the four magnet rare earths grow from 91 kt in 2024 to 150 kt in 2040 under today's policies. Planned supply covers 105% of 2035 needs: the concern is concentration, not quantity ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **Recycling and substitutes:** scrap and old magnets could meet 27% of magnet demand in 2035 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). Substitutes exist for many uses but work less well ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 30%, level 0:** magnet prices soar and some car plants pause. Our judgment, from two cases. China's 2010 export quota fell 15,000 to 20,000 t short of use outside China, with about 125,000 t sold worldwide: 15,000 ÷ 125,000 = 12%, or 16% at the upper figure ([NMBU case study](https://arken.nmbu.no/~robega/ECN320/Case%20studies/Case_study_Rare_earth-elements.pdf)). China's April 2025 controls on seven elements ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)) made some carmakers cut or pause output ([IEA 2026 release](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/17072026/supply-concentration-export-restrictions-and-declining-investment-put-critical-mineral-security-at-risk/)). Neither took essentials from people.
- **30 to 100%, level 1:** factories stall, and cars, wind power and electronics stay dearer for years. Our judgment: costly and slow to fix, but not a loss of food, water or shelter.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. The odds turn on politics: China suspended its wider October 2025 controls for one year ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)), and refineries planned outside the dominant suppliers could process only about two-thirds of the ore expected there by 2035 ([IEA 2026 via Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/economy/explainer-why-the-iea-says-critical-minerals-have-become-a-65-trillion-economic-security-risk/4000476)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could design motors that need fewer rare earths and cheaper separation chemistry. New plants still take years to build, and export controls are a political choice.
