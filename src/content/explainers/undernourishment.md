## What it is

The prevalence of undernourishment (PoU) is the share of people whose usual food intake does not give enough energy for a normal, active, healthy life ([World Bank/FAO](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS)). It is not a head count from surveys. FAO models it from national food supply, the spread of consumption across households, and calorie needs by age and sex ([SDG 2.1.1 metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-02-01-01.pdf)). It cannot say which individuals are hungry. FAO says the margin of error would likely exceed ±2.5 points in most cases. So national values under 2.5% are not published, and the World Bank shows them as 2.5. France's 2.5 means "below 2.5%", not exactly 2.5%. The indicator counts calories only, not vitamins or diet quality.

## Why it matters

Food is one of the five essentials. Abundance requires at least 90% of people to be fed, which means a PoU under 10%. Collapse means a majority losing reliable access to food for five years or more. Famine, conflict and price shocks all show up here, though with a lag.

## Where it stands

In 2025, 7.8% of the world, about 645 million people, faced hunger. That is down from 8.1% in 2024 and 8.6% in 2022, the third yearly fall in a row ([FAO, SOFI 2026](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)). Africa, at 20.0%, now has more hungry people than Asia: about 309 million against 292 million. DR Congo is at 39.5%, Kenya at 34.9% and Nigeria at 22.2% ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS), 2024).

FAO projects 510 to 520 million hungry people in 2030, about 6% of the world, without counting the 2026–27 El Niño. FAO's older scenarios for 2050 range from 3.5% in a "towards sustainability" world to 12.4% in a "stratified societies" world, with 7.6% for business as usual ([FAO 2018](https://openknowledge.fao.org/3/I8429EN/i8429en.pdf)). They start from an older, higher baseline, so their levels are not directly comparable with today's. We found no FAO or IFPRI projection to 2100.

## The lines that matter

Both lines come from this site's definitions.

- **10%.** Above it, abundance is impossible. On today's data series, the world was above 10% as recently as 2006, at 11.0% ([World Bank/FAO](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS)). Our estimate is a 25% chance it goes back above 10% at some point before 2100. FAO's own pessimistic scenario crosses this line, and a war, a volcanic winter or a failure in several breadbaskets at once could do it for a year or more.
- **50%.** A majority hungry is the collapse line. Our estimate for the world is 2%, essentially a global food-system collapse.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

FAO's 2018 study concludes that ending hunger requires cutting poverty and inequality, and that a fairer spread of income is the most effective lever. SOFI 2026 names conflict, weather extremes and cuts to aid as the main threats to progress. Superintelligence could raise crop yields, improve weather and pest forecasts, and cut waste, all of which help. It cannot by itself get food through a siege line or raise the incomes of the poorest. Those depend on peace and on who captures the gains.
