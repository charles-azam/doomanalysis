## Why it matters here

Materials help only through other technologies: cheaper batteries and solar cells, catalysts for fertiliser and fuels, membranes for clean water. The card measures how long a new material takes from discovery to first use, [10 to 20 years](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26723/chapter/3) by the US National Academies' account. Faster materials speed up the energy, water and food cards; we count only that speed-up here, so we stop at level 3.

## In numbers

- **A classic case.** John Goodenough showed the cobalt oxide cathode, the battery's positive side, [in 1980; the first viable lithium-ion battery came in 1985 and sales began in 1991](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/press-release/): 1991 − 1980 = 11 years.
- **AI discovery.** Google DeepMind's GNoME model predicted [2.2 million new crystals, about 380,000 of them stable, and outside labs had made 736](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/millions-of-new-materials-discovered-with-deep-learning/) by late 2023: 736 ÷ 380,000 = 0.2%.
- **Doubts.** Two chemists who checked the list found [little sign of compounds that were at once new, credible and useful](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11044265/). Critics of a robot lab that made some of them argued [it had found no new materials](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article). Prediction is fast; proving a material works is not.

## What each level looks like

- **0.5–2 years, level 3.** Our judgment: batteries, catalysts and membranes improve almost as fast as software. Energy and water get cheaper years sooner for billions, if factories keep up.
- **2–10 years, level 2.** Our judgment: faster than today, so gains arrive sooner, but building factories still sets the pace.
- **10–40 years, level 1.** Our judgment: today's pace. Materials matter, but each new one takes a decade or two to reach use.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published for 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. A US programme has aimed to [cut the development cycle by more than half](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26723/chapter/3); the National Academies say much has been done but gaps remain.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Within a few years it could predict candidate materials in days and run robot labs that make and test them in weeks. A battery or catalyst must still prove it lasts for years in real use, and the plants that make it in bulk take years to build.
