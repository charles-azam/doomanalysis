## Why it matters here

Batteries took 88% of the world's lithium in 2025, for electric cars, grid storage and electronics ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-lithium.pdf)). A shortfall makes them dearer and slower to spread. It takes no food, water or shelter from anyone directly, and sodium-ion batteries, which need no lithium, are gaining ground ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)), so this card stops at level 1.

## In numbers

- **Years of reserves:** 37 Mt ÷ 0.29 Mt produced in 2025 (United States output withheld) = about 128 years. Known resources are about 150 Mt ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-lithium.pdf)).
- **Risk of being cut off:** Australia, China and Chile mined 92 + 62 + 56 = 210 of 290 kt in 2025 (72%) ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-lithium.pdf)). The top three refining countries held 96% in 2024. China makes 70% of lithium chemicals but also uses about three quarters of the world's lithium ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **Demand:** 205 kt in 2024, 700 kt in 2035 and 1,160 kt in 2050 under today's policies, 5.7 times as much. Planned mines cover 61% of 2035 needs; closing the gap would take 55 more average-sized mines ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). The 2026 edition still sees a deficit through 2035 ([ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/critical-mineral-concerns-shift-from-meeting-demand-to-securing-supply-chains-iea/)).
- **Recycling:** it cuts the need for new mines by over 20% by 2050 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 20%, level 0:** batteries cost more and spread more slowly. Our judgment: prices rose eightfold in 2021–22 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)) on a gap the USGS first put at (134,000 − 130,000) ÷ 134,000 = 3%, leaving out US output ([USGS 2023](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2023/mcs2023-lithium.pdf)). Revised figures show 146,000 t produced and 142,000 t used, a small surplus ([USGS 2024](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2024/mcs2024-lithium.pdf)). Nobody lost essentials.
- **20 to 80%, level 1:** storage stalls and clean power gets dearer, hitting the poorest first. Our judgment: a lasting battery-metal shock could raise battery pack prices by 40–50% ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)), a heavy cost but not a loss of essentials.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. The IEA's 40% gap counts only announced projects, and new lithium projects are much easier to develop than copper ones: prices fell by over 80% after 2023 as supply outgrew demand ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could speed new ways to pull lithium from brines and clays, and better sodium batteries that need none. Mines and refineries still have to be permitted, financed and built.
