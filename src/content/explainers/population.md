## Why it matters here

Population is the denominator of this site. Every essential has to be provided per person, so more people means more food, homes and energy to share, and fewer means fewer workers. On its own it is neither doom nor abundance. A sharp fall would be the clearest sign of catastrophe.

## In numbers

- **Today.** 8.23 billion people in 2025, up 70 million, or 0.9%, from 8.16 billion in 2024 ([UN WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_PopTot.xlsx)). The World Bank has 8.22 billion ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)).
- **The peak.** The UN expects growth for another 50 to 60 years, to a peak of about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s, then 10.2 billion by 2100. It puts the chance that the peak comes this century at 80%, against about 30% in its 2013 projections ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)).
- **Lower than expected.** The 2100 figure is about 700 million below the UN's projection of a decade ago, partly because fertility fell faster than expected, in China above all ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)).

## The lines on the card

- **4.1 billion:** half of today, a catastrophe without precedent.
- **6.16 billion:** the world in 2000 ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)).
- **8.8 billion:** the 2100 projection of the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/07/200715150444.htm)).
- **10.3 billion:** the UN's expected peak.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The UN's probabilistic projections are the published points: a median of 10.18 billion in 2100, an 80% range of 9.41 to 11.03 billion and a 95% range of 9.05 to 11.44 billion ([UN WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_PopTot.xlsx)). IHME's 2020 study expected an earlier, lower peak, about 9.7 billion in 2064, and 8.8 billion in 2100 ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/07/200715150444.htm)). The UN report gives IHME's 95% range as 6.8 to 11.8 billion, and the Wittgenstein Centre's 2100 figure as 9.9 billion ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)). We use the UN as the most authoritative and recent. In our reading none of them allows for a global catastrophe, so the judges add the lower tail.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It barely changes the number of people already born, and the world is already down to 2.25 births per woman, from 3.31 in 1990 ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)). Slowing aging could raise numbers late in the century; an AI catastrophe could cut them.
