## What it is

Gross domestic product (GDP) per person: everything a country produces in a year, divided by its population. It is converted at purchasing power parity (PPP), an exchange rate that makes a dollar buy the same basket of goods everywhere. The figures here are from the [World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD), in 2021 international dollars, so inflation is removed.

## Why it matters

Income is the budget for every essential. On this site, abundance is a ratio: the five essentials must cost under 10% of national income. That ratio can fall because essentials get cheaper or because incomes rise. Income also buys resilience, such as hospitals, sea walls and spare power plants.

GDP has blind spots. It is an average, so it says nothing about who gets the money. It counts repairs after a disaster as output. It leaves out unpaid work at home.

## Where it stands

In 2025 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD)):

- Norway $95,200, the United States $76,900, Sweden $63,400, Germany $62,800
- France $55,100, the United Kingdom $54,000, Japan $48,300
- China $25,100, Brazil $20,000, India $10,000, Nigeria $8,100
- World $21,900

The [IMF](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/PPPPC@WEO) projects world income per person rising from $26,200 in 2025 to $34,700 in 2031. These figures are in current dollars, so they include inflation.

For 2100 the main reference is the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs), standard scenarios used by climate researchers. In early OECD runs of the middle scenario, SSP2 ([Dellink](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/330258/files/6444_Dellink.pdf), in 2005 dollars), income per person in 2100 reaches:

- $101,600 in the US and $101,300 in China
- $83,900 in France
- $77,800 in India and $57,000 in Nigeria

Across scenarios, world income ranges from just over $25,000 to more than $150,000. A 2023 update is "less optimistic across all scenarios" ([Crespo Cuaresma and Zellmann](https://iamconsortium.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Crespo-presentation.pdf)).

## The lines that matter

- **$1,095 a year, danger.** This is the World Bank's extreme poverty line of $3.00 a day ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2025/06/05/june-2025-update-to-global-poverty-lines)), times 365. A country producing less than this per person would stay poor even if income were shared perfectly evenly.
- **$3,030 a year, limit.** This is the $8.30-a-day poverty line the Bank uses for upper-middle-income countries, times 365.
- **The abundance link.** By our estimate from [World Bank](https://databank.worldbank.org/source/icp-2021), [WHO](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.XPD.CHEX.GD.ZS) and [SIPRI](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/MS.MIL.XPND.GD.ZS) data, essentials cost about 26% of world output today. If spending on them stayed flat per person, incomes would need to rise about 2.6-fold for that share to fall to 10%. SSP2 implies growth of that size by 2100, but history shows essentials spending, especially on health, rises with income.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It can raise output. If AI and robots do most work, income per person could grow far faster than the roughly 1–2% a year typical of these scenarios. Musk calls this "universal high income" ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/economy/musk-predicts-ai-create-universal-high-income-make-saving-money-unnecessary)).

It cannot decide who receives the output. Average GDP can soar while many people still lack essentials. GDP may also miss abundance itself: something that becomes free adds almost nothing to GDP, even when everyone uses it.
