## Why it matters here

Wild species [pollinate crops, clean water and protect coasts](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2019/new-study-global-modeling-of-natures-contributions-to-people). As they decline, the harm reaches people through diets and water long before a species is gone. We count species threatened or extinct because experts have estimated it for 2100. Even beyond what they expect, it stays at level 2.

## In numbers

- **Today and 2100:** 3,331 surveyed experts put [30% of species](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10399722) threatened or extinct since 1500 (bounds 16 to 50%). They expect 37% by 2100 on current trends, and 25% with every known conservation measure.
- **The Living Planet Index:** 35,803 monitored wildlife populations shrank by [73% on average](https://ourworldindata.org/data-insights/the-living-planet-index-reports-a-73-average-decline-in-studied-wildlife-populations) from 1970 to 2022. That is an average rate of change, not the share of animals or species lost, and about half of the populations did not decline.
- **Pollination today:** too few pollinators cut fruit, vegetable and nut harvests by 3 to 5% and cost [about 427,000 lives a year](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9749483). Against [61.7 million deaths in 2023](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/births-and-deaths-projected-to-2100), that is 0.43 ÷ 61.7 = 0.7% of deaths. An earlier model cited there finds that losing all pollinators would cost about 1.4 million lives a year: 1.4 ÷ 61.7 = 2.3%.

## What each level looks like

- **10 to 30%, level 1.** Our judgment: less harm than today.
- **30 to 37%, level 1.** Derived: today's pollination gap causes [under 1 death in 100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9749483).
- **37 to 50%, level 2.** Our judgment: losses approach those of losing all pollinators (2.3% of deaths), and wild foods fail too.
- **50 to 70%, level 2.** Our judgment: beyond the experts' upper bound. At half of species they see [up to 70% less](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10399722) of what nature gives people, and up to [5 billion people](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2019/new-study-global-modeling-of-natures-contributions-to-people) could face dirtier water and weaker pollination by 2050. Facing poorer services is not losing decent living: losing every pollinator costs 2.3% of deaths, and the soil, fish and fresh-water cards keep such losses below 1 in 10.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The one published point is the survey median, 37% on current trends. The experts' 20 to 50% range is the median of their own low and high guesses, not a 5 to 95% range, so we leave it out. Species already go extinct [over ten times faster](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_35009/component/file_35022/PlanetaryHealthCheck2026.pdf) than the safe limit.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Higher yields could free farmland for nature, and monitoring would improve. Extinct species do not return, habitats regrow over decades, and freeing land is a political choice.
