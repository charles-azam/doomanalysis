## Why it matters here

Growing food, pumping water, making steel and running hospitals all take energy, so energy use per person is close to a physical measure of a living standard. Jean-Marc Jancovici argues that machines now do most of the physical work, and that an economy is unlikely to grow faster than its energy supply ([Jancovici](https://jancovici.com/en/energy-transition/energy-and-us/what-is-energy-actually/)). Very low use means poverty. Very high use is not needed for decent living.

## In numbers

- **Today.** The world used 20,258 kWh per person in 2025, the rich world 52,512, the United States 75,051 and India 7,419 ([Energy Institute via OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/per-capita-energy-use)). The rich world uses 2.6 times the world average.
- **Trend.** World use per person rose from 12,470 kWh in 1965 to 20,258 in 2025: 1.6 times in 60 years, 0.8% a year. In the rich world it fell from 56,973 kWh in 2000 to 52,236 in 2024 ([OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/per-capita-energy-use)), while consumption per person rose 46% ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/NE.CON.PCAP.PP.KD?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). These figures cannot tell how much of that is efficiency and how much is industry moved abroad.
- **Muscle against machines.** A human body can deliver at most about 150 kWh of mechanical work a year, and Jancovici puts the work done by machines at about 200 times our muscles on average ([Jancovici](https://jancovici.com/en/energy-transition/energy-and-us/what-is-energy-actually/)).
- **A floor.** Decent housing, food, health, mobility and communication for everyone could take 13 to 18.4 GJ of final energy per person a year, 15.3 GJ on average ([Millward-Hopkins et al.](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/16764/1/1-s2.0-S0959378020307512-main.pdf)). 15.3 × 1,000 ÷ 3.6 = 4,250 kWh. Final energy is what reaches users; the primary energy on this card is larger, because power plants lose much of it as heat.

## The lines on the card

- **4,250 kWh:** decent living for all, in final energy.
- **52,512 kWh:** the rich world today.
- **75,051 kWh:** the United States today.
