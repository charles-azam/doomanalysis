## Why it matters here

Food is one of the five essentials, and hunger is where its failure shows first. Famine, war and price shocks all register here. FAO models the measure from the average calories people consume in a country and how unevenly they are spread, rather than counting hungry people one by one ([SDG metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-02-01-01.pdf)), so it counts calories, not vitamins or diet quality.

## In numbers

- **Today.** 7.8% of humanity, about 645 million people, faced hunger in 2025, down from 8.1% in 2024 and 8.6% in 2022: the third fall in a row ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)).
- **Where.** Africa, at 20.0%, now has more hungry people than Asia: about 309 million against 292 million ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)).
- **Pace.** The share was 13.0% in 2001 ([World Bank/FAO](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). From 13.0% to 7.8% in 24 years is 0.22 points a year. FAO expects 510 to 520 million hungry people in 2030 ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)).
- **The floor.** FAO's margin of error would likely exceed 2.5 points, so it does not publish national values below 2.5% ([SDG metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-02-01-01.pdf)). The rich world shows 2.55%, at that floor ([World Bank/FAO](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)): 2.5% is, in practice, hunger ended.

## The lines on the card

- **2.5%:** hunger ended, as far as FAO can measure.
- **13%:** the world in 2001.
- **20%:** Africa today.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

FAO's latest projection stops at 2030. Its 2018 scenarios for 2050 range from 3.5% on a path towards sustainability to 12.4% in a world of divided societies, with 7.6% for business as usual ([FAO 2018](https://openknowledge.fao.org/3/I8429EN/i8429en.pdf)). We found no published odds for 2100, so the curve is judged. In our judgment, a nuclear war, a large volcanic eruption or failed harvests in several breadbaskets at once could push it far up for a year or more; those risks have their own cards.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Better crops, forecasts and logistics could raise yields and cut waste. FAO names conflict, weather extremes and cuts in aid as the threats to progress, and 2.69 billion people cannot afford a healthy diet ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/un-report--global-hunger-levels-ease-for-third-consecutive-year-as-regional-disparities-persist/en)). Intelligence does not end wars or pay for food by itself.
