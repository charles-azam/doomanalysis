## Why it matters here

Copper is the metal of electricity: in the United States, buildings take 42% of copper products and electrical goods 23% ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf)). A shortfall does not empty shelves: prices rise until some buyers wait or switch, so clean energy comes slower and dearer ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). Wires already in place keep working, so this card stops at level 1.

## In numbers

- **Years of reserves:** 980 Mt ÷ 23 Mt mined in 2025 = about 43 years ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf)), up from 540 ÷ 15.8 = 34 years in 2009 ([USGS 2010](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/mineral-pubs/copper/mcs-2010-coppe.pdf)). Reserves grow with mining, so this is not a countdown.
- **Risk of being cut off:** Chile, Congo and Peru mine 11.2 of 23 Mt (49%); China, Congo and Chile refine 18.5 of 29 Mt (64%), China alone 48% ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf)). China also uses almost 60% of refined copper, so it buys more than it sells ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **Demand:** refined use grows from 27 Mt in 2024 to 33 Mt in 2035 and 37 Mt in 2050 under today's policies. Planned mines cover 70% of the mined copper needed in 2035: a 30% gap, over 40% on a net-zero path ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). The 2026 edition puts the main gap at 25% ([ANI](https://timesofoman.com/article/174904-copper-supply-may-face-25-deficit-by-2035-despite-record-prices-iea)).
- **Recycling and substitutes:** scrap meets under 17% of demand, almost 35% by 2050 with policy ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). Aluminium already replaces copper in power cable ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-copper.pdf)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 15%, level 0:** prices jump and projects slip. Our judgment: the largest yearly gap we found, in 2004, was 0.72 ÷ (17.4 − 0.87) Mt used = 4% ([USGS 2005](https://apps.usgs.gov/minerals-information-archives/copper/mis/mis-200501-coppe.pdf)), and left no lasting harm.
- **15 to 60%, level 1:** grids and clean power come slower and dearer; the poorest wait longer. Our judgment: such a gap slows new wiring but takes no power from people who have it.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. The IEA's gaps count only announced projects. Ore grades have fallen 40% since 1991, and a new mine takes about 17 years from discovery ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). Philibert and Arndt reply to those who see scarcity: reserves keep rising, and the real risks are slow mine growth and concentration ([Ifri 2025](https://www.ifri.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/ifri_arndt_and_philibert_copper_supply_june_2025.pdf)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

AI-based exploration could cut drilling costs by up to 60% and quadruple discovery rates ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). It cannot shorten the permits and construction of a mine.
