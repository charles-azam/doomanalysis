## Why it matters here

Warming makes heat waves, downpours and droughts more frequent. This card tracks one telling measure: how often a heat extreme that struck once in 50 years before industry now strikes. It has no danger band, because frequency does not translate into a share of humanity: disaster deaths have fallen as warnings improved, while losses rise. The harm is counted on the warming card.

## In numbers

- **Heat.** At about 1 °C, the 2001–2020 average, a once-in-50-years heat extreme already came [4.8 times in 50 years](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf). At 4 °C it would come 39.2 times, in 78% of years (39.2 ÷ 50), and run 5.3 °C hotter.
- **Rain and drought.** A once-in-10-years downpour comes [1.3 times as often at 1 °C, 1.7 at 2 °C and 2.7 at 4 °C](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf); a once-in-10-years drought in drying regions 1.7, 2.4 and 4.1 times.
- **Cost.** In 2025 natural disasters did [US$224 billion of damage](https://www.munichre.com/en/company/media-relations/media-information-and-corporate-news/media-information/2026/natural-disaster-figures-2025.html), $108 billion of it insured, and killed about 17,200 people; weather caused 92% of the damage. 17,200 ÷ 8 billion is 0.0002% of humanity a year.
- **Trend.** Weather, climate and water disasters killed [just over 2 million people in 1970–2021](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/economic-costs-of-weather-related-disasters-soars-early-warnings-save-lives), about 38,000 a year (2 million ÷ 52), and cost $4.3 trillion; early warnings cut the toll. Heat kills far more than these tallies show: [546,000 people a year on average](https://www.who.int/news/item/29-10-2025-climate-inaction-is-claiming-millions-of-lives-every-year--warns-new-lancet-countdown-report).

## The lines on the card

- **8.6 times:** a world 1.5 °C warmer.
- **13.9 times:** 2 °C warmer.
- **39.2 times:** 4 °C warmer (all from [IPCC Figure SPM.6](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf)).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We place the IPCC frequencies at [Climate Action Tracker's current-policy percentiles](https://climateactiontracker.org/documents/1451/CAT_2026-11-12_GlobalUpdate_COP31.pdf), drawing a straight line between 2 and 4 °C ((39.2 − 13.9) ÷ 2 = 12.65 more per °C): the 16th percentile, 2.2 °C, gives 16.4; the median, 2.7 °C, 22.8; the 84th, 3.4 °C, 31.6. Above 1.5 °C (8.6) is 99%, as on the warming card. The IPCC's 5–95% model ranges, such as 6.9–16.6 times at 2 °C, would widen the curve.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Better forecasts and early warnings save lives, as they already have. The extremes themselves follow the warming, which no forecast reduces.
