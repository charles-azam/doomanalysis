## Why it matters here

Gas is a quarter of world energy supply, 151 of 600 EJ in 2025 ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)). It heats homes, makes power and is the main feedstock of ammonia, 77% in an older IEA estimate ([CTCN](https://www.ctc-n.org/technologies/ammonia)), and ammonia fertiliser feeds about half of humanity ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-people-does-synthetic-fertilizer-feed)). Coal already makes about 22% of ammonia ([Ammonia Energy Association](https://ammoniaenergy.org/articles/iea-publishes-ammonia-technology-roadmap)). Our judgment: a slow decline by 2100 harms far less than a sudden cut-off.

## In numbers

- **Reserves.** 208,813 billion m³ (bcm) ÷ 4,172 bcm produced in 2025 = 50 years. Russia, Iran and Qatar hold half: 104,653 of 208,813 bcm ([Eni](https://www.eni.com/content/dam/enicom/documents/eng/visione/wer/2026/03_WER-2026-Natural-Gas.pdf)).
- **Few exporters.** The US (147 bcm), Qatar (111) and Australia (105) ship 63% of the world's 579 bcm of liquefied gas (LNG) ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)).
- **Europe.** The EU produced 34 bcm and used 329 in 2025, so (329 − 34) ÷ 329 = 90% came from outside it ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)). Russia still sent about 13% of EU imports; its LNG is banned from 2027 and its pipeline gas from autumn 2027 ([EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/ukraine/russian-gas-imports-council-gives-final-greenlight-stepwise-ban_en)).
- **Hormuz.** Its closure in 2026 cut off Persian Gulf gas exports, Qatar's included ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)). By November the IEA counted 60 bcm lost; nearly 90% of the LNG through the strait had gone to Asia ([Shafaq News](https://shafaq.com/en/Economy/IEA-Hormuz-disruptions-cut-oil-supply-by-2-8bn-barrels)).

## What each level looks like

- **0–1,000 bcm, level 1.** Our judgment: under a quarter of today. Fertiliser and heat must come from electricity, hydrogen or coal; where they do not, the poorest pay. Losing all gas would cut supply by a quarter, level 1 or 2 on the parent card.
- **1,000–3,000 bcm, level 0.** Our judgment: gas kept for the hardest uses.
- **3,000–10,000 bcm, level 0.** No scarcity; burning it is counted under climate.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes odds for 2100. For 2050, the IEA has 5,800 bcm under current policies, 4,800 under stated policies and 4,100 with a stronger response to the 2026 crisis ([OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/economics-markets/news/55420309/iea-sees-potential-for-looser-oil-lng-markets-after-hormuz-crisis)). Pathways limiting warming to 1.5 °C cut gas use by a median 45% from 2019 ([IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf)): 3,985 bcm in 2019 ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)) × 0.55 = 2,190 bcm.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheaper electrolysers and heat pumps could make gas easy to replace. Terminals, pipelines and factories take years to build, and geology fixes who holds the reserves.
