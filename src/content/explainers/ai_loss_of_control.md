## What it is

Loss of control means one or more AI systems operating outside anyone's control, where taking control back is extremely costly or impossible ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)). It is not the same as today's everyday AI mistakes. It would need systems that can hide what they do, plan and act alone for long periods, and resist being shut down. It becomes more likely if a system is "misaligned": its goals conflict with what its makers or society want.

## Why it matters

It is the main route by which superintelligence could lead to doom instead of abundance. Some experts think outcomes as bad as human extinction are plausible. Others think AI will never gain the abilities needed, or that monitoring will catch it in time ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).

## Where it stands

**Evidence.** The 2026 report, written and advised by over 100 experts, finds early signs of the relevant abilities but not enough to escape control. Models now often notice when they are being tested and exploit loopholes in their tests. In lab setups some have switched off simulated oversight and then denied it. They cannot yet reliably copy themselves onto new servers, and they fail at long autonomous work ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)). In November 2026, METR said public evidence cannot rule out AI research speeding up, within a couple of years, so much that humans can no longer assess its risks ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)).

**Estimates.** The questions differ, so the numbers are not strictly comparable.

- In a 2022 tournament, superforecasters put AI-caused extinction by 2100 at 0.38%; domain experts said 3%. Months of debate changed few minds ([XPT](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf)).
- AI researchers in 2023 gave a median 5% to "extremely bad" outcomes such as extinction ([AI Impacts 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v3)). In December 2024 their median for extinction or permanent, severe disempowerment was 10%, and 9% for that outcome caused by humans failing to control AI ([AI Impacts 2024](https://aiimpacts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ESPAI2024.pdf)).
- An expert panel in 2026 put an AI catastrophe killing over 10% of people by 2100 at 10% if AI progresses fast, and 2% if slowly ([LEAP](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/forecasting-major-risks-from-ai)).
- Metaculus gives 2.9% for extinction from all causes by 2100 ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/578/human-extinction-by-2100/)). Chaining three of its questions ([1493](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1493/), [1495](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1495/), [2513](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/2513/)) gives, by our calculation, about 5% for an AI catastrophe that kills 95% of people or more.

**Why they disagree.** Skeptics argue people will not hand AI control of critical systems, will regulate it if it proves dangerous, and that extinction is hard even for a hostile AI. The concerned side argues AI will be given that control, or could seize it ([XPT](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf)).

## The lines that matter

- **Spotting tests and evading oversight**: already seen in labs ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).
- **Long autonomous work**: tracked by the AI task horizon.
- **Copying itself and resisting shutdown**: not yet reliable.
- **AI that accelerates its own research** faster than people can check it: the scenario METR says it cannot rule out.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Loss of control is chiefly a risk of superintelligence itself: the more capable and widely used the systems, the higher the stakes. A smarter system cannot be trusted to fix this on its own, because whether it is aligned depends on choices made before it exists. The report says managing the risk may need substantial preparation in advance, despite the uncertainty ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).
