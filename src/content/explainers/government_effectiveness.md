## What it is

The World Bank's Government Effectiveness indicator summarises perceptions of public services, the civil service and its independence from political pressure, and how well policies are made and carried out ([World Bank definition](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/GE.EST?format=json)). It combines 35 cross-country sources, mostly surveys of firms, households and experts, for 1996 to 2025 ([WGI](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators)). The estimate runs from about −2.5 (worst) to +2.5 (best) and is centred near zero across countries.

It has three limits. It measures perceptions, which lag real reforms, and the World Bank says to treat it as an estimate, not a rating. It is relative: if every government got worse together, the scores would barely move. A 0–100 score anchored to fixed reference points now exists for tracking absolute change. And it is uncertain: France's standard error is 0.22, so the 90% band is about ±0.36.

## Why it matters

Power grids, water utilities, food safety nets, hospitals and police are run or regulated by governments. Effective states recover from shocks. Weak ones let a drought or a price spike become a famine. Collapse is far more likely where the state cannot deliver, and abundance needs a state that can.

## Where it stands

France scores 1.21 in 2025, down from 1.57 in 1996 and 1.37 in 2023. Norway is at 1.84, Sweden at 1.82, Japan at 2.04 and the United States at 1.19. At the bottom are DR Congo at −1.67 and South Sudan at −2.25 ([WGI data](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/GE.EST?format=json)).

Twenty-six countries score below −1.0. They hold about 7.2% of the world's people. No one publishes governance forecasts. But population growth is fastest in low-scoring countries. If every country kept its 2025 score, the population-weighted world average would fall from 0.16 to −0.16 by 2100, using UN population projections ([WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/1_Indicator%20(Standard)/CSV_FILES/WPP2024_Demographic_Indicators_Medium.csv.gz)). The share of people in countries below −1.0 would double, to about 14.3%. This is our own calculation, not a forecast.

## The lines that matter

Both lines are our judgment.

- **0 (below average).** Public services are worse than in the typical country. Large falls are rare for strong states. Of 29 places scoring 1.0 or more in 1996, only Puerto Rico later fell below zero. From that base rate, we put France's chance of falling below 0 by 2100 at about 8%.
- **−1.0 (fragile zone).** Many countries here are in or near conflict or state failure, including Haiti, Sudan, Yemen and Myanmar. No high-scoring country has fallen this far since 1996. We put France's chance at about 2%.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could make administration much cheaper and better: faster tax collection, fewer errors in benefits, better planning of infrastructure. That could lift weak states quickly. It could also do the reverse. Tools that make a state efficient also make surveillance and control efficient, and a captured state can be very "effective" at the wrong goals. This indicator measures capacity, not consent or fairness. Superintelligence cannot supply what makes institutions work: trust, accountability, and the will to serve citizens.
