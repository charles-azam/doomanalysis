## What it is

On this site, superintelligence (SI) means AI systems that outperform the best humans at virtually every cognitive task, including science, engineering and strategy, and that are deployed widely. This follows philosopher Nick Bostrom's classic idea of an intellect that "greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintelligence)). Forecasters use nearby terms. Metaculus counts an AI as superintelligent if it can do any task humans could do in 2021 as well as the best humans ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/9062/how-many-months-from-weak-agi-to-first-superintelligence/)). Surveys ask about "high-level machine intelligence": machines doing every task better and more cheaply than human workers ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ESPAI2024.pdf)). Wide deployment matters because a system kept in a lab changes little.

## Why it matters

SI is the biggest swing factor on this site, and it pushes both ways.

- **Toward abundance.** Anthropic's chief executive Dario Amodei argues AI could fit 50–100 years of biology progress into 5–10 years ([Amodei](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). Cheap expert work could cut the cost of energy, water, food and health care.
- **Toward doom.** An SI whose goals differ from ours could operate outside anyone's control, and some experts think outcomes as bad as extinction are plausible ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)). Over 79,000 people have signed a call to ban building SI until it is shown to be safe and controllable and has public support ([FLI](https://superintelligence-statement.org/)).

## Where it stands

No system meets the definition today. Forecasts disagree, but most put SI within this century.

- Metaculus forecasters expect weakly general AI around August 2027 ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/3479/date-weakly-general-ai-is-publicly-known/)) and a median of 23 months from there to SI ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/9062/how-many-months-from-weak-agi-to-first-superintelligence/)). Combining the two, we derive about 40% for SI by 2030, 80% by 2050 and 88% by 2100.
- Manifold traders give 39% by 2030 ([Manifold](https://manifold.markets/IsaacKing/will-there-be-a-superintelligence-b-10fdd93e8092)) and 86% by 2100 ([Manifold](https://manifold.markets/IsaacKing/will-there-be-a-superintelligence-b)), assuming no catastrophe first.
- AI researchers surveyed in December 2024 gave human-level machine intelligence even odds by 2042 ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ESPAI2024.pdf)).
- In 2022, superforecasters gave 1% that philosopher Nick Bostrom would declare general AI achieved by 2030, and 75% by 2100; a small expert group gave 9% and 87% ([XPT](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf)).
- The AI Futures Project, among the most bullish, gives 35–65% for AI that beats top experts at virtually all tasks before 2028 ([AI Futures](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q35-timelines-update-looks-like-the)).

## The lines that matter

- **AI that speeds up AI research.** METR estimates one leading developer's capability gains now run about twice their earlier pace ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment)). This feedback loop, described by I. J. Good in 1965, is the classic route from general AI to SI ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/9062/how-many-months-from-weak-agi-to-first-superintelligence/)).
- **Top-expert level at nearly all tasks.** The SI line itself.
- **Wide deployment** in the economy, government and the military, where benefits and dangers become real.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Intelligence is not the only bottleneck. Amodei lists limits: experiments in the physical world take fixed time, some questions lack data, some systems are chaotic, laws and institutions move slowly, and physics sets hard limits ([Amodei](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). SI might design cheaper desalination or vaccines quickly, but building plants, running trials and reaching the poorest places still take years. It also cannot settle on its own who controls it, which is the heart of the doom question.
