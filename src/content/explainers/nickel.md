## Why it matters here

Steels and alloys take more than 85% of the nickel used in the United States ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)). Clean technologies, mainly electric car batteries, took 562 of 3,371 kt worldwide in 2024 and reach 42% of demand by 2040 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). A shortfall makes steel and batteries dearer. Low-nickel steels and nickel-free batteries exist, so this card stops at level 1.

## In numbers

- **Years of reserves:** over 140 Mt ÷ 3.9 Mt mined in 2025 = at least 36 years. Known resources exceed 350 Mt, and seabed deposits hold about 4.5 billion tonnes ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)).
- **Risk of being cut off:** Indonesia, the Philippines and Russia mined 2.6 + 0.27 + 0.2 = 3.07 of 3.9 Mt (79%), Indonesia alone 67% ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)). The top three refining countries held 78% in 2024 ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).
- **Demand:** 3,371 kt in 2024 and 5,685 kt in 2040 under today's policies, almost double by 2050. Planned mines cover 85% of 2035 needs, and earlier-stage projects could close the rest ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)). For now the market is in surplus: 182,000 t in 2024 ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)).
- **Recycling and substitutes:** scrap, mostly stainless steel, supplies about 60% of US use ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)). Recycling cuts the need for new mines by 15% by 2050, and nickel-free LFP batteries hold nearly half the electric car market ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 25%, level 0:** steel and batteries cost more and makers switch chemistry. Our judgment: the largest recent gap, in 2021, was 166,600 ÷ 2,772,500 t used = 6% ([INSG via Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2023-02-21/update-1-nickel-market-oversupplied-112200-tonnes-2022-insg-says)), and passed without lasting harm.
- **25 to 80%, level 1:** steel goods and batteries stay dearer for years. Our judgment: low-nickel steels and titanium alloys can stand in for many uses ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-nickel.pdf)), so the harm is mostly cost, felt first by the poorest.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. The main risk is concentration, not geology: Indonesia supplied most of the recent growth, and the IEA expects mining to become more concentrated, not less ([IEA 2025](https://sites.utexas.edu/mineralstransition/files/2025/05/Extract_GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2025-compressed.pdf)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could design batteries and steels that use less nickel, and cleaner refining. It cannot move the ore, most of which lies in Indonesia, or shorten the years needed to build mines and refineries.
