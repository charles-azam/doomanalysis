## What it is

"Safely managed" drinking water must meet three conditions. It comes from an improved source that is on the premises, available when needed, and free of faecal and priority chemical contamination ([SDG 6.1.1 metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-06-01-01.pdf)). It is a strict standard. A clean public tap ten minutes away does not count, and a piped supply that fails a single test for E. coli does not count either. Data on water quality are patchy, and definitions of availability and safety are not yet standard across countries. There is no estimate at all for China, Argentina, Kenya, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia or Australia ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS)). A looser measure, "at least basic" water, counts an improved source within a 30-minute round trip ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.BASW.ZS)). It covers far more people.

## Why it matters

Fresh water is one of the five essentials. Unsafe water spreads disease, and fetching water from far away costs hours every day. Abundance needs safe water at home for nearly everyone. Collapse would show up as large numbers of people falling back on unsafe or distant sources.

## Where it stands

Worldwide, 73.7% of people used safely managed water in 2024, up from 67.7% in 2015 ([World Bank/JMP](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.SMDW.ZS)). That still leaves 2.1 billion people without it, and 106 million drink untreated surface water ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/26-08-2025-1-in-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-to-safe-drinking-water---who--unicef)). In fragile countries, coverage is 38 percentage points lower than elsewhere ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/fast-facts-1-4-people-globally-still-lack-access-safe-drinking-water-who-unicef)).

France is at 99.7%, Mexico at 43.0%, Ethiopia at 13.5% and DR Congo at 11.9%. On the basic measure, the world is at 91.5% and Mexico at 99.5% ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SH.H2O.BASW.ZS), 2024). WHO says universal safe water by 2030 looks increasingly out of reach. We found no published projection to 2100.

## The lines that matter

Both lines come from this site's definitions.

- **90%.** Below it, abundance is impossible. The world is already below it. Our estimate is a 40% chance that it is still below in 2100. The recent pace would reach 90% around mid-century, but the standard is strict and growth is fastest where coverage is lowest.
- **50%.** A majority without safe water is the collapse line. Our estimate for the world is 4%. Caution: on this strict measure, functioning countries such as Mexico are already below 50%. The basic measure is a better test of collapse.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could cut the cost of water testing, leak detection, treatment and network planning, and it could help regulators collect the quality data that are missing today. But safe water at home needs pipes, pumps, treatment plants, electricity and decades of maintenance paid for by someone. Those are physical assets and institutions. Superintelligence can design them faster. It cannot fund or maintain them where states are weak or at war.
