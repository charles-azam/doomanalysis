## Why it matters here

Higher seas let storms and tides reach further inland until land is lost. The water rises over decades and people can build defences or move, so we put it at level 2 at most by 2100. It keeps rising [for centuries after that](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf).

## In numbers

- **Today, about 8 cm.** At the end of 2025 seas stood [11 cm above January 1993](https://library.wmo.int/records/item/69807-state-of-the-global-climate-2025). The 1995–2014 baseline is centred on mid-2004: 11.5 years × 2.65 mm a year, the 1993–2011 rate, ≈ 3 cm, and 11 − 3 = 8.
- **Faster.** The rate rose to [4.75 mm a year over 2012–2025](https://library.wmo.int/records/item/69807-state-of-the-global-climate-2025). Held steady, that adds 4.75 × 75 ≈ 36 cm, so 44 cm by 2100; the IPCC's middle 56 cm needs further speed-up.
- **People on land that floods.** About 250 million live below today's yearly flood level, and by 2100 under high emissions [up to 630 million would](https://iwlearn.net/marine/documents/31242): 630 ÷ 10,200 ≈ 6% of the [10.2 billion expected in 2100](https://www.dawn.com/news/1845492). Living there is not losing a home: defences decide that.

## What each level looks like

- **0–50 cm, level 1.** Worse floods, absorbed by sea walls and planned retreat. Our judgment.
- **50–100 cm, level 1.** Floods that came once a century come yearly at [60–82% of tide gauges by 2100](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter09.pdf). With sensible defences, [4 to 72 million people migrate this century](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/migration-land-loss-and-costs-to-2100-due-to-coastal-flooding-und/): at most 72 ÷ 10,200 = 0.7%.
- **100–200 cm, level 2.** If defences are not upgraded, [up to 187 million people are displaced at up to 2 m](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/news/2010/11/global-temperature-increase-of-four-degrees.page): 187 ÷ 10,200 = 1.8%. Near 1 m the share is lower; we judge that fast rise outruns defences in poor deltas and islands.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Under intermediate emissions (SSP2-4.5), close to current policies, the IPCC projects [56 cm, likely 44–76](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/climate_change/proj_global_sea_level.htm). "Likely" is the 17–83% range: 83% above 44 cm, 17% above 76 cm. A two-piece normal curve gives 68% above 50 cm: (56 − 44) ÷ 0.954 = 12.6 cm, and Φ(6 ÷ 12.6) = 0.68. These ranges leave out poorly understood ice-sheet collapse, which in a [low-likelihood, high-impact storyline](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter09.pdf) adds over a metre under high emissions: [nearly 2 m by 2100 cannot be ruled out](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf). The IPCC's low-confidence range for very high emissions tops out at [1.6 m](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/climate_change/proj_global_sea_level.htm).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It can make defences and relocation cheaper and better planned. It cannot stop the rise already committed by heat stored in the ocean and ice already melting.
