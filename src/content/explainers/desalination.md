## Why it matters here

Desalination helps through one channel: fresh water where rain and rivers fall short. It already supplies [more than 300 million people in over 150 countries](https://woa.un.org/third-world-ocean-assessment/socioecological-systems/sustainable-and-inclusive-ocean-economy/chapter-8-desalination) with drinking water. The larger prize is farming, which only cheap water can serve. Water carried far inland stays costly, so we put its ceiling at level 3.

## In numbers

- **Energy.** Large reverse-osmosis plants, which push seawater through a membrane that holds back salt, use [3–4 kWh per m³; physics sets a floor of about 1 kWh](https://aip.brightspotcdn.com/PTO.v68.i6.66_1.online.pdf). At the 2014 US industrial price of 7 ¢/kWh, that is 21–28 ¢/m³. At the floor and an assumed 3 ¢/kWh: 1 × 3 = 3 ¢/m³.
- **Cost.** About [$0.5/m³ at large plants and over $1.25 at small ones](https://woa.un.org/third-world-ocean-assessment/socioecological-systems/sustainable-and-inclusive-ocean-economy/chapter-8-desalination). Tariffs have fallen [from about $5 to under $0.5](https://guest.meed.com/veolia-ceo-says-water-tariffs-have-room-to-improve).
- **Farms.** In south-east Spain, farmers paid [€0.06/m³ for river water, €0.18 for groundwater and €0.60 for desalinated water](https://www2.ual.es/sea4crop/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Desalination-548-2023.pdf): 0.60 ÷ 0.06 = 10 times more. Watering outdoor vegetables with desalinated water alone cut profit by €2,433 per hectare.

## What each level looks like

- **$0.02–0.15/m³, level 3.** Our judgment: near what farmers pay for river or well water, so dry coasts could grow food with it. [Half a billion people face severe water scarcity all year, 4 billion for at least a month](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4758739/); we judge those near a coast exceed a tenth of humanity.
- **$0.15–2, level 2.** Derived: today's range supplies over 300 million, and 300 ÷ [8,200 million](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf) = 3.7% of humanity.
- **$2–5, level 1.** Our judgment: at or above what small plants charge today, only islands, ships and rich enclaves use it.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no published forecast of desalination costs for 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. Veolia's chief executive [expects tariffs to keep falling](https://guest.meed.com/veolia-ceo-says-water-tariffs-have-room-to-improve). The energy floor limits how far, and so does disposing of brine, the salty leftover, at [5–33% of total cost](https://woa.un.org/third-world-ocean-assessment/socioecological-systems/sustainable-and-inclusive-ocean-economy/chapter-8-desalination).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Within a few years it could design membranes that clog less and cheaper ways to handle brine. It cannot lower the floor of about 1 kWh per m³. Each plant, intake and pipeline still takes years to permit and build, and lifting water to inland cities and fields remains an energy bill.
