## Why it matters here

Degraded land [grows less food](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-report--1.7-billion-people-experience-lower-crop-yields-due-to-land-degradation/en), and soils grow [95% of what we eat](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/story/Polluting-our-soils-is-polluting-our-future/en). The harm falls first on farmers in degraded areas. So far the world has [grown more food anyway](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/better-land--soil-and-water-management-key-to-feeding-10-billion-people--fao-warns/en), so this card stops at level 2.

## In numbers

- **Today:** more than 60% of the [1.6 billion hectares degraded](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/expanding-agricultural-area-no-longer-viable-report) by human use lies on farmland, which covers 4.8 billion hectares. 0.6 × 1.6 ÷ 4.8 = 20% of farmland, a lower bound.
- **Harvests:** [1.7 billion people](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-report--1.7-billion-people-experience-lower-crop-yields-due-to-land-degradation/en) live where degradation has cut crop yields by 10%. Reversing a tenth of it on cropland would feed 154 million more people a year.
- **Absorbed so far:** world farm output [tripled in 60 years](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/better-land--soil-and-water-management-key-to-feeding-10-billion-people--fao-warns/en) while farmland grew 8%.
- **Slow to repair:** a few centimetres of topsoil take [about 1,000 years](https://www.unccd.int/resources/unccd-library/fao-warns-90-cent-earths-topsoil-risk-2050) to form.

## What each level looks like

- **5 to 20%, level 1.** Our judgment: less degradation than today, so less harm than today's.
- **20 to 40%, level 1.** Our judgment: as today, yields fall where 1.7 billion people live, but world output keeps rising. Fewer than 1 person in 100 lastingly loses enough food through soil alone.
- **40 to 70%, level 2.** Our judgment: twice today's share or more. If the people affected rise in step, 3.4 billion or more live with lower yields. IPBES expects degradation and climate change together to cut [world yields by 10% on average, and up to 50% in some regions](https://www.ipbes.net/news/media-release-worsening-worldwide-land-degradation-now-%E2%80%98critical%E2%80%99-undermining-well-being-32), by 2050. That would push over 1 in 100 people into hunger. We stop short of 1 in 10 because farms elsewhere can still intensify, as they have for 60 years.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing published projects degraded farmland to 2100, so the judges estimate it. Views differ widely: FAO warned in 2022 that [90% of topsoil could be at risk by 2050](https://www.unccd.int/resources/unccd-library/fao-warns-90-cent-earths-topsoil-risk-2050), far beyond the 20% measured on farmland now.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Better breeding, soil sensing and precision farming could cut erosion and raise yields on less land. Rebuilding soil still takes years of work, field by field, and depends on what farmers can afford.
