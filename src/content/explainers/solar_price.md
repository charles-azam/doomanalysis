## Why it matters here

This price decides how much of the parent card, solar and wind with storage, solar can win. It is the levelised cost: all a farm costs over its life, divided by the electricity it makes. It is a measure, not a level; its effect on people counts in the parent card.

## In numbers

- The world average for new solar farms fell from $417 per MWh in 2010 to $43 in 2024, in 2024 dollars: a 90% fall ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf)). In 2025 it was $44, and $35 in India ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/solar-pv-lcoe-ticks-marginally-upwards-2025-irena/)).
- A 2025 farm cost $667 per kW to build (same source). At the 17% capacity factor, average output as a share of peak, of 2024 ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf)), a kW makes 0.17 × 8,760 ≈ 1,490 kWh a year, or 37,000 kWh if it runs 25 years. Building alone is then $667 / 37,000 ≈ $18 per MWh, under half the $44. The rest is upkeep and the cost of borrowing.
- Over 90% of new large renewable projects in 2025 beat the cheapest new fossil plant, and new gas plants in Italy, Germany and Japan neared $100 per MWh ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/solar-pv-lcoe-ticks-marginally-upwards-2025-irena/)).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody forecasts this cost to 2100. In 2023 DNV expected the learning rate, the cost cut for each doubling of panels built, to slow from 26% to 17% by 2050, giving $21 per MWh ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/dnv-solar-pvs-lcoe-to-halve-by-2050)). In 2025 DNV said yearly falls, 8% a year over the past decade, would shrink to under 1% a year in the 2050s ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/10/21/nearly-half-of-solar-capacity-will-be-co-located-with-storage-by-2060-says-dnv/)). The fall has paused: PV Tech reports $43 rising to $44, [RenewEconomy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/renewables-remain-cheapest-new-power-option-and-avoided-us480bn-in-fossil-fuel-costs-in-2025/) a flat $44.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. It could quickly find better cell materials and leaner factories, and cut the build cost. But building is already under half the total. The cost of borrowing is set by lenders and country risk, not by intelligence: IRENA's assumed rates ran from 3.8% in Europe to 12% in Africa in 2024 ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/Agency/Publication/2025/Jul/IRENA_TEC_RPGC_in_2024_Summary_2025.pdf)).
