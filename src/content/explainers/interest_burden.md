## What it is

The interest a government pays on its debt, as a share of the money it collects in taxes and other revenue. The card uses the [World Bank series](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/GC.XPN.INTP.RV.ZS), which covers central government only. That is why some figures differ from national ones that cover all levels of government.

## Why it matters

Interest has to be paid before anything else. When it rises, governments must raise taxes, borrow more or cut other spending. Interest is also hard to cut quickly, because it depends on past borrowing and on market rates. [UNCTAD](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/osginf2026d11_en.pdf) counts 3.7 billion people living in countries that spend more on interest than on health or education. For the abundance test, this is the most direct way debt competes with the essentials.

## Where it stands

- **World:** 6.54% of revenue in 2024 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/GC.XPN.INTP.RV.ZS)). Interest rose from 2% to nearly 3% of world GDP in four years ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/execsum.pdf)). In developing countries, the median share of revenue spent on interest was 8.1% in 2025, nearly double the 2010 level ([UNCTAD](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/osginf2026d11_en.pdf)).
- **France:** 4.23% in 2024 (World Bank). Across all public bodies, interest was €65.7 billion (2.2% of GDP) in 2025 and is forecast at €77.4 billion in 2026 ([Cour des comptes](https://www.vie-publique.fr/files/rapport/pdf/303867.pdf)). The Cour expects more than €100 billion by 2029 ([February 2026](https://www.vie-publique.fr/files/rapport/pdf/302139.pdf)). The [Treasury](https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/Articles/2026/10/06/le-rapport-sur-la-dette-des-administrations-publiques-2027-est-publie) sees 3.7% of GDP in 2030. On IMF projections, that is roughly 7% of revenue in 2031 (our calculation from the [IMF Fiscal Monitor data](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/GGR_G01_GDP_PT@FM)).
- **United States:** 20.29% of federal revenue in 2024 (World Bank). CBO projects net interest rising from 19% of revenue in 2026 to 37% in 2056 ([House Budget Committee](https://budget.house.gov/press-release/03/03/2026/chairman-arrington-statement-on-cbo-long-term-budget-outlook)).
- **High burdens today:** Brazil 30.09% (2024) and India 33.98% (2022), on World Bank data.

## The lines that matter

- **10% of revenue:** UNCTAD uses this line to flag acute fiscal pressure. In 2025, 57 developing countries were above it, up from 29 in 2010.
- **14%, 18% and 23%:** IMF and World Bank debt-distress thresholds for low-income countries, set by how much debt each country can carry ([LIC-DSF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/about/partners/cso/2026-lic-dsf-review-presentation-for-public-consultation.pdf)). They measure all external debt service, principal included, so they are a rough guide for interest alone.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Faster growth raises revenue, which lowers the ratio even if the interest bill stays the same. Cheaper public services would also cut the need to borrow. Superintelligence does not set interest rates. Markets and central banks do. Debt issued today at higher rates will cost more for years ([Cour des comptes](https://www.vie-publique.fr/files/rapport/pdf/303867.pdf)). Strong growth driven by new investment could even push rates up.
