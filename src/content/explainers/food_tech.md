## Why it matters here

Food technology helps by making food cheaper and freeing land. In 2025, [645 million people faced hunger and 2.69 billion could not afford a healthy diet](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167987). The card tracks cereal yields, recorded for every country since 1961, because better crops act through them. Fermented proteins (made by microbes) and cultivated meat (grown from animal cells) would instead free land used for animals, which this number does not show.

## In numbers

- **Trend.** World cereal yields rose from [1.42 t/ha in 1961 to 4.23 in 2023](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/AG.YLD.CREL.KG): (4.23 − 1.42) ÷ 62 = 0.045 t/ha a year. A straight line gives 4.23 + 0.045 × 77 = 7.7 t/ha in 2100.
- **Keeping pace.** Population goes from [8.2 billion in 2024 to 10.2 billion in 2100](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf). On today's grain land, yields must reach 4.23 × 10.2 ÷ 8.2 = 5.3 t/ha just to hold grain per person.
- **Warming.** Without adaptation, CO₂ fertilisation or better varieties, each 1 °C of warming cuts global yields of [maize by 7.4%, wheat by 6.0% and rice by 3.2%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28811375/).
- **Animals.** [About 80% of farmland goes to meat and dairy; an all-plant diet would shrink farmland from 4.1 to 1 billion hectares](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets). New proteins could free part of it.

## What each level looks like

- **1–5.3 t/ha, level 1.** Our judgment: yields fall behind population; the technology adds little.
- **5.3–8, level 2.** Our judgment: harvests keep pace with 10 billion people and some warming, sparing hundreds of millions the hunger a standstill would bring.
- **8–16, level 3.** Our judgment: about twice today's yield makes staples and animal feed much cheaper, bringing a healthy diet within reach of many of the 2.69 billion, over a tenth of humanity. Food is one essential of five, so we stop at level 3.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no published forecast of world cereal yields for 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. Warming pulls the 7.7 t/ha trend down; gene editing could push it up.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. Within a few years it could design crop varieties, soil microbes and fermentation strains. Each new crop still needs several growing seasons of field trials, seed multiplication and approval, and farmers adopt over years. Cheap fermented protein needs factories built at the scale of the food industry.
