## Why it matters here

Each armed conflict is a place where food, water, power, health care and safety are being destroyed, and a chance for fighting to spread. The count shows how much of the world is fighting, not how deadly the fighting is: 65 small conflicts can kill fewer people than one large war. So this card has no danger levels; the damage is read on the top card.

## In numbers

- The Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) counts a conflict when fighting between two sides, at least one of them a government, [kills 25 people or more in a year](https://www.uu.se/en/department/peace-and-conflict-research/research/ucdp/ucdp-definitions). It calls it a war at 1,000 deaths or more in a year.
- It counted [65 in 2025](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2026/2026-06-09-ucdp-record-number-of-conflicts-between-states), the most since 1946. Of these, 13 were wars, the most since 1992, and 8 were between states, up from 2 in 2023.
- The count was [31 in 2010](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/number-of-state-based-conflicts). 65 / 31 = 2.1: it has doubled in 15 years. Of the 57 civil conflicts in 2025, 22 had foreign troops fighting on one side or both.

## The lines on the card

- **31, the 2010 low.** No year since 1975 has had fewer.
- **53, in 1991.** The peak at the end of the Cold War. The count then fell, with bumps, to the 2010 low.
- **65, the 2025 record.** A 2100 count above this line means a world more often at war than today.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes a forecast of this count for 2100. The closest is [Hegre et al. 2016](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/11/5/054002), who project the share of countries in civil war to 2100 under five standard scenarios of world development. Conflict falls as education and incomes rise. With slow growth, fast population growth and little schooling, the end-of-century rate is twice today's, and four times that of the optimistic "conventional development" scenario. The study predates the recent rise and leaves out wars between states.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Faster growth and better early warning can lower the risk of civil war. Cheap autonomous weapons lower the cost of starting one. Disputes over land, power and identity are political, and intelligence alone does not settle them.
