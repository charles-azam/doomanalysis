## What it is

The number of armed conflicts in a year where at least one side is a government. It is counted by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP). A conflict enters the count once it causes at least 25 battle deaths in a calendar year. A conflict with 1,000 or more battle deaths in a year is called a war ([UCDP definitions](https://www.uu.se/en/department/peace-and-conflict-research/research/ucdp/ucdp-definitions)).

## Why it matters

War is the fastest way to lose all five essentials at once. Power plants, water systems, farms and hospitals are destroyed or cut off, and people flee. Conflict also stops development: places at war stay poor, and poor places are more likely to fight. At the global scale, every war between armed states is a chance for escalation, including to nuclear use.

## Where it stands

UCDP counted 65 state-based conflicts in 2025, the most since its records begin in 1946 ([UCDP 2026](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2026/2026-06-09-ucdp-record-number-of-conflicts-between-states)). The count was 59 in 2023 and 61 in 2024, and it has almost doubled since 2010 ([UCDP 2025](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2025/2025-06-11-ucdp-sharp-increase-in-conflicts-and-wars)). Conflicts between two states rose to 8, up from 2 in 2023. There were 13 wars, the most since 1992. Organized violence killed about 244,600 people in 2025. The Russia–Ukraine war accounted for about 62% of battle deaths.

Wars are also harder to end. The [Global Peace Index 2026](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Global-Peace-Index-2026-Report.pdf) finds that the share of state-based conflicts ending in a peace agreement fell from 23% in the 1970s to about 4% in the 2010s. The number of countries involved in an external conflict rose from 59 in 2008 to 103.

No one publishes a count forecast for 2100. The closest is [Hegre et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/11/5/054002), which projects the share of countries in civil conflict to 2100 under the five standard socioeconomic scenarios (SSPs). Conflict declines in the sustainability, middle-of-the-road and fossil-fueled growth scenarios. It rises in the "fragmentation" and "inequality" scenarios, where education stalls and population grows fast. By 2100, conflict in the fossil-fueled growth scenario is only a quarter of the fragmentation level. The model predates the post-2020 surge and does not cover wars between states.

## The lines that matter

No agency defines a danger threshold for this count. The site uses two judgment lines.
- **65 conflicts (the 2025 record).** Going above it means the world is still getting more violent, not stabilizing.
- **80 conflicts.** About a quarter above the record. At this level, peacekeeping, aid and diplomacy would be stretched across more crises than they can handle. More wars between states would mean more chances of a war between nuclear powers.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could make economies grow faster and early-warning systems sharper. Hegre's work suggests that broad development, especially education in poor countries, is what lowers conflict risk. SI could also make cheap, precise weapons such as drones even cheaper, which lowers the cost of starting a fight. Wars are political choices about land, power and identity. SI does not remove those motives, and it may raise the stakes of controlling SI itself.
