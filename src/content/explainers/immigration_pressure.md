## Why it matters here

This card is a measure, not a hazard, so it has no danger band. It is here to test a common worry: that immigration weakens social cohesion and leads to conflict. Any such harm would pass through the civil-war card above it.

## In numbers

- **Stock.** About 170 million people in OECD countries were born abroad in 2025: 12% of the population, up from 9.2% in 2015 ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/international-migration-outlook-2026_a9eab36b-en/full-report/recent-developments-in-international-migration-movements-and-labour-market-inclusion-of-immigrants_a8f69192.html)).
- **Flow.** 6.1 million new permanent immigrants in 2025, down 1.6% ([OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/11/migration-to-oecd-countries-declines-for-second-consecutive-year-but-remains-high-by-historical-standards.html)). With 170 ÷ 0.12 = 1.4 billion people in the OECD, that is 0.4% a year.
- **Work.** Almost 71% of immigrants have a job. Their employment rate is 0.7 points below the native-born, and equal or higher in 19 countries. Their unemployment rate is 2.7 points higher. Graduates face a wider gap: 81.4% employed against 88.3% ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/international-migration-outlook-2026_a9eab36b-en/full-report/recent-developments-in-international-migration-movements-and-labour-market-inclusion-of-immigrants_a8f69192.html)).

## What research finds

- **Trust.** Across 87 studies, more ethnic diversity goes with lower social trust, most clearly trust in neighbours ([Dinesen et al. 2020](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/e7179562-9ed9-469c-8fb4-caefb5a536e2)).
- **Votes.** Pooling 20 causal studies, local immigration barely moves far-right voting on average once selective reporting is corrected, though effects are large in some places ([Cools et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12613)).
- **Terrorism.** In 145 countries from 1970 to 2000, larger migrant inflows went with fewer attacks, while migrants from terror-prone states helped it spread ([Bove and Böhmelt 2016](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/77011)).
- **Refugees and war.** Refugees from neighbouring countries raised the risk of conflict in the host in one study, though most never fight ([Salehyan and Gleditsch 2006](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/refugees-and-the-spread-of-civil-war/661D0F75EBC76E48585151BEBF858436)). A study of provinces worldwide, 1990–2018, found no such effect, and some sign of less conflict where hosting brought development ([Zhou and Shaver 2021](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/10c09447-6515-4a98-bfc7-b11b400c1857)).
- **Not established.** We found no study tying immigration into a rich country to civil war. Diversity does not predict civil war once income is accounted for ([Fearon and Laitin 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/ethnicity-insurgency-and-civil-war/B1D5D0E7C782483C5D7E102A61AD6605)). These studies cover the shares seen so far, not much higher ones.

## The lines on the card

- **9.2%:** the OECD in 2015.
- **15%:** half of OECD countries are above it.
- **33%:** Switzerland and Australia today ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/international-migration-outlook-2026_a9eab36b-en/full-report/recent-developments-in-international-migration-movements-and-labour-market-inclusion-of-immigrants_a8f69192.html)).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source publishes odds, so the curve is judged. As arithmetic, not a forecast: the 2015–2025 pace of 0.28 points a year, kept for 75 years, gives 12 + 21 = 33%. Flows follow policy: humanitarian migration fell 26% in 2025 ([OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/11/migration-to-oecd-countries-declines-for-second-consecutive-year-but-remains-high-by-historical-standards.html)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

If machines do much of the work, the labour-shortage argument for immigration weakens. Translation and job-matching tools could ease integration. How many people a country admits remains a political choice.
