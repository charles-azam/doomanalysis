## Why it matters here

Coal supplies 28% of world energy, 166 of 600 EJ in 2025, and China mines over half of it ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)). Scarcity is not the issue: reserves last more than a century. The danger is the CO₂, which the climate cards count. A 50% chance of holding warming to 1.5 °C means leaving about 90% of coal reserves in the ground ([UKERC](https://ukerc.ac.uk/news/unextractable-fossil-fuels-in-a-1-5c-world/)). This card tracks how much is still mined.

## In numbers

- **Reserves.** 1,166 billion tonnes in 2023 ([EIA via Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/coal-proved-reserves)) ÷ 9.05 billion tonnes mined in 2025 ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)) = 129 years.
- **Mostly burned at home.** Of 180.8 EJ mined, 35.3 EJ crossed between regions: 35.3 ÷ 180.8 = 20%. Indonesia (10.5 EJ) and Australia (8.9 EJ) shipped (10.5 + 8.9) ÷ 35.3 = 55% of that trade ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)).
- **Exposed importers.** Japan imports nearly all its coal, 4.24 of 4.36 EJ. Europe imports 41% of what it burns, China 11% ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)). Our judgment: a cut-off raises power prices but rarely leaves a country without coal for long, since many suppliers remain.

## The lines on the card

- **0.4 billion tonnes:** pathways limiting warming to 1.5 °C cut coal use by a median 95% from 2019 to 2050 ([IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf)): 8.05 billion tonnes mined in 2019 ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)) × 0.05 = 0.4.
- **1.2 billion tonnes:** 2 °C pathways cut it by a median 85%: 8.05 × 0.15 = 1.2.
- **4.7 billion tonnes:** China's output alone in 2025 ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source publishes odds for 2100. Of the five baseline scenarios without new climate policy that the IPCC draws on, two (SSP3 and SSP5) rely more and more on coal ([Riahi et al.](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/13280/1/The%20shared%20socioeconomic%20pathways%20and%20their%20energy,%20land%20use,%20and%20greenhouse%20gas%20emissions%20implications.pdf)). Meanwhile US coal use rose 10% in 2025 when gas prices jumped by more than half ([Energy Institute](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/au/pdf/2026/statistical-review-of-world-energy.pdf)): coal can return when other fuels get dear.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheaper clean power could retire coal plants faster, mostly in Asia. It does not change how much coal lies underground, or the CO₂ already emitted.
