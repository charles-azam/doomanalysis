## Why it matters here

A living standard costs time. As rich countries grew richer, their workers also worked far fewer hours ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/annual-working-hours-per-worker)), and abundance would mean being able to choose free time. This card is the time side of the living standard.

## In numbers

- **Today.** People in work worked 40.9 hours a week on average in 2025 ([ILO](https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator/?id=HOW_2EMP_SEX_NB_A&ref_area=X01&sex=SEX_T&format=.csv)). Over 52 weeks, 40.9 × 52 = about 2,130 hours a year.
- **Rich and poor.** High-income countries work 34.7 hours a week (1,806 a year), lower-middle-income countries 43.9 (2,284). Germany is at 29.7 hours a week, India at 45.7 ([ILO](https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator/?id=HOW_2EMP_SEX_NB_A&sex=SEX_T&timefrom=2024&timeto=2025&format=.csv)).
- **150 years.** A full-time factory worker put in about 3,100 hours a year in the United States in 1870 and 3,300 in Germany; by 2023 the average worker put in 1,789 and 1,335 ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/annual-working-hours-per-worker)). In Germany that is 59% less.
- **Recent pace.** The world average fell from 42.6 hours a week in 2005 to 40.9 in 2025 ([ILO](https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator/?id=HOW_2EMP_SEX_NB_A&ref_area=X01&sex=SEX_T&format=.csv)): 1.7 hours a week less in 20 years, or 1.7 × 52 = about 90 hours a year.
- **Two sources, two numbers.** The ILO estimates and the series since 1950, drawn mainly from national accounts ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/annual-working-hours-per-worker)), measure differently. For Germany the first gives 29.7 × 52 = 1,542 hours a year and the second 1,335, so compare like with like.

## The lines on the card

- **780 hours:** the 15-hour week Keynes imagined in 1930 for his grandchildren, 15 × 52 ([Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/?p=448841)).
- **1,542 hours:** Germany today, on the ILO basis.
- **1,806 hours:** the rich world today, on the ILO basis.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no projection of working hours to 2100. The curve is judged. On the recent world pace of about 4.5 hours a year less, 2100 would be near 1,800 hours, the rich-world level today.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Automation could shrink the work a given living standard needs to a fraction. Whether that becomes free time or unemployment depends on who owns the machines and how gains are shared.
