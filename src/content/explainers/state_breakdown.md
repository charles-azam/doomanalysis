## Why it matters here

A civil war cuts people off from food, water, power and care, and drives them from home. Conflict causes more of the deaths, displacement and hunger in today's crises than anything else ([OCHA](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point)). Since 1989 it has not gone beyond level 2. China's civil wars of the 1850s and 1940s reached level 3 on this card.

## In numbers

- **The counting rule.** A country counts in a year when fighting between its government and rebels kills over 10 in 100,000 inhabitants. In 2025: Sudan, Burkina Faso, Somalia, Haiti and Palestine, 113 of 8,232 million people, or 1.4% ([UCDP via Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/conflict-data), [population](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections)). The peak since 1989 is 3.1%, in 2020. Wars between states sit on the war cards. The count is a floor: DR Congo and Syria fall under the line yet have 34 million people in need ([OCHA](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/west-and-central-africa-4), [UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/global-trends)).
- **The record.** The Taiping war (1850–1864) killed at least 20 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion)). In 1853 China held 401 of 1,283 million people: 31%. In 1949, at the end of its later civil war, 538 of 2,468 million: 22% ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population)).
- **From countries to people.** In the five countries of 2025, 53 of 113 million people need aid: 47% (OCHA: [Sudan, Somalia](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/southern-and-eastern-africa-0), [Burkina Faso](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/west-and-central-africa-4), [Palestine](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/middle-east-and-north-africa-4), [Haiti](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/latin-america-and-caribbean-4)).

## What each level looks like

- **Under 1%, level 1.** In 2001–2005 the share stayed under 1% and about 0.6% of humanity was displaced ([UNHCR data](https://www.unhcr.org/refugee-statistics)). Derived.
- **1 to 5%, level 2.** Today. The 47% ratio alone gives 1.4 × 0.47 = 0.7%, but harm spreads beyond the counted countries: 118 million displaced, 1.4% of humanity ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/mid-year-trends)), and 239 million in need, 2.9% ([OCHA](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point)). Derived.
- **5 to 20%, level 2.** 5 × 0.47 = 2.4% to 20 × 0.47 = 9% harmed. Derived.
- **20 to 80%, level 3.** 20 × 0.47 = 9% to 80 × 0.47 = 38%. India will hold 15% of humanity in 2100, Africa 38% ([UN](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections)). Derived.
- **Over 80%, level 4.** Our judgment: in Sudan 33.7 of 51.7 million need aid, 65%, and 80 × 0.65 = 52%.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published for this measure, so the curve is judged. Forecasts count countries, not people. [Hegre and colleagues (2013)](https://prio.org/publications/5152) expected the share of countries with internal conflict to fall from 15% in 2009 to 7% in 2050. [Their 2016 study](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/11/5/054002) finds that by 2100 the rate doubles if the world fragments and halves under fast growth.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Civil wars break out mostly in poor countries with weak states ([Fearon and Laitin 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/ethnicity-insurgency-and-civil-war/B1D5D0E7C782483C5D7E102A61AD6605)). Superintelligence could raise incomes and state capacity quickly. It could also arm rebels and rulers with cheap autonomous weapons, and it does not settle who governs.
