## Why it matters here

Room to sleep, cook and study is part of decent living: a study of the minimum needed for a decent life for everyone allows 15 m² per person ([Millward-Hopkins et al.](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/16764/1/1-s2.0-S0959378020307512-main.pdf)). Space is also a large, slow-moving demand on energy and materials: every square metre is built once and then heated, cooled and lit for decades.

## In numbers

- **The world.** Buildings covered 273 billion m² of floor in 2024, and one of the report's authors says more than three quarters of it is housing ([UNEP and GlobalABC via Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/greenline/climate-change/global-construction-boom-outpacing-climate-gains-in-building-sector-report-warns/1830385)). 273 × 0.75 ÷ 8.16 billion people ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_PopTot.xlsx)) = at least 25 m² per person.
- **Countries that measure it.** Germany had 49.5 m² per person in 2025, up 3.3 m² since 2015 ([Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/07/PD26_250_31231.html)). China's statistics office reported 41.7 m² in 2020, up from 31.1 in 2010 ([Yicai](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinese-have-more-elbow-room-at-home-than-a-decade-ago-nbs-data-show)). In government data from thirteen countries, the range ran from 8.7 m² in India (2008) to 84 m² for new homes in Australia (2009) ([Ellsworth-Krebs](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/140062/1/Ellsworth_Krebs_Manuscript2019.pdf)).
- **Growth.** Floor area grew 1.7% in 2024 ([Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/greenline/climate-change/global-construction-boom-outpacing-climate-gains-in-building-sector-report-warns/1830385)), about twice as fast as population, which went from 8.16 to 8.23 billion between 2024 and 2025, 0.9% ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_PopTot.xlsx)). Bigger homes for smaller households are one reason ([Ellsworth-Krebs](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/140062/1/Ellsworth_Krebs_Manuscript2019.pdf)).
- **Caution.** Countries measure differently: some count external walls, some only usable space, some include garages ([Ellsworth-Krebs](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/140062/1/Ellsworth_Krebs_Manuscript2019.pdf)). Germany's figure divides all available living space by the population, so it is not the space people actually occupy ([Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/07/PD26_250_31231.html)). Compare a country with itself over time rather than countries with each other.

## The lines on the card

- **15 m²:** the decent-living minimum.
- **41.7 m²:** China, 2020.
- **49.5 m²:** Germany, 2025.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

We found no world projection of home space per person to 2100. The curve is judged.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheaper design, materials and building robots could make homes far less costly. Urban land, planning rules and the cement, steel and energy in each square metre do not change with intelligence.
