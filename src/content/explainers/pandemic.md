## What it is

The share of humanity killed by the worst pandemic of the period, counted as excess deaths within five years. Excess deaths include people killed by the disease and people who died because health systems were overwhelmed. This matches the way the [XPT forecasting tournament](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf) defined pandemic disasters: deaths within a five-year window, as a share of people alive at its start.

## Why it matters

A pandemic hits every country at once, so no one is free to send help. A severe one would empty clinics, close borders, disrupt food supply chains and stop work. The health essential would fail first, and the others could follow. Unlike most risks on this site, a pandemic can come from nature or from a lab, by accident or on purpose.

## Where it stands

COVID-19 caused about 14.9 million excess deaths in 2020–2021, with a range of 13.3–16.6 million ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2022-14.9-million-excess-deaths-were-associated-with-the-COVID-19-pandemic-in-2020-and-2021)). That is about 0.19% of the 7.9 billion people alive in 2021 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL)).

Big pandemics are less rare than intuition suggests. [Marani et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105482118) studied epidemics since 1600. They found that an epidemic as deadly per year as COVID-19 recurs on average every 59 years, or roughly 1.7% a year. An epidemic as intense as the 1918 flu, which killed about 32 million people in their data, recurs about every 400 years. New diseases are emerging from animals faster. Marani's team estimates this could make extreme epidemics up to three times more likely in the coming decades.

In the XPT (2022), forecasters gave these odds of a pathogen killing more than 1% of humanity in five years at some point before 2100:
- natural pathogen: 3.63% (superforecasters) and 8.14% (experts);
- engineered pathogen: 4% (superforecasters) and 10.25% (experts).

For more than 10% of humanity, the medians were 1% (natural) and 0.8% (engineered) among superforecasters. Experts gave 0.85% and 3%. The XPT authors note that history, with three plausible cases of a pathogen killing over 1% of humanity, implies 11–23% by 2100.

## The lines that matter

- **1% of humanity (about 80 million deaths today).** This is the XPT threshold, about five times COVID's toll. The XPT authors count only three plausible past cases: smallpox, the Black Death and the 1918 flu. Health systems in most countries would be overwhelmed.
- **10% of humanity.** This is the XPT definition of a global catastrophe. A loss on this scale, within a few years, could push many countries into collapse as defined on this site.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could design vaccines and drugs in days, spot outbreaks early and model how they spread. That cuts the time from first case to protection, which matters most. It could also lower the skill needed to build a dangerous pathogen. XPT experts, polled in 2022, already rated engineered pathogens as riskier than natural ones. Making, testing and delivering billions of doses, and getting people to accept them, remain physical and social problems.
