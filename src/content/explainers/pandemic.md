## Why it matters here

A pandemic kills in every country at once, so no one is free to send help. The share of humanity killed reads directly on the danger scale: the 1918 flu reached level 2, the Black Death level 3.

## In numbers

- **1918 flu:** about 50 million dead among 1.8 billion people: 2.7%, with estimates from 1% to 5.4% ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/spanish-flu-largest-influenza-pandemic-in-history)).
- **COVID-19:** [27 million excess deaths](https://ourworldindata.org/historical-pandemics) among [8.1 billion people](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population): 0.3%.
- **Black Death:** about [50 million dead in Europe](https://ourworldindata.org/historical-pandemics), over half its people and 11% of the [456 million alive in 1300](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population).
- **How often:** a COVID-sized pandemic comes in about [2% of years](https://globalhealth.duke.edu/news/statistics-say-large-pandemics-are-more-likely-we-thought), one as intense as the 1918 flu every [400 years](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8536331/): 1 − e^(−74/400) = 17% by 2100. More diseases jumping from animals could make that up to three times likelier.

## What each level looks like

- **0.01 to 1%, level 1 (derived).** COVID-19's scale: hospitals overflow, societies hold.
- **1 to 10%, level 2 (derived).** The 1918 flu's scale: 0.1 to 1 billion dead among 2100's [10.2 billion people](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population-with-un-projections).
- **10 to 50%, level 3 (derived).** The Black Death's scale; Europe [took until the 16th century to recover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death).
- **50 to 99%, level 4.** Our judgment: even at 90% dead, as in [the Americas after 1492](https://ourworldindata.org/historical-pandemics), a billion would remain, [as in 1800](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population).
- **Above 99%, level 5.** Our judgment: about 100 million survivors, as in [1000 BC](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population), too scattered to keep industry running.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The curve uses the superforecasters of the [XPT tournament](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf) of 2022. They gave 3.63% for a natural and 4% for an engineered pathogen killing over 1% of humanity by 2100: 1 − 0.9637 × 0.96 = 7.5% for either. For over 10%, 1% and 0.8% give 1.8%; for extinction, [0.012%](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/9ddheDChiMhveCfSw/what-do-xpt-results-tell-us-about-biorisk). Its biorisk experts gave 18% for over 1%, closer to history: three pathogens arguably killed over 1% within five years, [implying 11–23%](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf) by 2100. [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1493/) puts a 10% fall in population at 45%, from biotechnology [25%](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1502/) or nature [9%](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/8745/) of the time: 15%. A Lancet commission gives [23% within a decade](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/halving-premature-deaths-2050-within-reach-says-Lancet-study) for a pandemic killing 25 million, about 0.25% of humanity: 1 − 0.77^7.4 = 86% by 2100.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It could design vaccines in days and spot outbreaks early, but also help someone build a pathogen. Making billions of doses and persuading people to take them stays physical and social work.
