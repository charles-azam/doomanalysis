## What it is

This is the share of residents born in another country, refugees included ([World Bank/UN](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SM.POP.TOTL.ZS)). It counts birthplace, not citizenship, so children born locally to immigrants are not in it. On this site it is context only and is not scored, because the evidence does not show migration to be good or bad in itself.

## Why it matters

Immigrants to ageing countries are mostly of working age. Whether that helps depends on whether they find work and integrate, which differs by reason for moving.

## Where it stands

There were 304 million migrants in 2024, 3.7% of the world ([Destatis/UN DESA](https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Countries-Regions/International-Statistics/Data-Topic/Population-Labour-Social-Issues/DemographyMigration/Migrants.html)). Sweden is at 21.4%, Norway at 18.2% and France at 13.8%, up from 14.6%, 10.7% and 11.5% in 2010 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SM.POP.TOTL.ZS)). The UN expects immigration to be the main driver of population growth in 52 countries through 2054. It adds that immigration can ease labour shortages for a while but cannot offset ageing ([UN](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)). No official projection of migrant shares to 2100 exists.

- **Work.** Among people aged 20–64, 73.7% of the foreign-born in Sweden had jobs, against 85.0% of natives. The figures were 73.0% and 82.3% in Norway, and 65.0% and 76.8% in France ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/lfsa_ergacob?geo=SE&geo=NO&geo=FR&geo=EU27_2020&age=Y20-64&sex=T&c_birth=NAT&c_birth=FOR&c_birth=NEU27_2020_FOR&unit=PC&time=2024), 2024).
- **Public finances.** In Sweden, refugees were a net fiscal cost in every year studied. Other immigrants pay in more than they take over a lifetime ([Konjunkturinstitutet](https://ucmediastoragewelive.blob.core.windows.net/758b64c0-bc83-4dab-adbd-aa4d8b065d36/media/kpgnt5iw/specialstudie-117-invandrades-nettobidrag-till-de-offentliga-finanserna-1983-2022.pdf)). In Norway, an immigrant arriving at 25 from Western countries adds about NOK 10.7 million to public finances over a lifetime; one from Africa or Asia costs about 1.3 million ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/innvandring-og-innvandrere/beregninger-fra-ssb-av-virkninger-av-okt-innvandring-pa-offentlige-finanser)).
- **Crime.** People born abroad were 2.5 times as likely as natives with native parents to be registered crime suspects in Sweden. After adjusting for age, sex, income, education and area, they were 1.8 times as likely ([Brå](https://bra.se/download/18.45e4b8e192705389a34c2b/1729515966490/2021_9_Registered_offending_among_persons.pdf)). In Norway, after adjusting for age and sex, immigrants were charged 21% more often than others in 2015, down from 62% in 2002 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/sosiale-forhold-og-kriminalitet/artikler-og-publikasjoner/innvandrere-mindre-overrepresentert-blant-siktede-enn-for)). Sweden's gun homicide rate, about 4 per million against a European 1.6, is tied to criminal networks ([Brå](https://bra.se/download/18.47bb43da192711ebb376b48/1732000833289/2021_8_Gun_homicide_in_Sweden_and_other_European_countries.pdf)). Elsewhere, a review of 51 mostly US studies finds a very weak negative link ([Ousey and Kubrin](https://news.uci.edu/2017/06/27/immigration-does-not-raise-crime-uci-led-study-finds-refuting-common-assumption/)). In the UK, asylum seekers modestly raised property crime while EU workers slightly lowered it ([Bell et al.](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/59323)).
- **Cohesion.** A meta-analysis of 87 studies links diversity to somewhat lower trust, mainly in neighbours ([Dinesen et al.](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/e7179562-9ed9-469c-8fb4-caefb5a536e2)). Norway's immigrants trust other people less than average, but trust the police and politics as much or more ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/sosiale-forhold-og-kriminalitet/artikler-og-publikasjoner/darligere-levekar-men-likevel-hoy-tillit-og-sterk-tilhorighet)).

**Sweden and Norway** have similar shares but a different mix. Labour migrants were a third of Norway's non-Nordic arrivals since 1990 ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/en/befolkning/innvandrere/statistikk/innvandrere-etter-innvandringsgrunn)). In Sweden, refugees were 7% of the population in 2022, a high share by Western standards ([Konjunkturinstitutet](https://ucmediastoragewelive.blob.core.windows.net/758b64c0-bc83-4dab-adbd-aa4d8b065d36/media/kpgnt5iw/specialstudie-117-invandrades-nettobidrag-till-de-offentliga-finanserna-1983-2022.pdf)). Across the Nordic countries, crime over-representation is lowest for labour migrants and highest for refugees ([Brå 2025](https://www.konj.se/media/42ydkrrl/brottsforebyggande-radet-migration-och-brott-en-kunskapssammanstallning.pdf)). Norway faces its own cost: its oil-fund income is shared among more residents ([SSB 2017](https://www.ssb.no/nasjonalregnskap-og-konjunkturer/artikler-og-publikasjoner/betydningen-for-demografi-og-makrookonomi-av-innvandring-mot-2100)).

## The lines that matter

No agency defines a safe or dangerous share, so none is shown. The employment gap matters more.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

If superintelligence automates much work, the labour-shortage case for migration weakens. Budgets would then depend on how all income is taxed. It could speed language learning and job matching. It cannot settle how many people a country should admit.
