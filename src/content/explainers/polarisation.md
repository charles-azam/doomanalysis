## Why it matters here

We found no study showing that political polarisation causes civil war by itself. It matters through a chain. When political camps turn hostile, democracies often erode: of 52 episodes of severe polarisation in democracies since 1950 (3 or more on V-Dem's original 0–4 scale), 26 ended with the democracy downgraded ([McCoy and Press](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2022/01/what-happens-when-democracies-become-perniciously-polarized?lang=en)). Half-democratic regimes are then the most prone to civil war ([Hegre et al. 2001](https://www.prio.org/publications/3)).

## In numbers

- **The score.** V-Dem asks country experts how far society is split into hostile political camps. A score of zero marks roughly the all-time average across countries ([Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/political-polarization-score)). Averaged over the countries V-Dem classes as democracies ([regime data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/political-regime)), it rose from −0.83 in 2005 to −0.07 in 2025: +0.76 in twenty years.
- **The spread.** 19 of 85 democracies scored above 1 in 2025, against 5 of 93 in 2005. The United States went from −0.5 in 2000 to 2.3.
- **What predicts instability.** A model with four variables told apart, two years ahead and with over 80% accuracy, countries heading for civil war or a democratic reversal ([Goldstone et al. 2010](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-global-model-for-forecasting-political-instability/)). The variables are regime type, infant mortality, neighbours at war and state discrimination against minorities. Democracies where politics is split into hostile rival blocs ("factionalism") faced odds of instability over 30 times those of full autocracies ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=9749)).
- **Identity.** Ethnic groups rebel more often the more they are shut out of state power ([Cederman et al. 2010](https://library.au.int/node/258837)). Diversity itself does not raise the risk once income is taken into account. Poverty and weak states do ([Fearon and Laitin 2003](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/ethnicity-insurgency-and-civil-war/B1D5D0E7C782483C5D7E102A61AD6605)).

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source publishes odds, so the curve is judged. Severe polarisation is hard to undo: of 16 episodes where a democracy brought severe polarisation down, the fall lasted in only 9 ([McCoy and Press](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2022/01/what-happens-when-democracies-become-perniciously-polarized?lang=en)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could tailor persuasion to each voter and deepen hostile camps. It could also power tools that help opponents find common ground. The outcome depends on who deploys it and under what rules.
