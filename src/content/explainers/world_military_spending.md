## Why it matters here

Military spending measures preparation for war. A rising share means states expect to fight, or fear they will have to. It also takes money from food, water, energy and health. Spending kills nobody by itself, so this card has no danger levels. It is a gauge of how aggressive the world is becoming.

## In numbers

- The world spent [$2,887 billion](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2604_milex_2025.pdf) on its armed forces in 2025: 2.5% of world GDP, up from 2.4% in 2024 and the highest share since 2009. Spending has risen 11 years in a row, and by 41% over 2016–2025.
- Per person: $2,887 billion / 8.2 billion people = $352 a year.
- The United States spent $954 billion, [33% of the total](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/yb26_summary_en_1.pdf). European states spent 3.2% of their GDP on average, up from 2.8% a year earlier. [Ukraine spent 40% and Russia 7.5%](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2604_milex_2025.pdf).
- At the 1962 share of 6.1%, the world would spend 2,887 × 6.1 / 2.5 = about $7,000 billion a year.

## The lines on the card

- **2.1%, in 2018.** The lowest share in the [World Bank series](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/MS.MIL.XPND.GD.ZS?locations=1W), which starts in 1960 and uses SIPRI data.
- **3.5%, the NATO core target.** In June 2025, NATO members agreed to spend 5% of GDP by 2035: at least 3.5% on core military needs, the rest on wider security ([SIPRI](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2604_milex_2025.pdf)). The line shows the world share if every country spent at NATO's core target.
- **6.1%, in 1962.** The Cold War peak in the same series.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nobody publishes a forecast for 2100. The series shows how far the share can move in a lifetime: it fell by two-thirds from 1962 to 2018, from 6.1% to 2.1%, and has climbed back to 2.5% since. The nearest thing to a forecast is NATO's target for 2035.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

A race for AI-enabled weapons could push spending up fast. Faster economic growth would shrink its share of GDP. How much states arm depends on how much they fear each other, which technology does not settle.
