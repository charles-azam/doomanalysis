## What it is

Deliberate, unlawful killings per 100,000 people per year, compiled by the UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) from police and health records ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/VC.IHR.PSRC.P5)).

## Why it matters

Homicide measures whether people are safe in daily life. Over 2019–2021 it killed about 440,000 people a year, around five times as many as armed conflict ([UNODC 2023](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/gsh/2023/GSH23_ExSum.pdf)). UNODC links nearly 40% of homicides to crime, mainly organized crime and gangs.

## Where it stands

The world rate was 5.2 in 2023, down from 6.9 in 2000. Rates range from 0.23 in Japan to 19.3 in Brazil and 49.4 in Jamaica ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/VC.IHR.PSRC.P5)).

**The Nordic contrast.** Sweden's overall rate (1.15 in 2023) is not extreme, but its gun violence is. In Brå's 2021 study, Sweden had about 4 gun homicides per million people a year, against a European average of about 1.6 across 22 countries. Gun homicide in Sweden rose from 2005, while most others declined ([Brå 2021](https://bra.se/english/publications/archive/2021-05-26-gun-homicide-in-sweden-and-other-european-countries)). Police counted 281 shootings and 34 deaths in 2017, peaking at 391 shootings and 62 deaths in 2022, about 6 per million. In 2025 there were 158 shootings and 43 deaths, including the Örebro school shooting ([Swedish Police](https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/sprangningar-och-skjutningar/)). In early 2026, shootings fell to the lowest since records began in 2017 ([Police](https://polisen.se/aktuellt/nyheter/nationell/2026/juli/skjutningarna-pa-historiskt-laga-nivaer/)). Norway recorded 19 homicide victims in 2025, a record low, and 4 killed with guns, under 1 per million ([Kripos](https://www.politiet.no/globalassets/tall-og-fakta/drap/nasjonal-drapsoversikt-2025.pdf)).

**The debate over causes.**
- *Gang conflict and drug markets:* Brå finds about 8 in 10 gun homicides occur among criminal networks. It suspects shootings that trigger revenge shootings. Drug-market conflicts and disadvantaged areas exist across Europe, so why Sweden alone, Brå says, is unclear.
- *Segregation:* Police link about 60% of 2022–2024 shootings to 65 "vulnerable areas" ([Police](https://polisen.se/aktuellt/nyheter/nationell/2025/december/polisen-presenterar-ny-lagesbild-over-utsatta-omraden/)). One Stockholm study found that parents' resources and neighbourhood segregation explain half to three-quarters of the registered-crime gap between sons of immigrants and sons of native Swedes ([Hällsten et al.](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/crime-price-inequality-gap-registered-crime-between-childhood)).
- *Immigration:* Prime Minister Kristersson blamed "an irresponsible immigration policy and a failed integration" ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/sweden-leader-summons-army-gang-violence-rocks-nation-rcna118015)). A 2026 Brå review finds migrants over-represented in Nordic crime registers, but too little research to judge migration's net effect in Sweden ([Brå 2026](https://bra.se/english/publications/archive/2026-06-29-migration-and-crime)).
- *Policing:* Police credit the post-2022 fall to arrests and prosecutions of network members.

## The lines that matter

- **10 per 100,000, "epidemic" level.** The media often attribute this to the WHO. UNODC says it is widely used but of unclear origin, and questions the label ([UNODC 2019](https://www.unodc.org/documents/data-and-analysis/gsh/Booklet2.pdf)). Treat it as a convention, not an official WHO threshold.
- **20 per 100,000 (judgment).** In 2017, the 20 countries above this level had 9% of the world's people but 49% of its homicides.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could help solve more crimes, trace illegal guns and drug money, and aim prevention at the few people and places most at risk. It cannot by itself remove the drug profits, exclusion and weak institutions that sustain violence. The same tools could also enable mass surveillance, trading one threat to security for another.
