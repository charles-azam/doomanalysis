## What it is

The Global Peace Index (GPI) scores 163 countries and territories, covering 99.7% of the world's population ([GPI 2026](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Global-Peace-Index-2026-Report.pdf)). It is produced each year by the Institute for Economics & Peace, a think tank. Each of 23 indicators is scored from 1 to 5 and then weighted into one overall score. **Lower is better: 1 is the most peaceful.** The indicators fall into three groups:
- **Safety and security** at home: homicide, violent crime, perceived crime, political instability, terrorism, access to small arms.
- **Ongoing conflict**: deaths from internal and external wars, relations with neighbours.
- **Militarization**: military spending, weapons imports and exports, nuclear and heavy weapons.

Internal peace counts for 60% of the score and external peace for 40%.

## Why it matters

Security is the essential that protects the others. Where people fear violence, farms are abandoned, clinics close, and power and water networks are not repaired. Peace also costs money when it fails. The GPI puts the economic impact of violence in 2025 at US$21.8 trillion, about 10.5% of world GDP in purchasing-power terms. Military spending made up 43% of that.

## Where it stands

Scores in 2026 run from 1.161 to 3.367:
- **Most peaceful:** Iceland (1.161), first for the 19th year running, then New Zealand (1.343), Switzerland (1.363), Slovenia and Ireland.
- **Middle of the table:** Norway 1.688 (33rd), Sweden 1.732 (40th), France 2.083 (99th), China 2.231 (118th), India 2.409 (127th), United States 2.535 (134th).
- **Least peaceful:** Russia (3.367) is last, below Sudan (3.195), DR Congo, Ukraine and Israel.

The world is getting less peaceful. Average peacefulness fell 0.7% in the latest year, the 12th fall in a row. In that year, 99 countries got worse and 62 improved. Since 2008, 119 countries have become less peaceful. The 25 least peaceful countries worsened by 18.9% on average, while the 25 most peaceful worsened by just 0.3%. The gap is widening. The 2026 index only partly captures the 2026 Iran war, because many conflict indicators stop at the end of 2025.

No one forecasts GPI scores to 2100.

## The lines that matter

The GPI itself sets no official danger line. The site uses two judgment thresholds:
- **2.0 (abundance threshold).** In 2026, 83 of 163 countries, about half, score at or below 2.0. Above it, insecurity starts to limit access to the other essentials.
- **3.0 (danger, judgment).** The nine countries above 3.0 in 2026 are all at war or in deep internal conflict, or just emerging from one: Russia, Sudan, DR Congo, Ukraine, Israel, South Sudan, Afghanistan, Yemen and Syria. At this level, losing at least one essential for a majority of people is a live risk.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

SI could help police solve crimes, help governments spot unrest early and help mediators find deals. It could also give whoever controls it powerful tools for surveillance, repression and war. That makes security the pillar where SI could hurt as much as it helps. Peace depends on how power is shared and checked, which no technology settles on its own.
