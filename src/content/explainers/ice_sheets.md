## Why it matters here

Greenland holds [over 6 m of sea level and West Antarctica up to 4 m](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/warming-seas-and-melting-ice-sheets/). Past a tipping point they keep losing ice for centuries, even if warming stops. This card measures what this century's peak warming locks in; what arrives by 2100 is on the sea-level card. Because most of the water comes after 2100, it stays at level 1 except at its top end.

## In numbers

- **What peak warming locks in over 2,000 years:** [2–3 m at 1.5 °C, 2–6 m at 2 °C and 19–22 m at 5 °C](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf); [4–10 m at 3 °C and 12–16 m at 4 °C](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter09.pdf). At 2025's [1.43 °C](https://library.wmo.int/records/item/69807-state-of-the-global-climate-2025), about 2 m.
- **Tipping points.** Best estimates are [1.5 °C (0.8–3) for Greenland, unfolding over about 10,000 years, and 1.5 °C (1–3) for West Antarctica, over about 2,000](https://pik-potsdam.de/members/johanro/ArmstrongMcKayetal2022_ClimateTippingPointsReassessment_AcceptedversionwithFigures.pdf). Both [have been at risk since at least 1 °C](https://wwfes.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/gtp_summary_report_2025-v4-pages-lo-res--1-.pdf).
- **Pace and debt.** The ice sheets lose [about 370 billion tonnes a year, four times the early-1990s rate](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2025/may/glaciers-global-temperature-increase.html). Greenland's [4,890 billion t lost over 1992–2020 raised seas 13.5 mm](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter09.pdf), so 370 × 13.5 ÷ 4,890 ≈ 1 mm a year. Greenland alone [already owes at least 27 cm](https://www.oulu.fi/en/news/new-study-reveals-greenland-ice-sheet-already-committed-least-27-cm-global-sea-level-rise).

## What each level looks like

- **1–3 m, level 1.** Warming held near 1.5 °C; coasts have generations to adapt. Our judgment.
- **3–10 m, level 1.** West Antarctica, then Greenland, lost over centuries. Our judgment: almost all of the rise comes after 2100.
- **10–25 m, level 2.** Needs 3–4 °C of peak warming or more, where the warming card is already at level 3. Our judgment: ice loss speeds up before 2100, and the land of the [billion people living within 10 m of high tide](https://iwlearn.net/marine/documents/31242) is given up over the following centuries.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source gives odds for this metric. It follows peak warming through the IPCC ranges, so the judges read it off the warming card. Sources disagree on what is safe: the IPCC expects the ice sheets [not to fully disintegrate over several centuries at 1.5–2 °C](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter09.pdf), while [Stokes et al.](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2025/may/glaciers-global-temperature-increase.html) expect several metres over the coming centuries at 1.5 °C.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It can cut emissions faster and sharpen forecasts. Past a tipping point, only cooling the planet back, which takes decades of CO₂ removal, stops the loss.
