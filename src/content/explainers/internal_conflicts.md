## Why it matters here

This card counts how many civil conflicts are active, not how deadly they are. A conflict enters the count when a government and an armed group fighting over power or territory cause at least 25 battle deaths in a year ([UCDP](https://www.uu.se/en/department/peace-and-conflict-research/research/ucdp/ucdp-definitions)). Any of them can grow into the full civil wars that the parent card measures.

## In numbers

- **57 in 2025**, equal to the 2023 record: 35 fought without foreign troops and 22 "internationalised", where another state's troops fight for one side ([UCDP via Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/number-of-state-based-conflicts)).
- **Nearly double 2010.** 57 ÷ 31 = 1.8. Internationalised conflicts went from 8 of 31 (26%) to 22 of 57 (39%).
- **Most conflict involving a state is internal.** UCDP counted 65 such conflicts in 2025, 8 of them between states: 65 − 8 = 57, or 88% ([UCDP](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2026/2026-06-09-ucdp-record-number-of-conflicts-between-states)).
- **Few are large.** Thirteen conflicts, wars between states included, passed 1,000 battle deaths in 2025, UCDP's line for a war. That is the most since 1992 ([UCDP](https://www.uu.se/en/press/press-releases/2026/2026-06-09-ucdp-record-number-of-conflicts-between-states)).

## The lines on the card

- **20:** the count in 1970.
- **31:** the low of 2010, also reached in 2003, after a peak of 51 in 1991.
- **57:** the record, in 2023 and again in 2025.
- **80:** 40% above the record (57 × 1.4). No source names this value. It gives the curve a point for a world clearly worse than today.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source publishes odds for this count, so the curve is judged. [Hegre and colleagues (2013)](https://prio.org/publications/5152) used population, education and infant mortality to forecast that the share of countries with internal conflict would fall from 15% in 2009 to 7% in 2050. The count has instead risen, from 37 in 2009 to 57. [Their 2016 study](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/11/5/054002) runs to 2100. If the world fragments and schooling stalls (scenario SSP3), the conflict rate ends twice as high as today's. Under fast growth (SSP5) it ends at half today's.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

In these models, schooling and less poverty in poor countries cut conflict risk the most ([Hegre et al. 2016](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/11/5/054002)). Superintelligence could speed both. Cheaper weapons push the other way: OCHA counts about 40 times as many drone attacks in war zones in 2024 as in 2020 ([OCHA](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point)). Disputes over land, power and identity remain.
