## Why it matters here

Nothing can replace phosphorus in farming ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)). Mineral fertiliser supplied 47% of the phosphorus crops could draw from farm soils in 2017, and nearly 60% in Western Europe, North America and Asia ([Demay et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-022-01092-0)). So this is the one mineral card that touches food, and it can reach level 2.

## In numbers

- **Years of reserves:** 73,000 Mt ÷ 250 Mt mined in 2025 = about 290 years ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)), up from 16,000 ÷ 158 = about 100 years in 2009 ([USGS 2010](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/mineral-pubs/phosphate-rock/mcs-2010-phosp.pdf)). Reserves grew 4.6 times while mining grew 58%, so this is not a countdown.
- **Risk of being cut off:** China, Morocco and the United States mined 110 + 36 + 20 = 166 of 250 Mt (66%), China alone 44%. Morocco holds 50,000 of 73,000 Mt of reserves (68%) ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)).
- **Demand:** fertiliser used 47.8 Mt of phosphorus pentoxide (P₂O₅, the standard measure of phosphate content) in 2025, rising to 51.5 Mt by 2029, while capacity grows from 63.7 to 71.7 Mt ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)).
- **Recycling:** none recorded for the rock ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)). Soils hold phosphorus, so farmers can skip a dose where past doses were good ([IFA 2023](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2023_IFA_medium_term_outlook_public_summary.pdf)).

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 10%, level 0:** prices spike and harvests hold. Our judgment: in 2008 North African prices passed five times their 2007 level ([USGS 2009](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/mineral-pubs/phosphate-rock/mcs-2009-phosp.pdf)) and use fell from 38.8 to 36.0 Mt, or 7% ([IFA 2009](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2009_shanghai_ifa_summary.pdf)); after the 2021–22 spike it fell 9% over two years ([IFA 2023](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2023_IFA_medium_term_outlook_public_summary.pdf)). We found no lasting harm traced to either.
- **10 to 30%, level 1:** poor farmers buy less, and yields and incomes fall on poor soils. Our judgment: price rises hit the poorest first; African fertiliser use fell 14% over 2021–22 ([IFA 2023](https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2023_IFA_medium_term_outlook_public_summary.pdf)).
- **30 to 100%, level 2:** harvests shrink for years and up to hundreds of millions go hungry. Our judgment: a long cut would drain the fertiliser-built phosphorus that feeds the most productive farmland ([Demay et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-022-01092-0)). We stop below level 3 because no study we found shows harm beyond a tenth of humanity.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

Nothing is published to 2100, so the judges estimate the curve. The USGS sees no imminent shortage ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-phosphate.pdf)). The risk is policy: in 2022 Chinese export curbs cut its phosphate fertiliser exports by about 5 million tonnes ([USGS 2023](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2023/mcs2023-phosphate.pdf)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence could help farmers put phosphorus only where soils lack it, and improve recovery from waste. It cannot replace phosphorus or move the deposits.
