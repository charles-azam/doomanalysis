## Why it matters here

Reefs feed coastal people, break waves and bring in tourists. [More than 275 million people live within 30 km of a reef, and reefs protect 150,000 km of shoreline in over 100 countries and territories](https://www.wri.org/news/press-release-75-worlds-coral-reefs-currently-under-threat-new-analysis-finds). [About 6 million people make their living fishing them](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2021/september/over-half-of-coral-reef-cover-lost-since-1950.html), and reefs matter to the [wellbeing of up to a billion](https://wwfes.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/gtp_summary_report_2025-v4-pages-lo-res--1-.pdf). Even losing every reef stays at level 1, close to the edge of level 2.

## In numbers

- **Half gone already.** [Living coral cover fell by about 50% from 1957 to 2007, and reef biodiversity by 63%](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2021/september/over-half-of-coral-reef-cover-lost-since-1950.html).
- **The 2023–2025 heat** put [84% of reef area under bleaching-level stress, in at least 83 countries and territories](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/worlds-fourth-mass-coral-bleaching-event-likely-ended-2025); the deaths are not yet counted.
- **What 1.5 °C means.** The IPCC projects a [further 70–90% loss at 1.5 °C and near-total loss by 2 °C](https://pik-potsdam.de/members/johanro/ArmstrongMcKayetal2022_ClimateTippingPointsReassessment_AcceptedversionwithFigures.pdf). On cover already halved: 50 + 50 × 0.7 = 85% and 50 + 50 × 0.9 = 95%.
- **Tipping point.** The [central estimate of the reefs' heat threshold, 1.2 °C, has been passed](https://wwfes.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/gtp_summary_report_2025-v4-pages-lo-res--1-.pdf); 2025 was [1.43 °C](https://library.wmo.int/records/item/69807-state-of-the-global-climate-2025).

## What each level looks like

- **30–70%, level 1.** Damaged reefs still feed people and break waves. Our judgment.
- **70–90%, level 1.** Most reefs stop growing; reef fishing and tourism shrink. Our judgment.
- **90–100%, level 1.** Reefs all but gone. Our judgment: those who lose decent living conditions, reef fishers and the poorest islanders, number in the tens of millions, while most reef neighbours can turn to other food and work. One percent of the [10.2 billion people of 2100](https://www.dawn.com/news/1845492) is 102 million, so even total loss stays below level 2.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source gives probabilities for the loss in 2100, so the judges estimate the 70% and 90% edges. Two findings point high: reefs are [over 99% likely to tip even if warming is held at 1.5 °C without overshoot](https://wwfes.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/gtp_summary_report_2025-v4-pages-lo-res--1-.pdf), and the IPCC range above implies at least 85% lost at 1.5 °C. A chance of tipping is not a chance of a given loss by 2100, so we do not turn it into a curve point.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Breeding heat-tolerant corals and restoring reefs could save some sites. Functional reefs at scale need warming brought back [below 1.2 °C and eventually 1 °C](https://wwfes.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/gtp_summary_report_2025-v4-pages-lo-res--1-.pdf), which takes decades of CO₂ removal.
