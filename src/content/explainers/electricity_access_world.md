## Why it matters here

Electricity is the energy essential, and the others lean on it: it pumps and treats water, keeps vaccines and food cold, and lights clinics and classrooms. The measure is yes or no, mostly from household surveys, so it says nothing about whether the power is enough, stays on or is affordable ([SDG metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-07-01-01.pdf)).

## In numbers

- **Today.** 91.9% of humanity had electricity in 2024, up from 78.2% in 2000 and 83.5% in 2010 ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)). That leaves 655 million people without it ([Tracking SDG7 2026](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2026-07/SEforALL%20Tracking%20SDG7%20-%20The%20Energy%20Progress%20Report%202026_Executive%20Version_22Jul2026-2.pdf)).
- **Where.** About 89% of them live in Africa. Africa's access rate rose from 45% to 62% since 2010, yet the number without power barely fell, from 591 to 581 million, because population grew. In sub-Saharan Africa 83% of town dwellers have power, but only 33% of rural people ([Tracking SDG7 2026](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2026-07/SEforALL%20Tracking%20SDG7%20-%20The%20Energy%20Progress%20Report%202026_Executive%20Version_22Jul2026-2.pdf)).
- **Pace.** From 83.5% to 91.9% in 14 years is 0.6 points a year. On current trends about 644 million people will still lack power in 2030. Closing the gap would mean connecting about 177 million people a year, for about USD 30 billion a year ([Tracking SDG7 2026](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2026-07/SEforALL%20Tracking%20SDG7%20-%20The%20Energy%20Progress%20Report%202026_Executive%20Version_22Jul2026-2.pdf)).
- **The rich world** is at 99.97% ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD;HIC/indicator/EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS?format=json&per_page=200&date=1990:2025)).

## The lines on the card

- **83.5%:** the world in 2010.
- **90%:** nine in ten people.
- **99.97%:** the rich world today, in effect everyone.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

The projections we found stop at 2030 ([Tracking SDG7 2026](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2026-07/SEforALL%20Tracking%20SDG7%20-%20The%20Energy%20Progress%20Report%202026_Executive%20Version_22Jul2026-2.pdf)). We found no published odds for 2100, so the curve is judged.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Cheaper solar, batteries and mini-grids, and better planning, could close the gap faster. The people without power live mostly in poor, rural and conflict-hit places, where money, governance and security are the bottleneck.
