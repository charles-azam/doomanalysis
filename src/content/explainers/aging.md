## What it is

The old-age dependency ratio counts people aged 65 and over for every 100 people aged 15 to 64. A ratio of 50 means two working-age people for each older person. It is a head count, not a money count: it says nothing about who actually works, how productive they are, or how healthy older people are. The [World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.DPND.OL) publishes it from UN population estimates.

## Why it matters

Pensions, health care and long-term care are largely paid for by people of working age. When the ratio rises, either taxes go up, benefits go down, people work longer, or output per worker rises. None of this is doom. But a very high ratio shrinks the margin a country has for other shocks, and care for frail older people needs human time that is hard to automate.

## Where it stands

France is at 36.8 in 2025, against 16.0 for the world ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.DPND.OL)). Japan, already at 51.0, shows that a rich country can run at this level without collapse.

The UN's medium projection puts France at 48.5 in 2050 and 56.2 in 2100 ([WPP 2024](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/1_Indicator%20(Standard)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/WPP2024_POP_F07_1_DEPENDENCY_RATIOS_BOTH_SEXES.xlsx)). The UN's [probabilistic projections](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Excel%20Files/2_Indicators%20(Probabilistic)/EXCEL_FILES/2_Population/UN_PPP2024_Output_Annual_OldAgeDepRatio.xlsx) give an 80% range of 42.6 to 78.4 for France in 2100. The world reaches 40.2 (36.8 to 43.7). South Korea goes from 29.3 today to 97.9, and China reaches 98.9. Worldwide, people aged 65 and over should outnumber children under 18 by the late 2070s ([UN Summary of Results](https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Summary-of-Results.pdf)).

## The lines that matter

No agency sets an official danger level, so both lines here are our judgment.

- **50 (two workers per older person).** Budgets come under strain and reforms become unavoidable. From the UN quantiles, France has about a 72% chance of being above 50 in 2100. The world has well under 1%.
- **100 (one worker per older person).** Each working-age person supports one older person, and essentials compete with care for money and staff. France has about a 2% chance of being above 100 in 2100, while South Korea and China are each near 49%.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence cannot change the head count much. Everyone who will be over 65 in 2100 will have been born by 2035, and most are alive today. It could change what the ratio means. If machines do much of the paid work and some care work, the number of workers per older person matters far less. The UN already urges countries to use technology, including automation, to raise productivity at all ages. Medical advances could push the ratio up by extending lives. They could also push the burden down if people stay healthy and working for longer. Superintelligence does not decide who gets the gains. That is still a political choice about taxes and transfers.
