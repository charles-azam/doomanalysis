## Why it matters here

Reactors give steady low-carbon power whatever the weather, filling the hours sun and wind miss. Cost and build time limit them. Fission reaches level 3 only above 30% of the world's electricity, over three times today's share.

## In numbers

- Nuclear made 8.9% of the world's electricity in 2025 ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/countries-and-regions/world/)). The IAEA counts 8.4%, from 413 reactors with 377 GW ([World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaeas-projections-suggest-there-could-be-1000-smrs-by-2060)).
- The seven reactors connected in 2024 took 114 months on average from the start of building, from 61 months in China to 204 for Flamanville 3 in France ([World Nuclear Association](https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/world-nuclear-performance-report/Global-nuclear-industry-performance)).
- Known uranium costing under three times today's price, 5.8 million tonnes, lasts 5,800,000 / 69,000 ≈ 84 years at today's use ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/nuclear-fuel-cycle/uranium-resources/supply-of-uranium)). Fast reactors, which also burn the uranium ordinary reactors leave, get about 60 times more energy from it ([WNA](https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/current-and-future-generation/fast-neutron-reactors)). Russia runs two power-scale ones; India's PFBR and Russia's BREST-300 were still being built in March 2026.

## What each level looks like

- **0 to 10%, level 1.** Our judgment: reactors in a few dozen countries; other sources could supply the same power at a somewhat higher cost.
- **10 to 30%, level 2.** Our judgment: up to three times today's share, steady clean power for hundreds of millions more people.
- **30 to 60%, level 3.** Our judgment: a main source of power, lifting over a tenth of humanity.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

No source forecasts the 2100 share. The IAEA's 2026 projections take capacity from 377 GW to 641–1,045 GW in 2050 and 696–1,284 GW in 2060; small modular reactors, built in factories, make 120 to 284 GW of the 2060 total ([World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaeas-projections-suggest-there-could-be-1000-smrs-by-2060)). The IEA's stated-policies case keeps nuclear [just under 10%](https://www.powermag.com/the-age-of-electricity-and-5-other-forces-reshaping-the-global-energy-outlook/) of a growing total. Jean-Marc Jancovici [judged in 2018](https://jancovici.com/en/energy-transition/renewables/100-renewable-electricity-at-no-extra-cost-a-piece-of-cake/) that nuclear power for all of France's current use would cost about €600 billion over a century, against €4,000 billion for wind with storage.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Suppose superintelligence arrives in the 2030s. It could quickly settle designs, check safety cases and design factory-built modules and fuels. Concrete, licences and skilled crews still set the pace: the reactors finished in 2024 took over nine years on average, and the IEA found that new mines take almost [17 years](https://mining.ca/flippingbooks/mac-report-2023/53/) from discovery to production.
