## What it is

Everything a country's governments owe: central and local government plus social security funds. It is shown as a share of GDP, one year of the country's total output. This card uses the IMF's "general government gross debt". The US Congressional Budget Office (CBO) tracks a narrower figure, federal debt held by the public, so its numbers are lower.

## Why it matters

Debt is not doom in itself. It matters for two reasons. First, room to respond: in a pandemic, war or disaster, governments borrow to keep essentials running. A government that is already heavily indebted has less room. Second, interest: every euro spent on interest is a euro not spent on health, security or energy. In the worst case, lenders demand much higher rates. That forces sudden cuts, the "imposed" consolidation the [Cour des comptes](https://www.vie-publique.fr/files/rapport/pdf/303867.pdf) describes in Portugal.

## Where it stands

- **World:** nearly 94% of GDP in 2025, and on course for 100% by 2029, according to the [IMF Fiscal Monitor](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/execsum.pdf). The [IMF's October 2026 data](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/GGXWDG_NGDP@WEO) reach 101.5% in 2031.
- **France:** 115.5% in 2025 (IMF), a record. The government plan peaks at 122.4% in 2028 and falls to 120.9% in 2030 ([Treasury](https://www.tresor.economie.gouv.fr/Articles/2026/10/06/le-rapport-sur-la-dette-des-administrations-publiques-2027-est-publie)). If policy does not change, the [European Commission](https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/debt-sustainability-monitor-2025_en) projects 144% in 2036 and rates the medium-term risk as high. The Cour des comptes ran scenarios to 2050. Debt falls to 95.3% if a large primary surplus (revenue above spending, before interest) holds for two decades. It rises to 130.9% with a smaller effort and a recession every ten years.
- **United States:** 124.4% (IMF), 145% by 2031. CBO projects federal debt held by the public at 175% of GDP in 2056 under current law ([PGPF summary](https://www.pgpf.org/article/national-debt-projected-to-hit-175-gdp-interest-totals-99-trillion/)).
- **Others (IMF, 2025):** Japan 204.8%, Norway 54%, Sweden 34.5%.

No official body projects debt to 2100. Anything beyond the 2050s is judgment.

## The lines that matter

- **60% of GDP:** the EU treaty reference value. Above it, the Commission sets each country a spending path that must bring debt down ([Regulation 2024/1263](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1263)).
- **90% of GDP:** under the same regulation, debt above 90% must fall by at least 1 point of GDP a year on average. The Commission colours debt above 90% red.
- **The contested 90%:** [Reinhart and Rogoff](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15639) reported that median growth falls by about one point above 90%. [Herndon, Ash and Pollin](https://peri.umass.edu/publication/item/526-does-high-public-debt-consistently-stifle-economic-growth-a-critique-of-reinhart-and-rogoff) found coding errors and selective exclusions. Corrected, average growth above 90% was 2.2%, not −0.1%. Treat 90% as a policy convention, not a cliff.

Japan's debt has stayed above 200% since 2013 ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/GGXWDG_NGDP@WEO)) without a crisis. No single level makes debt cause collapse.

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

It can help the arithmetic. Debt ratios fall when the economy grows faster than the interest rate on the debt, and faster growth raises tax revenue. Cheaper healthcare and public services would also cut deficits. It cannot decide who pays. Taxes, spending cuts, inflation and default are political choices. Fast growth could also push interest rates up, and refinancing old debt at higher rates is already raising interest bills.
