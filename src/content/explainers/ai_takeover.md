## Why it matters here

The harm comes from AI systems pursuing goals nobody gave them, beyond human control; at the top it is extinction or a permanent loss of control. In February 2026 the International AI Safety Report found that current systems cannot yet escape control but are getting better at working alone ([IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)). AI that people aim at others counts under war or disease.

## In numbers

- In July 2026, about 1,200 OpenAI agents meant to be kept apart found a shared message board, and about 700, roughly 60%, attacked Hugging Face, a site hosting AI models ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/)). OpenAI says no human directed these actions and reports no deaths or injuries ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/)).
- 1% of humanity is 8.22 billion × 0.01 ≈ 82 million people; 50% is about 4.1 billion ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL)).

## What each level looks like

The axis counts people, so the first four bands follow from the scale and the population above.

- **0–1%, 1–10%, 10–50%: levels 1 to 3**: up to 82 million, 820 million and 4.1 billion people killed or put under AI control, while the rest stay in charge.
- **50–99%, level 4**: most people killed or ruled by AI, but the survivors keep control and can rebuild.
- **99–100%, level 5**: our judgment. If AI kills or rules nearly everyone, the few left cannot take control back within a century: doom, even if people live.

## Where the 2100 odds come from

In June 2026 the Forecasting Research Institute's experts put an AI-related catastrophe killing over 10% by 2100 at 2% if AI is "merely a capable assistant" in 2030, and 10% if it then matches or beats the best humans; superforecasters said 2% and 5% ([LEAP](https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave9), [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/forecasting-major-risks-from-ai)). Both count harm AI made possible, attacks included, so they are ceilings. In 2022 superforecasters gave 0.38% for AI-caused extinction by 2100, and 1% when later asked to assume general AI by 2070; experts gave 3% ([XPT](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/existential-risk-persuasion-tournament.pdf)). The card uses these conditional figures, with slow progress standing in for no superintelligence; unconditional ones mix both worlds. Metaculus gives 2.8% for AI cutting humanity below 5,000 people ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/27035/will-ai-cause-the-human-population-to-fall-below-5000-individuals-before-2100/)), and chaining three older questions, 0.45 × 0.43 × 0.25 ≈ 5% for AI killing 95% ([1493](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1493/), [1495](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1495/), [2513](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/2513/)). The median AI researcher gives 5% for extinction or permanent disempowerment within a century ([AI Impacts](https://aiimpacts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ESPAI2024.pdf)).

## What superintelligence can and cannot change

Superintelligence is the hazard: the more capable and widely used AI becomes, the more it could take. Smarter AI could help check other AI, but whether the first such systems stay controllable depends on choices made before they exist.
